\begin{letter}{The Editors-in-Chief \\ \emph{Decision Support Systems}}

\opening{Dear Editors,}

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, \emph{``SPADE: spline additive-noise DAG
estimation for interpretable nonlinear causal discovery in managerial decision
support,''} for consideration as a research article in \emph{Decision Support
Systems}.

Managers increasingly need not just accurate forecasts but an account of
\emph{why} an outcome will occur---which drivers to act on, and how. Yet the
methods that dominate practice force a choice between transparent-but-linear
econometrics and accurate-but-opaque deep networks. Our paper contributes a
decision-support artifact that dissolves this trade-off for
moderate-dimensional, non-linear business systems.

We believe the work fits the journal's scope for three reasons:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A genuine methodological advance with a clear decision-support
  purpose.} SPADE couples Kolmogorov--Arnold spline edge functions with
  differentiable causal-structure learning so that a single model yields both a
  forecast and an interpretable, lag-resolved causal graph whose edge functions
  expose the \emph{shape} of each dependency (linear, saturating, threshold).
  \item \textbf{A decisive, rigorously evaluated result.} On non-linear
  instantaneous additive-noise DAGs, SPADE attains AUROC~$\approx$~0.88 and
  substantially outperforms the strongest modern DAG learners (DAGMA-nonlinear,
  GraN-DAG, NOTEARS-MLP). We ground the method in additive-noise identifiability
  theory and evaluate with threshold-free metrics and paired significance tests
  against recent baselines run from their authors' implementations.
  \item \textbf{Transparency and reproducibility.} We report null and negative
  results candidly (a documented macro policy shock is \emph{not} cleanly
  recoverable from observational data), correct and openly retract two
  unreproducible figures from earlier drafts, and release all code and data.
\end{itemize}

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors
approve its submission. We have no conflicts of interest to declare. In
accordance with the journal's double-blind policy, we provide an anonymized
manuscript together with a separate title page containing author details.

We suggest the manuscript may be handled under the areas of business analytics,
machine learning for decision support, and explainable AI. Thank you for your
consideration.
