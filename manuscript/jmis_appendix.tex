\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

We restate and prove the two propositions of Section~\ref{sec:identif}. Throughout,
$P$ denotes the population law of the (weakly stationary) process, and the
one-step reconstruction risk of a predictor $g=(g_1,\dots,g_d)$ is
$R(g)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_P\big[(x_{t,i}-g_i)^2\big]$.

\subsection*{A.1\quad Identifiability of SPADE's target (Proposition~\ref{prop:identif})}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The argument has three steps.

\emph{Step 1 (SPADE's target is a bona fide restricted ANM).} By construction
(Eq.~\ref{eq:predict}), SPADE predicts each target through additive univariate
functions of its candidate parents and nothing else, so any element of its
hypothesis class has the form $x_{t,j}=\sum_{h,i} f^{(h)}_{ij}(x_{t-h,i})+e_{t,j}$
with mutually independent residuals---identical in form to the data-generating
Eq.~\eqref{eq:svar}. Under Assumption~\ref{as:markov} (causal minimality) each
mechanism depends non-trivially on its argument, so the model is a
\emph{restricted} additive-noise model in the sense of
\citet[\S 4]{peters2014causal}.

\emph{Step 2 (lagged and contemporaneous identification).}
For lagged edges, condition on the past: given
$\mathbf{x}_{<t}$, the map $\mathbf{x}_{t-h}\mapsto x_{t,j}$ is a regression whose
non-vanishing partial dependence on coordinate $i$ certifies
$A^{(h)}_{ij}=1$; time ordering fixes the direction, so
$\{A^{(h)}\}_{h\ge 1}$ is identified from the joint law without an acyclicity
requirement. For the contemporaneous block, restrict to the instantaneous SEM
over $\mathbf{x}_t$: by \citet[Thm.~31]{peters2014causal}, a restricted ANM
satisfying causal minimality has its DAG identifiable from the observational
distribution, provided the degenerate configuration of
\citet[Cond.~18]{peters2014causal} does not hold. Assumption~\ref{as:restrict}
excludes exactly that configuration (of which linear--Gaussian is the canonical
instance, cf.\ \citealt{hoyer2008nonlinear}), giving unique identification of
$A^{(0)}$; without it, $A^{(0)}$ is identified only up to Markov equivalence, the
general statement in part~(b).

\emph{Step 3 (the constrained population minimizer recovers $A$).} B-splines of
order $p$ are dense in the H\"older/Sobolev classes as the grid refines, and KAN
regressors built from them attain the minimax-optimal nonparametric rate
\citep{Liu2024,liu2025kanconvergence}; hence for a sufficiently fine grid the
true mechanisms lie in (the closure of) SPADE's class and the population risk
$R$ is minimized at the true regression functions. Because, by Steps~1--2, the
true functions correspond to a \emph{unique} adjacency once the feasible set is
restricted to DAGs on the contemporaneous block ($h(W^{(0)})=0$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:notears}), the constrained minimizer's summary graph equals $A$.
Finite-sample recovery is not claimed; the statement is a well-specified,
large-sample identifiability guarantee, consistent with our empirical finding
that recovery is strongest where the target is genuinely identifiable (non-linear
instantaneous structure) and weaker on near-integrated lagged series.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{A.2\quad Necessity of the information bottleneck (Proposition~\ref{prop:bottleneck})}

\begin{proof}
Let the predictor for target $i$ be $g_i=\psi_i(\mathbf{x}_{-i})$ drawn from a
class $\mathcal{F}$ of functions of all other coordinates
$\mathbf{x}_{-i}$, and suppose $\mathcal{F}$ is rich enough to contain the
conditional mean $m_i(\mathbf{x}_{-i})=\mathbb{E}_P[x_{t,i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{-i}]$.
The per-target risk $\mathbb{E}_P[(x_{t,i}-\psi_i)^2]$ is minimized, over all
measurable $\psi_i$, uniquely (in $L^2(P)$) at $\psi_i=m_i$, and its value depends
only on $\psi_i$. Now suppose an adjacency $\hat A$ is read \emph{post hoc} from
the fitted predictor by any rule that does not itself enter the objective. Since
the objective $R(g)=\sum_i\mathbb{E}_P[(x_{t,i}-\psi_i)^2]$ contains no term in
$\hat A$, its minimizer $g^\star=(m_1,\dots,m_d)$ is unchanged for every choice of
$\hat A$. Consequently all adjacencies are risk-equivalent at the optimum and
$\hat A$ is not identified by risk minimization.

Conversely, impose the bottleneck: require
$g_i=\sum_{j}\mathbf{1}[\hat A_{ji}=1]\,\phi_{ji}(x_j)$, i.e.\ $\hat A$ indexes
\emph{which} additive edge terms are permitted. Then the attainable function set
is $\mathcal{G}(\hat A)=\{\sum_{j\in\pa(i)}\phi_{ji}\}$, and $R$ becomes a genuine
function of $\hat A$ through the constraint $\mathcal{G}(\hat A)$. Under
Assumptions~\ref{as:markov}--\ref{as:restrict} the conditional mean $m_i$ is
additive in the true parents (Eq.~\ref{eq:svar}), so $m_i\in\mathcal{G}(\hat A)$
iff $\hat A$ contains the true parent set of $i$; among such $\hat A$, causal
minimality makes the true (minimal) parent set the unique risk minimizer once a
sparsity preference breaks ties toward fewer edges. Hence the risk-minimizing
structure is unique, which is what the bottleneck buys.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:bottleneck} is not specific to KANs: it explains why
\emph{any} sufficiently expressive shared-backbone predictor fails as a structure
learner, and why component-wise neural-Granger designs
\citep{Tank2021,GCKAN2024} and per-node score-based learners
\citep{zheng2020sparse,lachapelle2020grandag} adopt the same restriction. SPADE's
contribution is to combine this bottleneck with \emph{learnable spline edges},
which is what lets it identify the non-linear mechanisms that fixed-architecture
learners approximate poorly (Section~\ref{sec:instdag}).
\end{remark}

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

All experiments use the released code and fixed seeds. Unless stated otherwise,
SPADE uses $E{=}150$ epochs (400 for the instantaneous-DAG study), learning rate
$5\times10^{-3}$, spline grid $G{=}8$ of cubic ($p{=}3$) B-splines on
$[-3,3]$, group-lasso weight $\lambda_g{=}10^{-2}$ (headline instantaneous-DAG
study: $\lambda_g{=}10^{-2}$, selected on held-out validation seeds
100--119, disjoint from the seeds reported in Table~\ref{tab:instdag} (0--4 at
$d{=}6,10$; 0--2 at $d{=}20$). Mean validation AUROC across
$\lambda_g\in\{0.005,0.008,0.01,0.012,0.015,0.02\}$ was
$0.955/0.969/0.969/0.959/0.948/0.940$; a paired Wilcoxon test of
$\lambda_g=0.01$ against $5\times10^{-3}$ over the same twenty seeds gives
$p=0.39$. The value $0.01$ therefore sits inside a broad plateau of statistically
indistinguishable settings (its peak is at $0.008$), while $0.02$ is clearly
outside it; the sweep script is \texttt{scripts/tune\_instdag\_lambda.py}),
gradient clipping at $5.0$,
and---when contemporaneous edges are modelled---an Augmented-Lagrangian schedule
$(\rho_0,\alpha_0,\beta,K)=(1,0,2,50)$ with $\rho$ capped at $10^{10}$. Series are
$z$-scored per training window (Remark~\ref{rem:varsort}). Baselines use their
authors' reference implementations: DAGMA \citep{Bello2022} via the
\texttt{dagma} package; GraN-DAG \citep{lachapelle2020grandag}, GOLEM
\citep{ng2020golem}, and non-linear NOTEARS \citep{zheng2020sparse} via
\texttt{gcastle}; PCMCI \citep{Runge2019} via \texttt{tigramite}; VAR-LiNGAM
\citep{Hyvarinen2010} via \texttt{lingam}; SCORE \citep{rolland2022score} and
NoGAM \citep{montagna2023nogam} via \texttt{dodiscover} (the py-why project's
\texttt{dodiscover.toporder} module, which the NoGAM authors' own repository
identifies as its reference release; not distributed on PyPI under that name,
so installed from a source clone of the project rather than \texttt{pip
install dodiscover}). Any baseline that failed to run in our
environment within the compute budget is reported as such rather than omitted.
NoGAM's kernel-ridge cross-validated pruning step did not complete within
budget for the full five-seed protocol at $d{=}20$; we report it there over a
single seed, flagged in Table~\ref{tab:instdag}, rather than omit the width or
extrapolate.

Three further settings were fixed by held-out procedures that never touched
the seeds used for a reported number: the forecasting epoch count (300, chosen on
an inner train/validation split of the training fold;
\texttt{scripts/tune\_forecast\_epochs.py}), the finite-and-bounded screen with
deterministic reseeding for the non-linear lagged generator
(Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}; scoped to
\texttt{scripts/honest\_causal\_benchmark.py}), and the width-dependent
$\lambda_g{=}0.002$ at $d{=}50$ (validation seeds 142--144;
\texttt{scripts/tune\_d50\_lambda.py}; documented in \texttt{fit\_cdkan}'s
docstring). The bike-sharing study (Section~\ref{sec:bike}) used no tuning at
all: its protocol was fixed and committed before any method was run on that data
(\texttt{scripts/realworld\_bike\_sharing.py}).

\section{Technical supplement to Section~\ref{sec:method}}
\label{app:techsupp}

This appendix collects technical material moved out of the main text to keep
Section~\ref{sec:method} readable; nothing here changes the method.

\subsection*{C.1\quad Acyclicity as a smooth constraint}

\begin{theorem}[Acyclicity, \citealt{Zheng2018}]\label{thm:notears}
For a weighted adjacency $W\in\Real^{d\times d}$ with non-negative entries, let
$h(W)=\operatorname{tr}\!\big(e^{\,W\circ W}\big)-d$, where $\circ$ is the
Hadamard product and $e^{(\cdot)}$ the matrix exponential. Then $h(W)\ge 0$, and
$h(W)=0$ if and only if the graph induced by the non-zeros of $W$ is a directed
acyclic graph. Moreover $\nabla h(W)=\big(e^{W\circ W}\big)^{\!\top}\!\circ 2W$.
\end{theorem}
An equivalent log-determinant characterization,
$h^{s}_{\mathrm{ldet}}(W)=-\log\det(sI-W\circ W)+d\log s$, has better-conditioned
gradients \citep{Bello2022}; either may be used, and our implementation defaults to
Theorem~\ref{thm:notears}. We apply $h(\cdot)$ \emph{only} to the same-period block
$W_0$. Lagged blocks are left unconstrained because temporal precedence already
forbids cycles across time; constraining them would incorrectly penalize
legitimate feedback such as $x_{t-1}\!\to\! y_t$ and $y_{t-1}\!\to\! x_t$. This
block treatment mirrors DYNOTEARS \citep{Pamfil2020} and resolves the limitation of
a single flat $d\times d$ constraint.

\subsection*{C.2\quad The instantaneous variant (self-masked SEM)}

When same-period effects are modelled, SPADE realizes a non-linear structural
equation model: it predicts $x_{t,i}$ from its \emph{same-period} parents
$x_{t,j\neq i}$ (self-loops masked) in addition to its lags, with $W_0$ the
same-period edge-importance matrix scored as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:importance}. The ALM
drives $h(W_0)\!\to\!0$, so the instantaneous graph is a DAG and the SEM is
identifiable. This is a non-linear, KAN-based analogue of DYNOTEARS; we validate it
on data with a known instantaneous DAG in Section~\ref{sec:contemp}. The purely
instantaneous specialization---the model behind our headline result---is the
cleanest instance of the identifiability theory of Section~\ref{sec:identif}: each
variable is predicted from its same-period parents only, self-loops are masked, and
the constraint of Theorem~\ref{thm:notears} is dualized on the edge-norm matrix $W$.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:instant} gives the full procedure; it is the estimator whose
population minimizer Proposition~\ref{prop:identif} shows to be consistent.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{SPADE-Instantaneous: self-masked nonlinear SEM with acyclicity (ALM)}
\label{alg:instant}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} standardized data $X\in\Real^{N\times d}$; grid $G$; group
weight $\lambda_g$; learning rate $\eta$; epochs $E$; ALM schedule
$(\rho{=}1,\alpha{=}0,\beta{=}2,K{=}50)$, tolerance $\tau{=}10^{-8}$
\STATE Initialize spline coefficients $C\in\Real^{d\times d\times(G+p)}$, bias $b$; fix self-mask $M_{ii}{=}0,\,M_{ij}{=}1$
\FOR{epoch $=1$ to $E$}
    \STATE $\hat{x}_{\cdot i} \leftarrow b_i + \sum_{j\neq i}\phi_{ij}(x_{\cdot j})$ \quad(self-masked SEM forecast, Eq.~\ref{eq:predict})
    \STATE $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}} \leftarrow \tfrac1{Nd}\lVert \hat X - X\rVert_F^2$
    \STATE $W_{ij} \leftarrow \lVert (C\odot M)_{ij,:}\rVert_2$ \quad(edge-norm matrix)
    \STATE $h \leftarrow \operatorname{tr}(e^{W\circ W})-d$ \hfill(Theorem~\ref{thm:notears})
    \STATE $\mathcal{L} \leftarrow \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}} + \lambda_g\!\sum_{ij}W_{ij} + \alpha\,h + \tfrac{\rho}{2}h^2$
    \STATE Adam step on $\mathcal{L}$ with $\eta$; gradient-clip at $5.0$
    \IF{epoch $\bmod\ K = 0$ \textbf{and} $h>\tau$}
        \STATE $\alpha \leftarrow \alpha + \rho\,h$;\quad $\rho \leftarrow \min(\beta\rho,\,10^{10})$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE $S_{ij} \leftarrow \mathrm{std}_t\big[(C\odot M)_{ij}\!\cdot\! B(x_{t,j})\big]$ \quad(edge scores, $[\text{effect},\text{cause}]$)
\STATE \textbf{Return} $S$, spline edge functions $\{\phi_{ij}\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection*{C.3\quad On the choice of the sparsity penalty}

We deliberately avoid an $\ell_1$ penalty on stochastic gating logits, which recent
work shows can bias continuous DAG learning toward many small-magnitude edges and
which we found empirically unstable under temperature annealing
\citep{ng2020golem,Bello2022}. The group-lasso acts at the granularity of whole
edges, and our threshold-free evaluation (AUROC/AUPRC, Section~\ref{sec:synthetic})
reports ranking quality independently of any single sparsity level. An
$\ell_0$-style gate is a natural extension.


\section{Corrections made during development}
\label{app:corrections}

For transparency we list every correction to the benchmarks or reported numbers
that was made while preparing this manuscript, with the cause and the effect on
the headline results. None was made to favour SPADE: several of them lowered its
reported performance, and each correction was accompanied by a runnable script.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Group-lasso weight on the instantaneous-DAG benchmark.} The
initial default $\lambda_g{=}0.02$ was over-regularized (AUROC $\approx0.88$). A
retuned value, $5\times10^{-3}$, appeared to give AUROC $\approx0.97$ and
a clean win over SCORE and NoGAM at every width, but the tuning script had swept
the same seeds that were used to report results, so that figure was inflated by
selection on the evaluation data. The script now uses disjoint validation seeds;
re-sweeping selects $\lambda_g{=}0.01$ and gives the results reported in
Section~\ref{sec:instdag} (AUROC $0.947/0.926/0.915$, with NoGAM ahead at
$d{=}10$). The sweep was then extended from five to twenty validation seeds
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
\item \textbf{Forecasting epochs and normalization.} SPADE's forecasting head
was first trained for 30 epochs while the baselines used 80, and the baselines
had no normalization. After adding RevIN to every baseline, SPADE trailed three
of four. An inner-validation sweep (training fold only) selected 300 epochs,
reducing MSE from 0.048 to 0.029 at the cost of training time (36\,s to 357\,s),
so SPADE is now the slowest forecaster in Table~\ref{tab:forecast}.
\item \textbf{Test-leaking normalization.} An early forecasting result (MSE
$\approx0.0008$) came from computing normalization statistics on the full series.
All reported results use train-only statistics (Table~\ref{tab:leak}). An
earlier lagged-benchmark F1 ($0.897$) could not be reproduced and was withdrawn.
\item \textbf{Non-linear lagged generator.} Some $(d,\text{seed})$ draws at
$d\in\{10,20,50\}$ produced non-finite or extreme-scale data, which silently
removed classical baselines and left neural methods at initialization. A
finite-and-bounded screen with deterministic reseeding raised SPADE's
non-linear lagged AUROC from $0.76$ to $0.93$ (and affected every method).
\item \textbf{Width-dependence of $\lambda_g$ at $d{=}50$.} With $\lambda_g{=}0.01$
the linear $d{=}50$ AUROC was $0.829$; a validation sweep selected $0.002$, giving
$1.000$.
\item \textbf{Timing.} Wall-clock values in an early raw result file were
inflated by CPU contention (138--172\,s); they were re-measured on an otherwise
idle machine (SPADE 2.1/1.9/4.9\,s at $d{=}6/10/20$), and the speed-up over NoGAM
was restated accordingly ($275$--$930\times$). A runtime-table row that mixed two
training protocols ($d{=}15$) was corrected.
\item \textbf{Presentation.} The architecture figure, a table caption that
contradicted its Wilcoxon $p$-values, a claim that group-lasso leaves ranking
quality unchanged (AUROC $0.902$ with versus $0.984$ without), and the statement
that the number of seeds was uniform across widths were corrected; the
returns-based re-analysis (Section~\ref{sec:returns}) was added after concerns
about non-stationarity.
\end{enumerate}
