
\section{The SPADE Artifact}
\label{sec:method}

SPADE (SPline Additive-noise DAG Estimation) is a design-science artifact, built
on Kolmogorov--Arnold networks, that does two things at once: it forecasts a set
of related business time series, and it shows \emph{which series drive which, and
how}. This section is organized so that readers can stop early. Section~\ref{sec:plain}
explains the idea and what the artifact outputs in plain language, with no
mathematics, and is sufficient to follow the results and the managerial
discussion. Sections~\ref{sec:formulation}--\ref{sec:complexity} give the formal
specification needed for reproduction (problem and assumptions, why structure is
recoverable, architecture, training, cost). Proofs, the acyclicity theorem, and
the instantaneous-variant pseudocode are in Appendices~\ref{app:proofs}
and~\ref{app:techsupp}. A reference implementation is in the accompanying
repository.

\subsection{SPADE in plain language}
\label{sec:plain}

\paragraph{The problem.} A manager watches several linked series---say daily
bike rentals, temperature, humidity, and whether the day is a working day---and
wants more than a forecast: which of these actually \emph{drive} rentals, through
what kind of relationship (steady, saturating, a threshold?), and with what delay?
Standard econometric models (VAR) give transparent but straight-line answers;
deep networks capture curves but hide them inside a black box.

\paragraph{Three design ideas.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \emph{One learnable curve per driver.} Each series is predicted only as a
    \emph{sum of curves}, one for each candidate driver (and lag). No hidden layer
    mixes drivers together afterwards. Because the curve from driver $j$ is the only
    path from $j$ to the forecast of $i$, the size of that curve is an honest
    measure of how much $j$ matters to $i$, and the curve itself can be plotted and
    read by a person (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}).
    \item \emph{Prune what is not needed.} A sparsity penalty pushes the curves of
    unhelpful drivers to exactly zero, so the surviving graph is small enough to
    inspect.
    \item \emph{No loops at the same instant.} If two series are allowed to affect
    each other \emph{within} the same period, a smooth ``no-cycles'' condition keeps
    that same-period graph free of circular explanations. Effects across time need
    no such condition, since the past cannot be caused by the future.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What the artifact outputs.} (i) a directed graph of candidate drivers;
(ii) for every retained edge, the learned curve, so one can see whether an
influence is linear, saturating, or threshold-like; (iii) the typical delay with
which each driver acts; and (iv) a one-step-ahead forecast of every series, from
the same fitted model.

\paragraph{What it does not do.} SPADE's edges are \emph{candidate} causal
mechanisms for expert review, not proven causes. They are meaningful only if the
usual conditions hold---no important unmeasured common cause, relationships that
are not mere straight lines with Gaussian noise, and structure that is stable
over the fitting window (Assumptions~\ref{as:suff}--\ref{as:stat} below). We
therefore pair the method with explicit checks against domain knowledge on real
data (Sections~\ref{sec:real} and~\ref{sec:bike}) rather than asking the reader to
trust the graph.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

Let $\mathbf{X} = \{\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_T\}$ be a multivariate time
series with $\mathbf{x}_t \in \Real^d$. We posit a non-linear Structural
Vector Autoregressive (SVAR) data-generating process with maximum lag $L$: for
each target $j\in\{1,\dots,d\}$,
\begin{equation}
    x_{t,j} \;=\; \underbrace{\sum_{i\,\in\,\pa^{(0)}(j)} f^{(0)}_{ij}\!\big(x_{t,i}\big)}_{\text{same period}}
    \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_{h=1}^{L}\ \sum_{i\,\in\,\pa^{(h)}(j)} f^{(h)}_{ij}\!\big(x_{t-h,i}\big)}_{\text{earlier periods}}
    \;+\; \epsilon_{t,j}.
    \label{eq:svar}
\end{equation}
In words: each series equals a sum of (possibly curved) effects of its parents,
some measured in the same period and some in earlier periods, plus unexplained
noise. Formally, $\pa^{(h)}(j)=\{i: A^{(h)}_{ij}=1\}$ are the lag-$h$ parents of
$j$, encoded by adjacency $A^{(h)}\in\{0,1\}^{d\times d}$; the $f^{(h)}_{ij}$ are
univariate causal mechanisms; and the noises $\{\epsilon_{t,j}\}$ are jointly
independent across $j$ and i.i.d.\ across $t$. Equation~\eqref{eq:svar} is an
\emph{additive-noise model} (ANM). We separate the lagged adjacencies
$A^{(1)},\dots,A^{(L)}$ from the same-period adjacency $A^{(0)}$ because only the
latter needs an acyclicity constraint (Section~\ref{sec:identif}). The summary
graph reported throughout is the union $A_{ij}=1-\prod_{h=0}^{L}(1-A^{(h)}_{ij})$.

Our goal is twofold: (i) \textbf{forecasting}---predict $\mathbf{x}_{t+1}$ from
history $\mathbf{x}_{t-L:t}$; and (ii) \textbf{causal discovery}---recover the
adjacencies $\{A^{(h)}\}_{h=0}^{L}$ \emph{and} the mechanisms $f^{(h)}_{ij}$
that give the graph its managerial meaning.

We work under four standard assumptions, made explicit because directed edges
from observational data are causal only under stated conditions
\citep{Pearl2009,peters2014causal}.
\begin{assumption}[Causal sufficiency]\label{as:suff}
There are no unobserved common causes of the measured variables. \emph{In plain
words:} nothing important that moves several series at once is missing from the
data. In open economic systems this is strong; we therefore treat real-data edges
as \emph{candidate} mechanisms for expert review, not verified causes
(Section~\ref{sec:real}).
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Markov and causal minimality]\label{as:markov}
The joint law is Markov to the SVAR graph, and no proper sub-graph also renders
it Markov (equivalently, every declared mechanism $f^{(h)}_{ij}$ depends
non-trivially on its argument). \emph{In plain words:} the graph explains the
data, and every drawn edge really matters. This is the mild condition under which
ANMs are identifiable \citep{peters2014causal}.
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Restricted, non-linear mechanisms]\label{as:restrict}
The same-period mechanisms are \emph{non-linear} and $(f^{(0)}_{ij},$ noise,
cause$)$ triples do not solve the differential equation of
\citet[Cond.~18]{peters2014causal} (the degeneracy that would admit a reverse
model). \emph{In plain words:} the relationships are genuinely curved, not
straight lines with Gaussian noise---the one textbook case in which cause and
effect are provably indistinguishable.
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Within-window stationarity]\label{as:stat}
$\{A^{(h)},f^{(h)}\}$ are constant within each estimation window. \emph{In plain
words:} the system does not change its rules while we are fitting it. We probe
violations directly via rolling-window stability (Section~\ref{sec:stability})
and a structural-change experiment (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).
\end{assumption}
These delimit the artifact's boundary conditions and are revisited in the
Discussion.

\subsection{Identifiability: what makes structure recoverable}
\label{sec:identif}

Before specifying the estimator we state \emph{why} its target is well posed.
Two facts drive the design: (i) a non-linear ANM is identifiable up to its
graph, whereas a linear--Gaussian one is not; and (ii) identifiability is a
property of the \emph{hypothesis class}, not merely of the data---a
sufficiently flexible predictor destroys it. These motivate, respectively,
SPADE's spline edge functions and its information-bottleneck architecture. Same-period
acyclicity is enforced through the smooth criterion
$h(W)=\operatorname{tr}(e^{W\circ W})-d$ of \citet{Zheng2018}, which is zero exactly
when the graph has no directed cycle (Theorem~\ref{thm:notears},
Appendix~\ref{app:techsupp}); we apply it \emph{only} to the same-period block,
because lagged edges are ordered by time's arrow.

\paragraph{The estimation target is identifiable.} SPADE's forecast of each
target flows only through additive univariate functions of its candidate parents
(Section~\ref{sec:arch}); it is therefore an ANM of the same form as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:svar}, and inherits the identifiability theory of restricted
additive-noise models.
\begin{proposition}[Identifiability of SPADE's target]\label{prop:identif}
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:suff}--\ref{as:stat}, suppose the data follow
Eq.~\eqref{eq:svar} with mechanisms in a class approximable by order-$p$
B-splines. Then (a) the lagged adjacencies $\{A^{(h)}\}_{h\ge 1}$ are identified
by the joint law of $(\mathbf{x}_t,\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\dots)$; and (b) the
same-period DAG $A^{(0)}$ is identified up to Markov equivalence in general,
and \emph{uniquely} under Assumption~\ref{as:restrict}. Consequently the
population minimizer of the one-step reconstruction risk over the component-wise
B-spline class, subject to $h(W^{(0)})=0$, recovers the summary graph $A$ as the
spline grid refines.
\end{proposition}
Proposition~\ref{prop:identif} applies the restricted-ANM identifiability theorem of
\citet[Thm.~31]{peters2014causal} (built on \citealt{hoyer2008nonlinear}) to our
function class, using the spline approximation guarantee for KAN regressors
\citep{Liu2024,liu2025kanconvergence}; the argument is in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. \emph{Linear} mechanisms with Gaussian noise are the
non-identifiable case, which is why a method built for the \emph{non-linear}
regime can succeed where its target is identifiable, while purely linear learners
cannot orient such edges.

\begin{remark}[What the proposition does, and does not, claim]\label{rem:whatprop1claims}
Proposition~\ref{prop:identif} is a statement about an idealized population
estimator. Three gaps separate it from the estimator we actually run.
(i)~\emph{The assumptions are posited, not verified.} The optimizer cannot
establish that a real process satisfies Assumptions~\ref{as:suff}--\ref{as:stat};
that is a modeling judgment, which is why real-data edges are reported as
candidates (Section~\ref{sec:real}).
(ii)~\emph{Sparsity shrinks.} The proposition concerns the risk minimizer under
the \emph{hard} constraint $h(W^{(0)})=0$; the fitted estimator
(Eq.~\ref{eq:constrained}) adds a group-lasso penalty $\lambda_g\Omega_{\mathrm{group}}$
that carries the usual shrinkage bias at finite $\lambda_g$. Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}
shows empirically that recovery is stable across a range of $\lambda_g$;
finite-sample consistency as $N\to\infty,\lambda_g\to 0$ is left open.
(iii)~\emph{Acyclicity holds only approximately.} The Augmented Lagrangian drives
$h(W^{(0)})$ to a small residual (e.g.\ $\approx 2\times10^{-3}$ in
Section~\ref{sec:contemp}), not exactly zero. Neither (ii) nor (iii) is claimed to
vanish without further analysis.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Why the bottleneck is necessary.} Identifiability can be forfeited by
the estimator even when the population target is well posed. This makes precise
the empirical collapse documented in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}.
\begin{proposition}[Necessity of the information bottleneck]\label{prop:bottleneck}
Let a predictor model each $x_{t,i}$ through a shared function class
$\mathcal{F}$ of \emph{all} other coordinates that is rich enough to contain the
true conditional mean, and let an adjacency $\hat A$ be read post hoc from the
fitted predictor. Then the reconstruction risk depends on the fitted function
alone and not on $\hat A$; hence every adjacency consistent with the unconstrained
optimum attains the minimum risk, and $\hat A$ is \emph{not} identified by risk
minimization. Restricting the hypothesis class so that the adjacency indexes the
function---predicting each target \emph{only} through per-parent edges---together
with a sparsity preference that breaks ties toward the \emph{minimal} such
adjacency, is sufficient to make the risk-minimizing structure unique.
\end{proposition}
\emph{In plain words:} if a flexible network is free to mix all variables, many
different graphs fit the data equally well, so the fit alone cannot tell them
apart; forcing the prediction to pass through one curve per driver removes that
freedom. The restriction is necessary but not by itself sufficient: the sparsity
penalty (the edge-level group-lasso of Eq.~\eqref{eq:constrained}) supplies the
tie-breaker (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). This is the formal reason a
``dense-backbone'' KAN forecaster recovers structure only at chance
(AUROC~$\approx 0.5$; Section~\ref{sec:ablation}), whereas component-wise
SPADE does not.

\begin{remark}[Guarding against benchmark artifacts]\label{rem:varsort}
Continuous DAG learners can exploit \emph{varsortability}---an accidental
alignment between marginal variance and causal order in simulated
data---inflating apparent accuracy \citep{reisach2021beware,reisach2023scale}. We
neutralize this by standardizing every series to unit variance before fitting (so
marginal scale carries no order information) and by reporting threshold-free
AUROC/AUPRC rather than tuned-threshold scores. Our advantage therefore cannot be
attributed to a variance-ordering shortcut.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The SPADE Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

The central design principle is an \emph{information bottleneck}: the forecast of
each target must flow \emph{only} through per-edge functions of its candidate
causes, with no shared network that re-mixes variables afterwards. The edge
functions are then the sole path from a cause to an effect, so their magnitude is
a faithful structural signal (as in neural-Granger models,
\citealt{Tank2021,Shojaie2022}). A variant that instead feeds the causal layer
into a dense residual backbone recovers structure only at chance
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/cdkan_architecture.png}
    \caption{The SPADE pipeline. RevIN removes per-window level/scale shifts;
    the component-wise causal layer predicts each target $i$ as a sum of
    B-spline edge functions of its lagged causes---one spline per (effect, cause,
    lag)---and nothing else. Structure is read from each edge's contribution to
    the forecast; a group-lasso drives whole edges to zero; and (when
    contemporaneous edges are modelled) acyclicity is enforced on the lag-0 block
    via the Augmented Lagrangian Method.}
    \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{(1) Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN).}
Many business series are non-stationary. We apply RevIN \citep{Kim2022}: for each
input window we subtract the window mean and divide by the window standard
deviation (computed on the input window only and detached from the computation
graph, so no future information leaks), apply a learnable affine transform, and
invert it on the output. The causal layer therefore operates on level- and
scale-removed signals.

\paragraph{(2) Component-wise causal layer with per-lag B-spline edges.}
For each ordered pair $(i,j)$ and lag $h$ we place a learnable B-spline edge
function, in the spirit of Kolmogorov--Arnold networks \citep{Liu2024},
\begin{equation}
    \phi^{(h)}_{ij}(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{G+p} c^{(ijh)}_k B_k(x),
    \label{eq:spline}
\end{equation}
where $\{B_k\}$ are order-$p$ B-spline bases on a grid of size $G$---a flexible
smooth curve whose shape is set by the coefficients $c_k$. The forecast of target
$i$ is the \emph{additive} aggregation of its incoming edges only,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{x}_{t,i} \;=\; b_i \;+\; \sum_{j=1}^{d}\ \sum_{h}\ \phi^{(h)}_{ij}\!\big(x_{t-h,\,j}\big),
    \label{eq:predict}
\end{equation}
with lags $h\in\{1,\dots,L\}$ (and $h=0$ added only when same-period effects are
modelled). There is no downstream layer mixing the $\hat{x}_{t,i}$ across $i$.
Because the nonlinearity sits \emph{on the edge}, each mechanism is inspectable:
plotting $\phi^{(h)}_{ij}$ shows whether an influence is linear, saturating, or
threshold-like (Section~\ref{sec:splines}).

\paragraph{(3) Structure and lag read-outs.}
Rather than reading structure from stochastic gating logits (which we found
unstable), we read it from the \emph{contribution} each edge makes to the
forecast. Let $c_{ij}(t)=\sum_h \phi^{(h)}_{ij}(x_{t-h,j})$ be the total signal
edge $(i,j)$ injects into target $i$. The edge score is its dispersion over the
data,
\begin{equation}
    S_{ij} \;=\; \mathrm{std}_t\big[c_{ij}(t)\big],
    \label{eq:importance}
\end{equation}
a deterministic measure of how much cause $j$ moves effect $i$. The expected
delay is the contribution-weighted mean lag,
$\mathbb{E}[h]_{ij}=\sum_h h\,m^{(h)}_{ij}/\sum_h m^{(h)}_{ij}$ with
$m^{(h)}_{ij}=\mathrm{std}_t[\phi^{(h)}_{ij}(x_{t-h,j})]$.

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:opt}

\paragraph{Objective.}
We fit the edge coefficients $\Phi=\{c^{(ijh)}\}$ and biases $b$ by minimizing
one-step forecasting error plus a \emph{group-lasso} that treats each
cause$\to$effect edge as a group,
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\Phi,b}\;\; \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}
    \;+\; \lambda_{g}\sum_{i,j}\Big\lVert\, \mathrm{vec}\big(\{c^{(ijh)}\}_{h}\big)\,\Big\rVert_2
    \quad \text{s.t.}\quad h(W^{(0)}) = 0 .
    \label{eq:constrained}
\end{equation}
The group norm couples all lags of edge $(i,j)$ so that an entire edge either
survives or vanishes, yielding sparse structure. The constraint
$h(W^{(0)})=0$, with $h(W)=\operatorname{tr}(e^{W\circ W})-d$ applied to the
same-period importance block $W^{(0)}_{ij}=\mathrm{std}_t[\phi^{(0)}_{ij}]$,
is enforced only when same-period edges are modelled and is dualized with the
Augmented Lagrangian Method:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\Phi,b;\alpha,\rho) = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}
    + \lambda_{g}\,\Omega_{\mathrm{group}}
    + \alpha\, h(W^{(0)}) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\, h(W^{(0)})^2 .
    \label{eq:alm}
\end{equation}
For purely lagged benchmarks (all edges have $h\!\ge\!1$) the summary graph may
contain cycles across time, so the acyclicity term is inactive. When same-period
effects are modelled, SPADE realizes a non-linear structural equation model in
which each series is predicted from the other same-period series (self-loops
masked); this \emph{instantaneous variant}, behind our headline result, is
specified in Appendix~\ref{app:techsupp} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:instant}).

\paragraph{Procedure.} Algorithm~\ref{alg:cdkan} lists the procedure: a single Adam
loop on Eq.~\eqref{eq:constrained} with gradient clipping and, when same-period
edges are modelled, an ALM step every $K$ epochs that tightens the acyclicity
penalty. Unless stated otherwise we use $E{=}150$ epochs, learning rate
$5\!\times\!10^{-3}$, grid size $G{=}8$, and $\lambda_g{=}10^{-2}$; a sensitivity
analysis over $\lambda_g$ and $G$ is in Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}, and the
full settings, including how $\lambda_g$ was chosen on held-out data, are in
Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{SPADE training}
\label{alg:cdkan}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} windows $\{(\mathbf{x}_{t-L:t},\mathbf{x}_t)\}$; group weight $\lambda_g$; (optional) $\rho_0{=}1,\alpha_0{=}0,\beta{=}2,K{=}10$
\STATE Initialize edge coefficients $\Phi$, biases $b$
\FOR{epoch $=1$ to $E$}
    \STATE $\hat{\mathbf{x}} \leftarrow$ component-wise forecast (Eq.~\ref{eq:predict})
    \STATE Adam step on $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}+\lambda_g\,\Omega_{\mathrm{group}}$ (+ ALM terms if same-period); gradient-clip at $5.0$
    \IF{same-period \textbf{and} epoch $\bmod\ K = 0$ \textbf{and} $h(W^{(0)})>10^{-8}$}
        \STATE $\rho \leftarrow \min(\beta\rho,10^{16})$;\quad $\alpha \leftarrow \alpha + \rho\,h(W^{(0)})$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Compute edge scores $S_{ij}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:importance}) and lags $\mathbb{E}[h]_{ij}$
\STATE \textbf{Return} scores $S$, expected lags, spline edge functions, forecaster
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}
Per epoch, SPADE evaluates $O(d^2 L)$ edge splines, each $O(G)$, over $N$ windows,
giving $O(N d^2 L G)$ cost that is \emph{linear in the number of samples} $N$. The
optional acyclicity term adds an $O(d^3)$ matrix exponential of the same-period
block. Because the model is a single component-wise layer with no deep backbone,
it is fast in practice (seconds at $d{=}5$; Section~\ref{sec:scalability}) and
suited to systems of moderate width; high-$d$ extensions are discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
