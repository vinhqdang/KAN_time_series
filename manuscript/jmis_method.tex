
\section{The SPADE Artifact}
\label{sec:method}

We present SPADE (SPline Additive-noise DAG Estimation), built on
Kolmogorov--Arnold networks, a design-science
artifact that jointly performs multivariate time-series forecasting and causal
structure learning. This section gives the complete specification needed for
reproduction: the problem formulation and its identifiability assumptions
(Section~\ref{sec:formulation}), the lag-aware architecture
(Section~\ref{sec:arch}), the constrained-optimization procedure with its full
hyperparameter schedule (Section~\ref{sec:opt}), and a complexity analysis
(Section~\ref{sec:complexity}). A reference implementation is available in the
accompanying repository.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

Let $\mathbf{X} = \{\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_T\}$ be a multivariate time
series with $\mathbf{x}_t \in \Real^d$. We posit a non-linear Structural
Vector Autoregressive (SVAR) data-generating process with maximum lag $L$: for
each target $j\in\{1,\dots,d\}$,
\begin{equation}
    x_{t,j} \;=\; \underbrace{\sum_{i\,\in\,\pa^{(0)}(j)} f^{(0)}_{ij}\!\big(x_{t,i}\big)}_{\text{contemporaneous}}
    \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_{h=1}^{L}\ \sum_{i\,\in\,\pa^{(h)}(j)} f^{(h)}_{ij}\!\big(x_{t-h,i}\big)}_{\text{lagged}}
    \;+\; \epsilon_{t,j},
    \label{eq:svar}
\end{equation}
where $\pa^{(h)}(j)=\{i: A^{(h)}_{ij}=1\}$ are the lag-$h$ parents of $j$,
encoded by adjacency $A^{(h)}\in\{0,1\}^{d\times d}$; the $f^{(h)}_{ij}$ are
(possibly non-linear) univariate causal mechanisms; and the noises
$\{\epsilon_{t,j}\}$ are jointly independent across $j$ and i.i.d.\ across $t$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:svar} is an \emph{additive-noise model} (ANM): each target is
an additive function of its parents plus independent noise. We separate the
\emph{lagged} adjacencies $A^{(1)},\dots,A^{(L)}$ from the \emph{contemporaneous}
adjacency $A^{(0)}$ because, as Section~\ref{sec:identif} shows, only the latter
needs an acyclicity constraint. The summary graph reported throughout is the
union $A_{ij}=1-\prod_{h=0}^{L}(1-A^{(h)}_{ij})$.

Our goal is twofold: (i) \textbf{forecasting}---predict $\mathbf{x}_{t+1}$ from
history $\mathbf{x}_{t-L:t}$; and (ii) \textbf{causal discovery}---recover the
adjacencies $\{A^{(h)}\}_{h=0}^{L}$ \emph{and} the mechanisms $f^{(h)}_{ij}$
that give the graph its managerial meaning.

We work under four standard assumptions, made explicit because directed edges
from observational data are causal only under stated conditions
\citep{Pearl2009,peters2014causal}.
\begin{assumption}[Causal sufficiency]\label{as:suff}
There are no unobserved common causes of the measured variables. In open economic
systems this is strong; we therefore treat real-data edges as \emph{candidate}
mechanisms for expert review, not verified causes (Section~\ref{sec:real}).
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Markov and causal minimality]\label{as:markov}
The joint law is Markov to the SVAR graph, and no proper sub-graph also renders
it Markov (equivalently, every declared mechanism $f^{(h)}_{ij}$ depends
non-trivially on its argument). Causal minimality is the mild condition under
which ANMs are identifiable \citep{peters2014causal}.
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Restricted, non-linear mechanisms]\label{as:restrict}
The contemporaneous mechanisms are \emph{non-linear} and $(f^{(0)}_{ij},$ noise,
cause$)$ triples do not solve the differential equation of
\citet[Cond.~18]{peters2014causal} (the degeneracy that would admit a reverse
model). This rules out the linear--Gaussian case, in which cause and effect are
provably indistinguishable.
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Within-window stationarity]\label{as:stat}
$\{A^{(h)},f^{(h)}\}$ are constant within each estimation window. We probe
violations directly via rolling-window stability (Section~\ref{sec:stability})
and a structural-change experiment (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).
\end{assumption}
These delimit the artifact's boundary conditions and are revisited in the
Discussion.

\subsection{Identifiability: what makes structure recoverable}
\label{sec:identif}

Before specifying the estimator we state \emph{why} its target is well posed.
Two facts drive the entire design: (i) a non-linear ANM is identifiable up to its
graph, whereas a linear--Gaussian one is not; and (ii) identifiability is a
property of the \emph{hypothesis class}, not merely of the data---a
sufficiently flexible predictor destroys it. These motivate, respectively,
SPADE's spline edge functions and its information-bottleneck architecture.

\paragraph{Acyclicity as a smooth constraint.} We first recall the
characterization that turns discrete DAG search into continuous optimization.
\begin{theorem}[Acyclicity, \citealt{Zheng2018}]\label{thm:notears}
For a weighted adjacency $W\in\Real^{d\times d}$ with non-negative entries, let
$h(W)=\operatorname{tr}\!\big(e^{\,W\circ W}\big)-d$, where $\circ$ is the
Hadamard product and $e^{(\cdot)}$ the matrix exponential. Then $h(W)\ge 0$, and
$h(W)=0$ if and only if the graph induced by the non-zeros of $W$ is a directed
acyclic graph. Moreover $\nabla h(W)=\big(e^{W\circ W}\big)^{\!\top}\!\circ 2W$.
\end{theorem}
An equivalent log-determinant characterization,
$h^{s}_{\mathrm{ldet}}(W)=-\log\det(sI-W\circ W)+d\log s$, was later shown to have
better-conditioned gradients \citep{Bello2022}; either may be used, and our
implementation defaults to Theorem~\ref{thm:notears}. Crucially, we will apply
$h(\cdot)$ \emph{only} to the contemporaneous block: lagged edges are ordered by
time's arrow and need not be acyclic.

\paragraph{The estimation target is identifiable.} SPADE's forecast of each
target flows only through additive univariate functions of its candidate parents
(Section~\ref{sec:arch}); it is therefore, by construction, an ANM of the same
form as Eq.~\eqref{eq:svar}. This lets us inherit the identifiability theory of
restricted additive-noise models.
\begin{proposition}[Identifiability of SPADE's target]\label{prop:identif}
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:suff}--\ref{as:stat}, suppose the data follow
Eq.~\eqref{eq:svar} with mechanisms in a class approximable by order-$p$
B-splines. Then (a) the lagged adjacencies $\{A^{(h)}\}_{h\ge 1}$ are identified
by the joint law of $(\mathbf{x}_t,\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\dots)$; and (b) the
contemporaneous DAG $A^{(0)}$ is identified up to Markov equivalence in general,
and \emph{uniquely} under Assumption~\ref{as:restrict}. Consequently the
population minimizer of the one-step reconstruction risk over the component-wise
B-spline class, subject to $h(W^{(0)})=0$, recovers the summary graph $A$ as the
spline grid refines.
\end{proposition}
Proposition~\ref{prop:identif} is an application to our function class of the
restricted-ANM identifiability theorem of \citet[Thm.~31]{peters2014causal} (in
turn built on \citealt{hoyer2008nonlinear}), combined with the spline
approximation guarantee for KAN regressors \citep{Liu2024,liu2025kanconvergence};
we give the argument in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. The content of
Assumption~\ref{as:restrict} is essential and honest: \emph{linear} mechanisms
with Gaussian noise are the textbook non-identifiable case, which is exactly why
a method built for the \emph{non-linear} regime (SPADE) can succeed where its
target is identifiable, while purely linear learners cannot orient such edges.

\paragraph{Why the bottleneck is necessary.} Identifiability can be forfeited by
the estimator even when the population target is well posed. The following makes
precise the empirical collapse we document in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}.
\begin{proposition}[Necessity of the information bottleneck]\label{prop:bottleneck}
Let a predictor model each $x_{t,i}$ through a shared function class
$\mathcal{F}$ of \emph{all} other coordinates that is rich enough to contain the
true conditional mean, and let an adjacency $\hat A$ be read post hoc from the
fitted predictor. Then the reconstruction risk depends on the fitted function
alone and not on $\hat A$; hence every adjacency consistent with the unconstrained
optimum attains the minimum risk, and $\hat A$ is \emph{not} identified by risk
minimization. Restricting the hypothesis class so that the adjacency indexes the
function---predicting each target \emph{only} through per-parent edges---is
sufficient to make the risk-minimizing structure unique.
\end{proposition}
The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) is elementary but consequential: it is the
formal reason a ``dense-backbone'' KAN forecaster recovers structure only at
chance (AUROC~$\approx 0.5$; Section~\ref{sec:ablation}), whereas the
component-wise SPADE does not.

\begin{remark}[Guarding against benchmark artifacts]\label{rem:varsort}
Continuous DAG learners can exploit \emph{varsortability}---an accidental
alignment between marginal variance and causal order in simulated
data---inflating apparent accuracy \citep{reisach2021beware,reisach2023scale}. We
neutralize this by standardizing every series to unit variance before fitting (so
marginal scale carries no order information) and by reporting threshold-free
AUROC/AUPRC rather than tuned-threshold scores. Our advantage therefore cannot be
attributed to a variance-ordering shortcut.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The SPADE Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

The central design principle of SPADE is an \emph{information bottleneck}: the
forecast of each target variable must flow \emph{only} through per-edge functions
of its candidate causes, with no shared network that re-mixes variables
afterwards. This is what makes the learned structure identifiable---the edge
functions are the sole path from a cause to an effect, so their magnitude is a
faithful structural signal (as in neural-Granger models,
\citealt{Tank2021,Shojaie2022}). We found this property to be decisive: a variant
that instead feeds the causal layer into a dense residual backbone recovers
structure only at chance, because the backbone can minimize forecasting error
regardless of the adjacency (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/cdkan_architecture.png}
    \caption{The SPADE pipeline. RevIN removes per-window level/scale shifts;
    the component-wise causal layer predicts each target $i$ as a sum of
    B-spline edge functions of its lagged causes---one spline per (effect, cause,
    lag)---and nothing else. Structure is read from each edge's contribution to
    the forecast; a group-lasso drives whole edges to zero; and (when
    contemporaneous edges are modelled) acyclicity is enforced on the lag-0 block
    via the Augmented Lagrangian Method.}
    \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{(1) Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN).}
Financial series are strongly non-stationary. We apply RevIN
\citep{Kim2022}: for each input window we subtract the window mean and divide by
the window standard deviation (statistics are computed on the input window only
and detached from the computation graph, so no future information leaks), apply a
learnable affine transform, and invert it on the output. Because the statistics
are window-local and detached, RevIN is an invertible reparameterization of
location and scale, and the causal layer operates on level/scale-removed signals.

\paragraph{(2) Component-wise causal layer with per-lag B-spline edges.}
For each ordered pair $(i,j)$ and lag $h$ we place a learnable B-spline edge
function, in the spirit of Kolmogorov--Arnold networks \citep{Liu2024},
\begin{equation}
    \phi^{(h)}_{ij}(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{G+p} c^{(ijh)}_k B_k(x),
    \label{eq:spline}
\end{equation}
where $\{B_k\}$ are order-$p$ B-spline bases on a grid of size $G$. The forecast
of target $i$ is the \emph{additive} aggregation of its incoming edges only,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{x}_{t,i} \;=\; b_i \;+\; \sum_{j=1}^{d}\ \sum_{h}\ \phi^{(h)}_{ij}\!\big(x_{t-h,\,j}\big),
    \label{eq:predict}
\end{equation}
with lags $h\in\{1,\dots,L\}$ (and $h=0$ added only when contemporaneous effects
are modelled). Crucially there is no downstream layer mixing the $\hat{x}_{t,i}$
across $i$; variable $j$ influences the forecast of $i$ solely through the edges
$\{\phi^{(h)}_{ij}\}$. Placing the learnable nonlinearity \emph{on the edge}
makes each mechanism inspectable---plotting $\phi^{(h)}_{ij}$ reveals whether an
influence is linear, saturating, or threshold-like (Section~\ref{sec:splines}).

\paragraph{(3) Structure and lag read-outs.}
Rather than reading structure from stochastic gating logits (which we found
unstable), we read it from the \emph{contribution} each edge makes to the
forecast. Let $c_{ij}(t)=\sum_h \phi^{(h)}_{ij}(x_{t-h,j})$ be the total signal
edge $(i,j)$ injects into target $i$. The edge score is its dispersion over the
data,
\begin{equation}
    S_{ij} \;=\; \mathrm{std}_t\big[c_{ij}(t)\big],
    \label{eq:importance}
\end{equation}
a data-grounded, deterministic measure of how much cause $j$ drives effect $i$.
The expected propagation delay is the contribution-weighted mean lag,
$\mathbb{E}[h]_{ij}=\sum_h h\,m^{(h)}_{ij}/\sum_h m^{(h)}_{ij}$ with
$m^{(h)}_{ij}=\mathrm{std}_t[\phi^{(h)}_{ij}(x_{t-h,j})]$.

\subsection{Constrained Optimization}
\label{sec:opt}

\paragraph{Acyclicity on the contemporaneous block only.}
Following the NOTEARS characterization \citep{Zheng2018}, a weighted matrix $W$ is
acyclic iff
\begin{equation}
    h(W) = \operatorname{tr}\!\big(e^{\,W\circ W}\big) - d = 0 .
    \label{eq:hnotears}
\end{equation}
Crucially, we apply Eq.~\eqref{eq:hnotears} \emph{only} to the contemporaneous
block $W_0$. Lagged blocks are left unconstrained because temporal precedence
already forbids cycles across time (Section~\ref{sec:identif}); constraining them would
incorrectly penalize legitimate feedback such as $x_{t-1}\!\to\! y_t$ and
$y_{t-1}\!\to\! x_t$. This block treatment mirrors DYNOTEARS \citep{Pamfil2020}
and resolves the limitation of a single flat $d\times d$ constraint.

\paragraph{Instantaneous variant (self-masked SEM).} When instantaneous effects
are modelled, SPADE realizes a non-linear structural equation model: it predicts
$x_{t,i}$ from its \emph{contemporaneous} parents $x_{t,j\neq i}$ (self-loops
masked) in addition to its lags, with $W_0$ the contemporaneous edge-importance
matrix scored as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:importance}. The ALM then drives
$h(W_0)\!\to\!0$, guaranteeing the instantaneous graph is a DAG so the SEM is
identifiable. This yields a non-linear, KAN-based analogue of DYNOTEARS; we
validate it on data with a known instantaneous DAG in Section~\ref{sec:contemp},
where it recovers the DAG and outperforms linear NOTEARS on non-linear mechanisms.

\paragraph{Objective.}
We fit the edge coefficients $\Phi=\{c^{(ijh)}\}$ and biases $b$ by minimizing
one-step forecasting error with a \emph{group-lasso} that treats each
cause$\to$effect edge as a group,
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\Phi,b}\;\; \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}
    \;+\; \lambda_{g}\sum_{i,j}\Big\lVert\, \mathrm{vec}\big(\{c^{(ijh)}\}_{h}\big)\,\Big\rVert_2
    \quad \text{s.t.}\quad h(W^{(0)}) = 0 ,
    \label{eq:constrained}
\end{equation}
where the group norm couples all lags of edge $(i,j)$ so that an entire edge
either survives or vanishes---the mechanism that yields sparse, identifiable
structure. The constraint $h(W^{(0)})=0$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:hnotears}), applied to the
contemporaneous importance block $W^{(0)}_{ij}=\mathrm{std}_t[\phi^{(0)}_{ij}]$,
is enforced only when instantaneous edges are modelled and is dualized with the
Augmented Lagrangian Method:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\Phi,b;\alpha,\rho) = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}
    + \lambda_{g}\,\Omega_{\mathrm{group}}
    + \alpha\, h(W^{(0)}) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\, h(W^{(0)})^2 .
    \label{eq:alm}
\end{equation}
For the purely-lagged benchmarks (all edges have $h\!\ge\!1$) the summary graph may
contain cycles across time, so the DAG term is inactive; it becomes relevant only
for datasets with instantaneous effects.

\paragraph{On the choice of the sparsity penalty.} We deliberately avoid an
$\ell_1$ penalty on stochastic gating logits, which recent work shows can bias
continuous DAG learning toward many small-magnitude edges and which we found
empirically unstable under temperature annealing \citep{ng2020golem,Bello2022}.
The group-lasso acts at the granularity of whole edges, and our threshold-free
evaluation (AUROC/AUPRC, Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}) reports ranking quality
independently of any single sparsity level. An $\ell_0$-style gate is a natural
extension.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{SPADE training}
\label{alg:cdkan}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} windows $\{(\mathbf{x}_{t-L:t},\mathbf{x}_t)\}$; group weight $\lambda_g$; (optional) $\rho_0{=}1,\alpha_0{=}0,\beta{=}2,K{=}10$
\STATE Initialize edge coefficients $\Phi$, biases $b$
\FOR{epoch $=1$ to $E$}
    \STATE $\hat{\mathbf{x}} \leftarrow$ component-wise forecast (Eq.~\ref{eq:predict})
    \STATE Adam step on $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}+\lambda_g\,\Omega_{\mathrm{group}}$ (+ ALM terms if contemporaneous); gradient-clip at $5.0$
    \IF{contemporaneous \textbf{and} epoch $\bmod\ K = 0$ \textbf{and} $h(W^{(0)})>10^{-8}$}
        \STATE $\rho \leftarrow \min(\beta\rho,10^{16})$;\quad $\alpha \leftarrow \alpha + \rho\,h(W^{(0)})$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Compute edge scores $S_{ij}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:importance}) and lags $\mathbb{E}[h]_{ij}$
\STATE \textbf{Return} scores $S$, expected lags, spline edge functions, forecaster
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:cdkan} lists the procedure. Training is a single Adam loop on
Eq.~\eqref{eq:constrained} with gradient clipping; when contemporaneous edges are
modelled, an ALM outer step every $K$ epochs tightens the acyclicity penalty. We
use $E{=}150$ epochs, learning rate $5\!\times\!10^{-3}$, grid size $G{=}8$, and
$\lambda_g{=}10^{-2}$ throughout; a sensitivity analysis over $\lambda_g$ and $G$
appears in Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}.

The purely-instantaneous specialization---the model behind our headline
result---deserves an explicit statement, as it is the cleanest instance of the
identifiability theory of Section~\ref{sec:identif}. Here each variable is
predicted from its contemporaneous parents only, self-loops are masked, and the
NOTEARS constraint of Theorem~\ref{thm:notears} is dualized on the edge-norm
matrix $W$ so the recovered graph is a DAG. Algorithm~\ref{alg:instant} gives the
full procedure; it is exactly the estimator whose population minimizer
Proposition~\ref{prop:identif} shows to be consistent.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{SPADE-Instantaneous: self-masked nonlinear SEM with acyclicity (ALM)}
\label{alg:instant}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} standardized data $X\in\Real^{N\times d}$; grid $G$; group
weight $\lambda_g$; learning rate $\eta$; epochs $E$; ALM schedule
$(\rho{=}1,\alpha{=}0,\beta{=}2,K{=}50)$, tolerance $\tau{=}10^{-8}$
\STATE Initialize spline coefficients $C\in\Real^{d\times d\times(G+p)}$, bias $b$; fix self-mask $M_{ii}{=}0,\,M_{ij}{=}1$
\FOR{epoch $=1$ to $E$}
    \STATE $\hat{x}_{\cdot i} \leftarrow b_i + \sum_{j\neq i}\phi_{ij}(x_{\cdot j})$ \quad(self-masked SEM forecast, Eq.~\ref{eq:predict})
    \STATE $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}} \leftarrow \tfrac1{Nd}\lVert \hat X - X\rVert_F^2$
    \STATE $W_{ij} \leftarrow \lVert (C\odot M)_{ij,:}\rVert_2$ \quad(edge-norm matrix)
    \STATE $h \leftarrow \operatorname{tr}(e^{W\circ W})-d$ \hfill(Theorem~\ref{thm:notears})
    \STATE $\mathcal{L} \leftarrow \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}} + \lambda_g\!\sum_{ij}W_{ij} + \alpha\,h + \tfrac{\rho}{2}h^2$
    \STATE Adam step on $\mathcal{L}$ with $\eta$; gradient-clip at $5.0$
    \IF{epoch $\bmod\ K = 0$ \textbf{and} $h>\tau$}
        \STATE $\alpha \leftarrow \alpha + \rho\,h$;\quad $\rho \leftarrow \min(\beta\rho,\,10^{10})$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE $S_{ij} \leftarrow \mathrm{std}_t\big[(C\odot M)_{ij}\!\cdot\! B(x_{t,j})\big]$ \quad(edge scores, $[\text{effect},\text{cause}]$)
\STATE \textbf{Return} $S$, spline edge functions $\{\phi_{ij}\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}
Per epoch, SPADE evaluates $O(d^2 L)$ edge splines, each $O(G)$, over $N$ windows,
giving $O(N d^2 L G)$ cost that is \emph{linear in the number of samples} $N$. The
optional acyclicity term adds an $O(d^3)$ matrix exponential of the
contemporaneous block. Because the model is a single component-wise layer with no
deep backbone, it is fast in practice (seconds at $d{=}5$; see
Section~\ref{sec:scalability}) and suited to systems of moderate width; we
quantify wall-clock and parameter growth with $d$ empirically and discuss high-$d$
extensions in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
