
\section{The SPADE Artifact}
\label{sec:method}

We present SPADE (SPline Additive-noise DAG Estimation), built on
Kolmogorov--Arnold networks, a design-science
artifact that jointly performs multivariate time-series forecasting and causal
structure learning. This section gives the complete specification needed for
reproduction: the problem formulation and its identifiability assumptions
(Section~\ref{sec:formulation}), the lag-aware architecture
(Section~\ref{sec:arch}), the constrained-optimization procedure with its full
hyperparameter schedule (Section~\ref{sec:opt}), and a complexity analysis
(Section~\ref{sec:complexity}). A reference implementation is available in the
accompanying repository.

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Assumptions}
\label{sec:formulation}

Let $\mathbf{X} = \{\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_T\}$ be a multivariate time
series with $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$. We assume a non-linear Structural
Vector Autoregressive (SVAR) data-generating process with a maximum lag $L$:
\begin{equation}
    x_{t,j} \;=\; \sum_{h=1}^{L} \sum_{i=1}^{d} A^{(h)}_{ij}\, f^{(h)}_{ij}\!\big(x_{t-h,i}\big)
    \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{d} A^{(0)}_{ij}\, f^{(0)}_{ij}\!\big(x_{t,i}\big)
    \;+\; \epsilon_{t,j},
    \label{eq:svar}
\end{equation}
where $A^{(h)} \in \{0,1\}^{d\times d}$ is the \emph{lag-$h$} adjacency
($A^{(h)}_{ij}=1$ iff variable $i$ at lag $h$ is a direct cause of variable $j$),
$f^{(h)}_{ij}$ are (possibly non-linear) causal mechanisms, and $\epsilon_{t,j}$
is exogenous noise. We explicitly separate \emph{lagged} adjacencies
$A^{(1)},\dots,A^{(L)}$ from the \emph{contemporaneous} adjacency $A^{(0)}$
because they require different treatment (Section~\ref{sec:opt}). The summary
graph used for reporting is the union $A_{ij} = 1 - \prod_{h=0}^{L}(1-A^{(h)}_{ij})$.

Our goal is twofold: (i) \textbf{forecasting}---predict $\mathbf{x}_{t+1}$ from
history $\mathbf{x}_{t-L:t}$; and (ii) \textbf{causal discovery}---recover the
$\{A^{(h)}\}_{h=0}^{L}$ and the mechanisms $f^{(h)}_{ij}$.

\paragraph{Identifiability assumptions.} Directed edges recovered from purely
observational data are only interpretable as causal under stated assumptions.
We make these explicit (following \citealt{Pearl2009,Runge2019}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item[\textbf{A1}] \emph{Causal sufficiency}: there are no unobserved common
    causes (confounders) of the measured variables. In open financial systems
    this is a strong assumption; we therefore treat real-data edges as
    \emph{candidate} mechanisms for expert review rather than verified causes
    (Section~\ref{sec:real}).
    \item[\textbf{A2}] \emph{Causal Markov and faithfulness}: the joint
    distribution is Markov and faithful to the SVAR graph.
    \item[\textbf{A3}] \emph{Weak stationarity of the causal mechanism}: the
    graph and functions $\{A^{(h)}, f^{(h)}\}$ are constant within each
    estimation window. We probe violations directly through the rolling-window
    stability analysis of Section~\ref{sec:stability}.
    \item[\textbf{A4}] \emph{Acyclic contemporaneous structure}: instantaneous
    effects $A^{(0)}$ form a DAG. Lagged effects need not be acyclic---time's
    arrow already orders them---so we enforce acyclicity only on $A^{(0)}$
    (cf.\ DYNOTEARS, \citealt{Pamfil2020}).
\end{itemize}
These assumptions delimit the boundary conditions of the artifact and are
revisited in the Discussion.

\subsection{The SPADE Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

The central design principle of SPADE is an \emph{information bottleneck}: the
forecast of each target variable must flow \emph{only} through per-edge functions
of its candidate causes, with no shared network that re-mixes variables
afterwards. This is what makes the learned structure identifiable---the edge
functions are the sole path from a cause to an effect, so their magnitude is a
faithful structural signal (as in neural-Granger models,
\citealt{Tank2021,Shojaie2022}). We found this property to be decisive: a variant
that instead feeds the causal layer into a dense residual backbone recovers
structure only at chance, because the backbone can minimize forecasting error
regardless of the adjacency (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

    via the Augmented Lagrangian Method.}
    \label{fig:architecture}

\paragraph{(1) Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN).}
Financial series are strongly non-stationary. We apply RevIN
\citep{Kim2022}: for each input window we subtract the window mean and divide by
the window standard deviation (statistics are computed on the input window only
and detached from the computation graph, so no future information leaks), apply a
learnable affine transform, and invert it on the output. Because the statistics
are window-local and detached, RevIN is an invertible reparameterization of
location and scale, and the causal layer operates on level/scale-removed signals.

\paragraph{(2) Component-wise causal layer with per-lag B-spline edges.}
For each ordered pair $(i,j)$ and lag $h$ we place a learnable B-spline edge
function, in the spirit of Kolmogorov--Arnold networks \citep{Liu2024},
\begin{equation}
    \phi^{(h)}_{ij}(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{G+p} c^{(ijh)}_k B_k(x),
    \label{eq:spline}
\end{equation}
where $\{B_k\}$ are order-$p$ B-spline bases on a grid of size $G$. The forecast
of target $i$ is the \emph{additive} aggregation of its incoming edges only,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{x}_{t,i} \;=\; b_i \;+\; \sum_{j=1}^{d}\ \sum_{h}\ \phi^{(h)}_{ij}\!\big(x_{t-h,\,j}\big),
    \label{eq:predict}
\end{equation}
with lags $h\in\{1,\dots,L\}$ (and $h=0$ added only when contemporaneous effects
are modelled). Crucially there is no downstream layer mixing the $\hat{x}_{t,i}$
across $i$; variable $j$ influences the forecast of $i$ solely through the edges
$\{\phi^{(h)}_{ij}\}$. Placing the learnable nonlinearity \emph{on the edge}
makes each mechanism inspectable---plotting $\phi^{(h)}_{ij}$ reveals whether an
influence is linear, saturating, or threshold-like (Section~\ref{sec:splines}).

\paragraph{(3) Structure and lag read-outs.}
Rather than reading structure from stochastic gating logits (which we found
unstable), we read it from the \emph{contribution} each edge makes to the
forecast. Let $c_{ij}(t)=\sum_h \phi^{(h)}_{ij}(x_{t-h,j})$ be the total signal
edge $(i,j)$ injects into target $i$. The edge score is its dispersion over the
data,
\begin{equation}
    S_{ij} \;=\; \mathrm{std}_t\big[c_{ij}(t)\big],
    \label{eq:importance}
\end{equation}
a data-grounded, deterministic measure of how much cause $j$ drives effect $i$.
The expected propagation delay is the contribution-weighted mean lag,
$\mathbb{E}[h]_{ij}=\sum_h h\,m^{(h)}_{ij}/\sum_h m^{(h)}_{ij}$ with
$m^{(h)}_{ij}=\mathrm{std}_t[\phi^{(h)}_{ij}(x_{t-h,j})]$.

\subsection{Constrained Optimization}
\label{sec:opt}

\paragraph{Acyclicity on the contemporaneous block only.}
Following the NOTEARS characterization \citep{Zheng2018}, a weighted matrix $W$ is
acyclic iff
\begin{equation}
    h(W) = \operatorname{tr}\!\big(e^{\,W\circ W}\big) - d = 0 .
    \label{eq:hnotears}
\end{equation}
Crucially, we apply Eq.~\eqref{eq:hnotears} \emph{only} to the contemporaneous
block $W_0$. Lagged blocks are left unconstrained because temporal precedence
already forbids cycles across time (assumption A4); constraining them would
incorrectly penalize legitimate feedback such as $x_{t-1}\!\to\! y_t$ and
$y_{t-1}\!\to\! x_t$. This block treatment mirrors DYNOTEARS \citep{Pamfil2020}
and resolves the limitation of a single flat $d\times d$ constraint.

\paragraph{Instantaneous variant (self-masked SEM).} When instantaneous effects
are modelled, SPADE realizes a non-linear structural equation model: it predicts
$x_{t,i}$ from its \emph{contemporaneous} parents $x_{t,j\neq i}$ (self-loops
masked) in addition to its lags, with $W_0$ the contemporaneous edge-importance
matrix scored as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:importance}. The ALM then drives
$h(W_0)\!\to\!0$, guaranteeing the instantaneous graph is a DAG so the SEM is
identifiable. This yields a non-linear, KAN-based analogue of DYNOTEARS; we
validate it on data with a known instantaneous DAG in Section~\ref{sec:contemp},
where it recovers the DAG and outperforms linear NOTEARS on non-linear mechanisms.

\paragraph{Objective.}
We fit the edge coefficients $\Phi=\{c^{(ijh)}\}$ and biases $b$ by minimizing
one-step forecasting error with a \emph{group-lasso} that treats each
cause$\to$effect edge as a group,
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\Phi,b}\;\; \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}
    \;+\; \lambda_{g}\sum_{i,j}\Big\lVert\, \mathrm{vec}\big(\{c^{(ijh)}\}_{h}\big)\,\Big\rVert_2
    \quad \text{s.t.}\quad h(W^{(0)}) = 0 ,
    \label{eq:constrained}
\end{equation}
where the group norm couples all lags of edge $(i,j)$ so that an entire edge
either survives or vanishes---the mechanism that yields sparse, identifiable
structure. The constraint $h(W^{(0)})=0$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:hnotears}), applied to the
contemporaneous importance block $W^{(0)}_{ij}=\mathrm{std}_t[\phi^{(0)}_{ij}]$,
is enforced only when instantaneous edges are modelled and is dualized with the
Augmented Lagrangian Method:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\Phi,b;\alpha,\rho) = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}
    + \lambda_{g}\,\Omega_{\mathrm{group}}
    + \alpha\, h(W^{(0)}) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\, h(W^{(0)})^2 .
    \label{eq:alm}
\end{equation}
For the purely-lagged benchmarks (all edges have $h\!\ge\!1$) the summary graph may
contain cycles across time, so the DAG term is inactive; it becomes relevant only
for datasets with instantaneous effects.

\paragraph{On the choice of the sparsity penalty.} We deliberately avoid an
$\ell_1$ penalty on stochastic gating logits, which recent work shows can bias
continuous DAG learning toward many small-magnitude edges and which we found
empirically unstable under temperature annealing \citep{Ng2024CALM,Bello2022}.
The group-lasso acts at the granularity of whole edges, and our threshold-free
evaluation (AUROC/AUPRC, Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}) reports ranking quality
independently of any single sparsity level. An $\ell_0$-style gate is a natural
extension.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{SPADE training}
\label{alg:cdkan}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} windows $\{(\mathbf{x}_{t-L:t},\mathbf{x}_t)\}$; group weight $\lambda_g$; (optional) $\rho_0{=}1,\alpha_0{=}0,\beta{=}2,K{=}10$
\STATE Initialize edge coefficients $\Phi$, biases $b$
\FOR{epoch $=1$ to $E$}
    \STATE $\hat{\mathbf{x}} \leftarrow$ component-wise forecast (Eq.~\ref{eq:predict})
    \STATE Adam step on $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}+\lambda_g\,\Omega_{\mathrm{group}}$ (+ ALM terms if contemporaneous); gradient-clip at $5.0$
    \IF{contemporaneous \textbf{and} epoch $\bmod\ K = 0$ \textbf{and} $h(W^{(0)})>10^{-8}$}
        \STATE $\rho \leftarrow \min(\beta\rho,10^{16})$;\quad $\alpha \leftarrow \alpha + \rho\,h(W^{(0)})$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Compute edge scores $S_{ij}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:importance}) and lags $\mathbb{E}[h]_{ij}$
\STATE \textbf{Return} scores $S$, expected lags, spline edge functions, forecaster
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:cdkan} lists the procedure. Training is a single Adam loop on
Eq.~\eqref{eq:constrained} with gradient clipping; when contemporaneous edges are
modelled, an ALM outer step every $K$ epochs tightens the acyclicity penalty. We
use $E{=}150$ epochs, learning rate $5\!\times\!10^{-3}$, grid size $G{=}8$, and
$\lambda_g{=}10^{-2}$ throughout; a sensitivity analysis over $\lambda_g$ and $G$
appears in Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}.

\subsection{Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}
Per epoch, SPADE evaluates $O(d^2 L)$ edge splines, each $O(G)$, over $N$ windows,
giving $O(N d^2 L G)$ cost that is \emph{linear in the number of samples} $N$. The
optional acyclicity term adds an $O(d^3)$ matrix exponential of the
contemporaneous block. Because the model is a single component-wise layer with no
deep backbone, it is fast in practice (seconds at $d{=}5$; see
Section~\ref{sec:scalability}) and suited to systems of moderate width; we
quantify wall-clock and parameter growth with $d$ empirically and discuss high-$d$
extensions in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
