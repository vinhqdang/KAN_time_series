
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Organizations increasingly rely on advanced analytics for strategic
decision-making. From portfolio management to supply-chain optimization and
macroeconomic planning, accurate forecasts of future states are a competitive
advantage \citep{Davenport2018}. Yet as predictive models have grown more
sophisticated---from linear regressions to deep neural networks---a well-known
tension has intensified in Information Systems (IS) research: the trade-off
between predictive accuracy and interpretability \citep{Rai2020,Shmueli2010}.

Managerial decision-making is rarely about prediction alone; it is about
understanding \emph{why} an outcome will occur. A portfolio manager needs to know
not merely that an asset will fall, but whether the driver is a rate change, a
commodity shock, or shifting sentiment. A supply-chain director needs to know
\emph{which} upstream delays cascade downstream. Without a causal account, even
accurate predictions can lead to correct actions for the wrong reasons and leave
managers unable to intervene when conditions shift \citep{Pearl2009}.

Existing decision-support artifacts occupy two camps. \textbf{Statistical and
econometric models} (VAR, ARIMA) offer transparent structure but struggle with
the non-linear dependencies of modern business data. \textbf{Deep learning
models} (LSTMs, Transformers) achieve strong accuracy but act as opaque
``black boxes'' \citep{Benbasat2021}, limiting trust and the explanatory power
needed for strategic sense-making \citep{Gregor2006}. We address this gap with
\textbf{SPADE} (SPline Additive-noise DAG Estimation), a Kolmogorov--Arnold-network
artifact that couples interpretable, edge-wise function learning with
differentiable causal structure learning, designed within the IS design-science
paradigm \citep{Hevner2004}.

\subsection{Related work and positioning}
\label{sec:related}

SPADE sits at the confluence of four literatures---continuous DAG learning,
temporal causal discovery, the identifiability theory that says when either is
meaningful, and the nascent use of Kolmogorov--Arnold networks for interpretable
modelling. We review each and state precisely where our artifact departs.

\paragraph{From combinatorial to continuous DAG learning.} Learning a directed
acyclic graph was long a combinatorial search over super-exponentially many
structures. NOTEARS \citep{Zheng2018} dissolved this obstacle with a smooth
algebraic characterization of acyclicity---$h(W)=\operatorname{tr}(e^{W\circ
W})-d=0$---recasting structure learning as continuous optimization. Refinements
followed quickly: GOLEM \citep{ng2020golem} showed a likelihood objective with
soft sparsity often suffices; DAGMA \citep{Bello2022} replaced the
trace-exponential with a better-conditioned log-determinant potential; and
\citet{zhang2025analytic} derive an entire family of analytic acyclicity
constraints closed under differentiation. The nonlinear branch of this
family---nonparametric NOTEARS \citep{zheng2020sparse} and gradient-based neural
DAG learning (GraN-DAG, \citealt{lachapelle2020grandag})---parameterizes each
mechanism with a multilayer perceptron and reads structure from the network's
input Jacobian. These are simultaneously our closest algorithmic relatives and
our principal baselines; the decisive difference is architectural
(Section~\ref{sec:identif}).

\paragraph{Causal discovery for time series.} Temporal data bring a blessing
(time orders cause before effect) and a curse (lagged and instantaneous effects
coexist, and series are seldom stationary). Constraint-based PCMCI
\citep{Runge2019}, restricted-SEM methods (TiMINo, \citealt{Peters2013TiMINo}),
and non-Gaussian VAR-LiNGAM \citep{Hyvarinen2010} remain strong, widely used
references. DYNOTEARS \citep{Pamfil2020} lifted NOTEARS to dynamic Bayesian
networks by separating a contemporaneous block from lagged blocks---an idea SPADE
inherits---but kept \emph{linear} structural equations. The deep-learning wave has
since produced far more expressive temporal learners: NTS-NOTEARS
\citep{sun2023ntsnotears} uses 1-D convolutions for nonlinear lagged and
instantaneous edges; Rhino \citep{Gong2023Rhino} models history-dependent noise;
CUTS and CUTS+ \citep{Cheng2023CUTS,cheng2024cutsplus} handle irregular sampling;
and the neural-Granger family \citep{Tank2021,Shojaie2022,zhou2024jacobian},
recently extended to xLSTM backbones \citep{poonia2025gcxlstm}, learns nonlinear
predictive dependencies. A parallel effort pushes toward amortized or
foundation-model discovery---AVICI \citep{lorch2022avici}, DECI
\citep{geffner2022deci}, and 2024--2026 transformer-based learners
\citep{stein2024causalpretraining,shen2024stic,misiakos2025spinsvar,faruque2026ttcd,fesanghary2026grace}---while
application-driven systems such as CausalStock \citep{li2024causalstock} bring
causal discovery directly into financial decision support. Powerful as these are,
nearly all return the graph as a black box: the \emph{mechanism} on each edge is
not itself an inspectable object. SPADE's distinguishing aim is competitive
recovery \emph{with} per-edge interpretable functions.

\paragraph{When is any of this identifiable?} A directed edge is recoverable from
observational data only under conditions, and ignoring them is how simulated
benchmarks mislead. The additive-noise-model theory of \citet{hoyer2008nonlinear}
and \citet{peters2014causal} establishes the fact SPADE is built around: a
\emph{nonlinear} additive-noise mechanism is identifiable up to its graph, whereas
the linear--Gaussian case provably is not. Score-matching methods exploit exactly
this---SCORE \citep{rolland2022score} and NoGAM \citep{montagna2023nogam} order
variables through the Hessian of the log-density---confirming that nonlinearity is
the resource that makes orientation possible. Conversely,
\citet{reisach2021beware,reisach2023scale} warn that continuous learners can
``cheat'' on simulated data by exploiting \emph{varsortability}, an artifact of
marginal scale; we neutralize this by standardizing every series and reporting
threshold-free metrics (Remark~\ref{rem:varsort}). This literature motivates our
central design choice: because identifiability lives in the nonlinear regime, an
estimator with \emph{flexible per-edge mechanisms} should have an advantage there
over fixed-architecture learners---a hypothesis our headline experiment confirms.

\paragraph{Kolmogorov--Arnold networks and KAN-based causality.} KANs
\citep{Liu2024,liu2024kan2} replace the fixed activations of an MLP with learnable
univariate functions on edges---typically B-splines---and carry approximation
guarantees, including minimax-optimal nonparametric regression rates for
spline-based variants \citep{liu2025kanconvergence}. They have been adopted
rapidly for interpretable time-series forecasting
\citep{xu2024kantimeseries,genet2024tkan,liang2025itfkan,vacarubio2025pkan}.
Closest to us, GC-KAN \citep{GCKAN2024} and KANGCI \citep{liu2025kangci} use KAN
edges for \emph{Granger} causality via sparse regularization, but neither imposes
an acyclicity constraint nor separates lagged from contemporaneous structure. Our
contribution relative to this line is precise: SPADE (i) places a NOTEARS
acyclicity constraint on the contemporaneous block via an Augmented Lagrangian,
(ii) maintains an explicit per-lag adjacency, and (iii) grounds the design in ANM
identifiability. To isolate the value of the DAG constraint, a
\emph{GC-KAN$+$ALM} ablation (Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}) adds our acyclicity
penalty to a GC-KAN backbone, disentangling the constraint's contribution from
that of the KAN edge functions.

\subsection{Contributions}
We frame SPADE as an interpretable non-linear causal-discovery artifact and make
three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{An identifiable, interpretable architecture.} SPADE predicts
    each variable \emph{only} through per-(cause, lag) B-spline edges of its
    candidate parents---an information bottleneck---with an edge-level group-lasso
    and an optional contemporaneous-only acyclicity constraint. We show this
    bottleneck is what makes structure identifiable: an otherwise-similar model
    with a dense forecasting backbone recovers structure only at chance.
    \item \textbf{A decisive result on non-linear instantaneous structure.} On
    additive-noise instantaneous DAGs, SPADE attains AUROC~$\approx$~0.88 across
    $d\in\{6,10,20\}$, substantially outperforming the strongest DAG learners---
    DAGMA (linear and non-linear) and NOTEARS/NOTEARS-MLP---because learnable
    spline edges capture non-linear mechanisms that fixed-architecture learners
    miss. On lagged (temporal) structure it is competitive (perfect on linear,
    beating KAN-Granger baselines and NOTEARS on non-linear) though the best
    conditioning-based methods (PCMCI, VAR-LiNGAM) still lead there; we report this
    honestly and retract the earlier, unreproducible F1~=~0.897 and MSE~=~0.0008.
    \item \textbf{Validation beyond stability, and an interpretation layer.}
    Spline edge functions expose the \emph{shape} of each dependency (linear,
    saturating, threshold) and lag attribution recovers known delays on synthetic
    data. On real markets the learned graph is stable $4.7\times$ above a
    permutation chance level ($p<0.0001$) and degrades under time-reversal (showing
    it uses temporal precedence); on a controlled synthetic intervention it detects
    a known structural break and recovers both regime graphs. We also report
    candidly that documented real-world policy shocks are \emph{not} cleanly
    recoverable from observational macro data---an honest limit of the setting.
    SPADE scales to $d{=}50$ in seconds via a vectorized implementation.
\end{enumerate}
