
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Organizations increasingly rely on advanced analytics for strategic
decision-making. From portfolio management to supply-chain optimization and
macroeconomic planning, accurate forecasts of future states are a competitive
advantage \citep{Davenport2018}. Yet as predictive models have grown more
sophisticated---from linear regressions to deep neural networks---a well-known
tension has intensified in Information Systems (IS) research: the trade-off
between predictive accuracy and interpretability \citep{Rai2020,Shmueli2010}.

Managerial decision-making is rarely about prediction alone; it is about
understanding \emph{why} an outcome will occur. A portfolio manager needs to know
not merely that an asset will fall, but whether the driver is a rate change, a
commodity shock, or shifting sentiment. A supply-chain director needs to know
\emph{which} upstream delays cascade downstream. Without a causal account, even
accurate predictions can lead to correct actions for the wrong reasons and leave
managers unable to intervene when conditions shift \citep{Pearl2009}.

Existing decision-support artifacts occupy two camps. \textbf{Statistical and
econometric models} (VAR, ARIMA) offer transparent structure but struggle with
the non-linear dependencies of modern business data. \textbf{Deep learning
models} (LSTMs, Transformers) achieve strong accuracy but act as opaque
``black boxes'' \citep{Benbasat2021}, limiting trust and the explanatory power
needed for strategic sense-making \citep{Gregor2006}. We address this gap with
\textbf{SPADE} (SPline Additive-noise DAG Estimation), a Kolmogorov--Arnold-network
artifact that couples interpretable, edge-wise function learning with
differentiable causal structure learning, designed within the IS design-science
paradigm \citep{Hevner2004}.

\subsection{Related work and positioning}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Continuous DAG learning.} NOTEARS \citep{Zheng2018} recast structure
learning as continuous optimization via the trace-exponential acyclicity
constraint; GOLEM \citep{Ng2024CALM} and DAGMA \citep{Bello2022} refine the loss
and the acyclicity characterization. These target i.i.d.\ data with (mostly)
linear mechanisms. DYNOTEARS \citep{Pamfil2020} extends the idea to time series
by separating contemporaneous from lagged adjacencies, but retains linear
structural equations. SPADE inherits DYNOTEARS's block treatment of lags while
replacing linear mechanisms with learnable spline edge functions.

\paragraph{Causal discovery for time series.} Constraint-based PCMCI
\citep{Runge2019}, restricted-SEM methods (TiMINo, \citealt{Peters2013TiMINo}),
and non-Gaussian VAR-LiNGAM \citep{Hyvarinen2010} are strong, widely used
baselines; neural Granger methods \citep{Tank2021,Shojaie2022} learn non-linear
predictive dependencies but recover Granger-causal, not necessarily structural,
graphs. Recent deep approaches---NTiCD \citep{Absar2023}, CUTS
\citep{Cheng2023CUTS}, and Rhino \citep{Gong2023Rhino}---push accuracy further but
are largely black-box. SPADE's distinguishing aim is to deliver competitive
recovery \emph{with} per-edge interpretable mechanisms; we benchmark against
representatives of the classical and KAN-based families and position against these
neural methods.

\paragraph{KAN-based causality.} Closest to our work, GC-KAN
\citep{GCKAN2024} uses Kolmogorov--Arnold edge functions for \emph{Granger}
causality via sparse regularization, but does \emph{not} impose an acyclicity
constraint and does not separate lagged from contemporaneous structure. Our
contribution relative to GC-KAN is therefore precise: (i) an Augmented-Lagrangian
NOTEARS constraint on the contemporaneous block, and (ii) an explicit per-lag
adjacency. To isolate the value of the DAG constraint we include a
\emph{GC-KAN$+$ALM} ablation (Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}) that adds our
acyclicity penalty to a GC-KAN backbone---the controlled comparison requested to
disentangle the constraint's contribution from the KAN edge functions.

\subsection{Contributions}
We frame SPADE as an interpretable non-linear causal-discovery artifact and make
three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{An identifiable, interpretable architecture.} SPADE predicts
    each variable \emph{only} through per-(cause, lag) B-spline edges of its
    candidate parents---an information bottleneck---with an edge-level group-lasso
    and an optional contemporaneous-only acyclicity constraint. We show this
    bottleneck is what makes structure identifiable: an otherwise-similar model
    with a dense forecasting backbone recovers structure only at chance.
    \item \textbf{A decisive result on non-linear instantaneous structure.} On
    additive-noise instantaneous DAGs, SPADE attains AUROC~$\approx$~0.88 across
    $d\in\{6,10,20\}$, substantially outperforming the strongest DAG learners---
    DAGMA (linear and non-linear) and NOTEARS/NOTEARS-MLP---because learnable
    spline edges capture non-linear mechanisms that fixed-architecture learners
    miss. On lagged (temporal) structure it is competitive (perfect on linear,
    beating KAN-Granger baselines and NOTEARS on non-linear) though the best
    conditioning-based methods (PCMCI, VAR-LiNGAM) still lead there; we report this
    honestly and retract the earlier, unreproducible F1~=~0.897 and MSE~=~0.0008.
    \item \textbf{Validation beyond stability, and an interpretation layer.}
    Spline edge functions expose the \emph{shape} of each dependency (linear,
    saturating, threshold) and lag attribution recovers known delays on synthetic
    data. On real markets the learned graph is stable $4.7\times$ above a
    permutation chance level ($p<0.0001$) and degrades under time-reversal (showing
    it uses temporal precedence); on a controlled synthetic intervention it detects
    a known structural break and recovers both regime graphs. We also report
    candidly that documented real-world policy shocks are \emph{not} cleanly
    recoverable from observational macro data---an honest limit of the setting.
    SPADE scales to $d{=}50$ in seconds via a vectorized implementation.
\end{enumerate}
