
\section{Discussion: managerial implications and boundary conditions}
\label{sec:discussion}

The evaluations position SPADE not as a uniformly dominant algorithm but as an
artifact that unifies forecasting with interpretable, lag-resolved structure
learning. We discuss what this affords managers, and---equally important---where
the artifact should not be trusted.

\subsection{From prediction to strategic sense-making}
Traditional forecasting systems act as ``oracles,'' returning predictions without
context. SPADE instead supports \emph{sense-making} \citep{Weick1995}: the
learned graph and its edge functions let managers ask \emph{why} a movement is
expected. In the financial application, SPADE surfaced lagged dependencies
(e.g., a USD-index effect on gold at a one-day lag) together with the
\emph{shape} of the mechanism from the spline (Section~\ref{sec:splines}).
Whether a driver is linear, saturating, or threshold-like changes the managerial
response---linear drivers justify proportional hedging, whereas saturating ones
imply diminishing returns to further exposure. \textbf{This illustration carries
the caveat established in Section~\ref{sec:returns}, not only the boundary-
conditions list below}: on this same panel, redoing the analysis on a
stationary (returns) representation collapses the graph to an uninformative,
fully-connected structure, so the specific edges cited here (and the hedging
logic built on them) should be read as evidence of shared, likely
non-stationary co-movement rather than a validated short-horizon causal
driver a manager should act on directly.

\subsection{A non-financial illustration: demand planning}
\label{sec:demandplan}
The bike-sharing study (Section~\ref{sec:bike}) shows the same workflow on data
where the answer can be checked. A fleet planner reading SPADE's learned graph
sees, first, which relationships the model trusts: calendar and lagged demand
drive current demand, and no edge points from demand back to the weather.
Second, the edge functions give magnitudes a planner can act on. Switching a day
from non-working to working changes the learned contribution to casual (leisure)
ridership by $-0.77$ standard deviations (range $-0.89$ to $-0.68$ across six
resamples) and to registered (commuter) ridership by $+0.51$ ($0.42$ to $0.69$);
the \emph{mix} of demand, not only its total, therefore shifts on working days,
which bears on where bikes are rebalanced and how many staff are rostered at
commuter versus leisure stations. Third, the structure can be audited against
knowledge the planner already has, which is how the BK-AUROC of
Section~\ref{sec:bike} turns ``the graph looks plausible'' into a measurable
check. These are decision-support readings of a \emph{candidate} structure, not
causal effect estimates: events, holidays, and fleet size are unobserved, and the
temperature edge to casual riders was too weakly supported to interpret.

\subsection{Risk management and intervention}
A causal (rather than merely correlational) map identifies candidate leverage
points: if $X$ and $Y$ are both driven by a confounder $Z$, intervening on $X$
will not move $Y$. SPADE's contemporaneous-DAG constraint and lag attribution
help distinguish plausible directional drivers from co-movement. We stress
\emph{candidate}: under observational data and Assumption~\ref{as:suff} (causal
sufficiency), unobserved confounders may still induce spurious edges, so the
graph is a hypothesis generator for expert review, not a substitute for
controlled experiments or natural-experiment identification. On the financial
panel specifically, this is not a hypothetical caveat: Section~\ref{sec:returns}
shows the levels-based graph does not survive a stationarity check, so before
any leverage point identified here is used to justify an intervention, a
practitioner should treat it as (at most) a candidate worth independent
investigation, not as validated causal evidence---a distinction that matters
most exactly where risk or capital-allocation decisions are on the line.

\subsection{Fostering trust through structural transparency}
The ``black box'' nature of deep models impedes adoption in high-stakes settings
\citep{Rudin2019}. SPADE offers a ``glass box'': an inspectable adjacency,
explicit per-edge functions, and propagation lags that can be audited against
domain knowledge. Counter-intuitive edges are visible and can be flagged,
supporting a human-in-the-loop workflow rather than opaque automation. We
deliberately avoid over-claiming ``interpretability'': a decomposable B-spline is
inspectable, but inspectability is necessary, not sufficient, for human
understanding. Quantifying interpretability (e.g., via expert user studies)
remains future work.

\subsection{Limitations and boundary conditions}
\label{sec:limits}
Several boundary conditions delimit responsible use:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Causal sufficiency and observational data
    (Assumptions~\ref{as:suff}--\ref{as:markov}).} Recovered
    directions are only causal under the stated assumptions; in open markets with
    latent macro drivers they are best read as structured hypotheses.
    \item \textbf{Stationarity (Assumption~\ref{as:stat}).} The mechanism is assumed constant within an
    estimation window. Our rolling-window stability test
    (Section~\ref{sec:stability}) quantifies, rather than assumes, temporal
    constancy; it also shows that window design (length and overlap) materially
    affects apparent stability, which practitioners must report. More
    fundamentally, Section~\ref{sec:returns} shows that redoing the real-data
    analysis on a genuinely stationary representation (log-returns rather than
    price levels) collapses the learned graph to an uninformative,
    fully-connected structure, because daily returns on these assets carry
    little one-step-ahead predictive signal: the sparse, stable graph reported
    on price levels should be read as evidence of shared non-stationary
    co-movement, not as a validated short-horizon causal structure that
    survives a stationarity check.
    \item \textbf{Where SPADE stands vs.\ classical methods.} SPADE matches the
    mature linear estimators on \emph{linear} lagged structure at every width we
    test, including $d{=}50$ (AUROC~0.999). On \emph{non-linear} lagged
    structure it is the best of the flexible neural/KAN learners: it
    significantly beats NOTEARS, Neural-GC, Correlation, and the KAN-Granger
    variant GC-KAN$+$ALM (Wilcoxon $p<0.05$) and is on par with GC-KAN and
    NTS-NOTEARS. The strongest conditioning-based, non-Gaussianity-exploiting,
    and sparse-linear methods (PCMCI, VAR-LiNGAM, VAR-Lasso) and the linear
    learner GOLEM retain an edge there, and all classical methods are far faster
    (Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}). On \emph{instantaneous} non-linear structure,
    SPADE decisively beats every fixed-architecture, gradient-based DAG learner
    and the score-matching method SCORE, at a fraction of the compute cost;
    against NoGAM the result is mixed (ahead at $d{=}6,20$, behind at $d{=}10$),
    though still at a fraction of NoGAM's compute throughout
    (Section~\ref{sec:instdag}). When \emph{only} a structure estimate is needed
    and interpretability of mechanisms is not required, a fast classical method
    may still suffice on \emph{lagged} structure, where PCMCI and VAR-LiNGAM keep
    the edge; SPADE's advantage is delivering competitive structure recovery
    \emph{together} with interpretable edge shapes and a coupled forecaster.
    \item \textbf{The $\ell_1$ penalty.} Consistent with recent critiques
    \citep{ng2020golem,Bello2022}, the $\ell_1$ sparsity term can bias structure
    recovery; our group-lasso term and threshold-free reporting mitigate but do
    not eliminate this.
    \item \textbf{Scalability.} The acyclicity term is $O(d^3)$ and the Python
    edge-wise spline evaluation adds constant overhead per edge, so wall-clock
    grows steeply with $d$ (Section~\ref{sec:scalability}). SPADE currently
    suits moderate-width systems (dozens of variables---departmental KPIs,
    macro indicators, asset baskets), not thousands of high-frequency series.
    Kernelized or low-rank acyclicity and vectorized spline evaluation are
    natural remedies.
\end{itemize}
These conditions are not incidental caveats but define the artifact's intended
scope: a moderate-dimensional, non-linear, interpretability-first decision-support
tool.
