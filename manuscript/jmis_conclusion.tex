
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented CD-KAN, an information-systems artifact that couples
Kolmogorov--Arnold edge functions with differentiable, lag-aware DAG learning to
produce, from one differentiable model, both a one-step forecast and an
interpretable causal graph. Methodologically, CD-KAN separates lagged from
contemporaneous adjacencies and enforces acyclicity only on the contemporaneous
block via an Augmented Lagrangian scheme---a formulation we specify in full,
including its hyperparameter schedule and convergence criteria.

Our evaluation is deliberately candid. On synthetic data with known ground truth,
CD-KAN recovers linear structure perfectly and is competitive on non-linear
structure---significantly outperforming KAN-based Granger baselines and matching
several classical methods, though not the very strongest (PCMCI, VAR-LiNGAM) on
the hardest cases. The key design lesson is that this identifiability hinges on an
information bottleneck: predicting each variable only through its incoming edges,
rather than through a dense forecasting backbone (which we show collapses to
chance). We retract the earlier, unreproducible F1~=~0.897 in favour of these
honest numbers. In forecasting, we corrected a prior artifact---an error of
$\approx$0.0008 from test-leaking normalization---and report leakage-free
rolling-origin results instead, under which CD-KAN is competitive. On real financial data, CD-KAN recovers a directed graph with plausible,
lag-resolved structure whose stability exceeds a permutation-based chance level
by roughly six-fold ($p<0.0001$), and whose spline edge functions expose the
shape of each dependency.

CD-KAN's contribution is therefore best understood as a \emph{unified,
interpretable decision-support artifact} that delivers competitive structure
recovery \emph{together} with interpretable mechanisms and coupled forecasting for
moderate-dimensional, non-linear settings. Its main limitations---the per-edge
spline evaluation cost (and the $O(d^3)$ acyclicity term when contemporaneous
edges are modelled), and the causal-sufficiency and stationarity assumptions
required to read edges causally---define a clear agenda: (1) vectorized spline
evaluation and low-rank/log-det acyclicity for higher $d$; (2) $\ell_0$-style
gates as an alternative to the group-lasso; (3) intervention- and event-based
validation (e.g., policy-announcement windows) to test recovered structure beyond
stability; and (4) expert user studies to quantify, rather than assert,
interpretability. As organizations lean further on AI for strategy, artifacts that
are simultaneously predictive, transparent, and honest about their boundaries are
essential; CD-KAN is a step in that direction.
