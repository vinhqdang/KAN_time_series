
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented SPADE, an information-systems artifact that couples
Kolmogorov--Arnold edge functions with differentiable, lag-aware DAG learning to
produce, from one differentiable model, both a one-step forecast and an
interpretable causal graph. Methodologically, SPADE separates lagged from
contemporaneous adjacencies and enforces acyclicity only on the contemporaneous
block via an Augmented Lagrangian scheme---a formulation we specify in full,
including its hyperparameter schedule and convergence criteria.

Our evaluation is deliberately candid. The headline result is on \emph{non-linear
instantaneous} causal discovery, where SPADE's learnable spline edges recover
additive-noise DAGs at AUROC~$\approx$~0.92--0.95 across $d\in\{6,10,20\}$ once
its group-lasso weight is properly tuned on genuinely held-out validation
seeds (an over-regularized default in an earlier draft had understated this
to $\approx$0.88; a seed-leakage bug in our own tuning script, found on
independent review, had briefly and incorrectly inflated it to
$\approx$0.97---we correct that here rather than let it stand), decisively
beating every fixed-architecture, gradient-based non-linear DAG learner (DAGMA-nonlinear, GraN-DAG,
NOTEARS-MLP, all $\le$0.6) and matching or beating the linear ones, at a
fraction of their runtime---the regime
where the KAN edge functions confer a real, distinctive advantage \emph{relative
to that family}. We are equally candid about the full arc of this comparison:
at the earlier, over-regularized setting the score-matching method NoGAM had
clearly outperformed SPADE at $d{=}6,10$; at the leakage-corrected setting
SPADE beats SCORE at every width and beats NoGAM at two of the three widths
tested ($d{=}6,20$), but trails NoGAM at $d{=}10$ (0.926 vs.\ 0.955)---an
honest, mixed result rather than the clean sweep an intermediate,
leakage-affected version of this analysis had briefly suggested. SPADE still
trains some $275$--$930\times$ faster than NoGAM and additionally
yields inspectable edge functions neither score-matching method provides,
including at $d{=}10$ where NoGAM leads on accuracy alone.
On lagged structure SPADE is competitive (perfect
on linear; on non-linear it beats KAN-Granger baselines and NOTEARS but trails
conditioning-based PCMCI/VAR-LiNGAM). The key design lesson is that
identifiability hinges on an information bottleneck: predicting each variable only
through its incoming edges, not a dense backbone (which collapses to chance). We
retract the earlier, unreproducible F1~=~0.897 in favour of these honest numbers,
and likewise corrected a forecasting artifact ($\approx$0.0008 from test-leaking
normalization), reporting leakage-free results under which SPADE is competitive. On real financial data---an observational setting where causal sufficiency cannot be
verified---SPADE recovers a directed graph of \emph{candidate} mechanisms, not
verified causal effects, whose stability exceeds a permutation-based chance
level by $4.7\times$ ($p<0.0001$) and whose spline edge functions expose the
shape of each candidate dependency for expert review.

Beyond lagged structure, the model handles \emph{instantaneous} causal structure
as a self-masked non-linear SEM whose contemporaneous graph is kept acyclic by the
ALM; on data with a known instantaneous DAG it recovers the graph and outperforms
linear NOTEARS on non-linear mechanisms. We also validate the recovered structure
beyond self-consistency---via a permutation test, a time-reversal placebo, and a
ground-truth structural-change experiment in which SPADE both detects a known
regime break and recovers the two distinct graphs.

SPADE's contribution is therefore best understood as a \emph{unified,
interpretable decision-support artifact} that delivers competitive structure
recovery (lagged and instantaneous) \emph{together} with interpretable mechanisms,
coupled forecasting, and scaling to $d{=}50$. We report two candid negative
results rather than omit them: documented real-world policy shocks are not
cleanly recoverable from observational macro data (Section~\ref{sec:real}),
and redoing the real-data causal analysis on a stationary (returns) rather
than a levels representation collapses the learned graph to an
uninformative, fully-connected structure, because daily returns on these
assets carry little one-step-ahead predictive signal
(Section~\ref{sec:returns})---the levels-based graph should therefore be read
as evidence of shared non-stationary co-movement, not validated short-horizon
causal structure. Its main limitations---the per-edge
spline evaluation cost (and the $O(d^3)$ acyclicity term for instantaneous
graphs), and the causal-sufficiency and stationarity assumptions required to read
edges causally---define a clear agenda: (1) low-rank/log-det acyclicity for higher
$d$; (2) $\ell_0$-style gates as an alternative to the group-lasso; and
(3) expert user studies to quantify, rather than assert, interpretability. As
organizations lean further on AI for strategy, artifacts that are simultaneously
predictive, transparent, and honest about their boundaries are essential; SPADE
is a step in that direction.
