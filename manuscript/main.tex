\title{{SPADE}: spline additive-noise {DAG} estimation for interpretable
nonlinear causal discovery in managerial decision support}

\begin{abstract}
Understanding causal relationships in organizational time-series data is critical
for managerial decision-making, yet forecasting systems often trade
interpretability for accuracy. We introduce \textbf{SPADE} (SPline Additive-noise
DAG Estimation), an information-systems artifact built on Kolmogorov--Arnold
networks that, from a single differentiable model, jointly discovers interpretable
non-linear causal structure and produces one-step forecasts. SPADE predicts each variable \emph{only} through per-(cause, lag) B-spline edge
functions of its candidate parents---an information bottleneck that makes the
learned structure identifiable---with an edge-level group-lasso for sparsity and
an optional acyclicity constraint on contemporaneous edges, and we ground the
design in the identifiability theory of nonlinear additive-noise models. We
evaluate it transparently against recent differentiable DAG learners (DAGMA,
GraN-DAG, GOLEM, nonlinear NOTEARS) and temporal causal-discovery methods (PCMCI,
VAR-LiNGAM), using threshold-free AUROC/AUPRC with paired significance tests and
guarding against known benchmark artifacts. Our headline result is on
\emph{non-linear} causal discovery: on instantaneous additive-noise DAGs, SPADE
attains AUROC~$\approx$~0.88 across widths $d\in\{6,10,20\}$, \textbf{decisively
outperforming the strongest modern DAG learners}---DAGMA-nonlinear, GraN-DAG, and
NOTEARS-MLP---because its learnable spline edges capture non-linear mechanisms
that fixed-architecture learners miss. We show this identifiability hinges on an information bottleneck: a
variant with a dense forecasting backbone collapses to chance. SPADE also handles
lagged (temporal) structure competitively---perfect on linear graphs and, on
non-linear graphs, beating KAN-Granger baselines and NOTEARS while trailing the
best conditioning-based methods (PCMCI, VAR-LiNGAM)---and scales to $d{=}50$ in
seconds. Under a leakage-free protocol it forecasts an 8-asset financial panel
competitively (MSE $\approx\forecastmse$; we retract an earlier $0.0008$ traced to
a test-leaking normalization). We validate recovered structure beyond
self-consistency (permutation test 4.7$\times$ chance, $p<0.0001$; a time-reversal
placebo; a ground-truth structural-change experiment), and report candidly that
documented policy shocks are \emph{not} cleanly recoverable from observational
macro data. Its spline edge functions expose linear, saturating, and
threshold-like dependencies---supporting a ``glass-box'' decision-support tool for
moderate-dimensional, non-linear business systems.
\end{abstract}
