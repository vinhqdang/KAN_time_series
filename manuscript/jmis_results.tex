
\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate CD-KAN on (i) synthetic data with known ground-truth graphs, (ii) a
leakage-free forecasting protocol, and (iii) real financial markets where no
ground truth exists. All code and configurations are released for reproduction;
every number below is produced by the accompanying scripts rather than reported
from a single tuned run.

\subsection{Datasets, baselines, metrics, and protocol}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Synthetic data.} We generate \emph{linear} data from a sparse
lower-triangular VAR process and \emph{non-linear} data from a structural causal
model whose mechanisms are drawn from $\{$linear, $\sin$, $\tanh$, signed-square$\}$
with additive noise and random lags in $\{1,2,3\}$. We evaluate across a wide
range of graph widths $d\in\{5,10,20,50\}$ (lengths $N$ from 1{,}500 to 3{,}000),
with three random seeds per configuration (each seed re-draws both graph and
data). This directly answers the concern that a $d{=}5$-only study is
uninformative. All series are standardized before fitting; scaling of accuracy
and cost with $d$ is reported in Section~\ref{sec:scalability}.

\paragraph{Baselines.} We compare against seven \emph{genuine} implementations:
VAR-Lasso \citep{Tibshirani1996}; linear NOTEARS \citep{Zheng2018}; GOLEM
\citep{Ng2024CALM}; PCMCI (tigramite, \citealt{Runge2019}); VAR-LiNGAM (lingam,
\citealt{Hyvarinen2010}); a lagged-correlation threshold; and GC-KAN
\citep{GCKAN2024} with genuine B-spline edge functions. We additionally include
\textbf{GC-KAN$+$ALM}, which adds our acyclicity penalty to the GC-KAN backbone,
to isolate the contribution of the DAG constraint from that of the KAN edge
functions (Reviewer-requested ablation). Forecasting comparisons add TSMixer
\citep{Chen2023}, PatchTST \citep{Nie2023}, N-BEATS \citep{Oreshkin2020}, and an
LSTM.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Because thresholding confounds structural-ranking quality
with a chosen operating point, we report \textbf{AUROC} and \textbf{AUPRC}
(threshold-free) as primary metrics, and F1/SHD at each method's own best-F1
threshold---the identical selection procedure applied to every method, CD-KAN
included, so no method is advantaged. All causal metrics are computed only on
synthetic data with known ground truth; on real data we use stability-based
validation (Section~\ref{sec:real}). For CD-KAN we use the edge-probability
(summary-adjacency) readout; a coefficient-magnitude readout performed markedly
worse and is not used.

\paragraph{Forecasting protocol.} We use strict rolling-origin (walk-forward)
splits with normalization statistics computed on each training window only,
preventing leakage \citep{Kim2022}. All models are trained for the same number of
epochs (matched budget); we report one-step-ahead MSE/MAE averaged over the
rolling folds and seeds.

\subsection{Causal discovery on synthetic data}
\label{sec:synthetic}

Table~\ref{tab:causal} reports results split by regime (aggregated over
$d\in\{5,10,20\}$ for non-linear and $d\in\{5,10,20,50\}$ for linear). On
\textbf{linear} structure CD-KAN is very strong (AUROC~$=$~0.96, essentially
perfect up to $d{=}20$ and 0.80 at $d{=}50$), close to the mature estimators
(VAR-Lasso, NOTEARS, GOLEM, PCMCI, VAR-LiNGAM, all $\approx$1.0). On
\textbf{non-linear} structure CD-KAN attains AUROC~$=$~0.76 (F1~$=$~0.64),
above the KAN-based Granger baselines (0.70) and NOTEARS (0.72), comparable to
the correlation baseline (0.76); the strongest constraint- and
non-Gaussianity-based methods (PCMCI~0.94, VAR-LiNGAM~0.94, VAR-Lasso~0.93) lead.
On the cleaner low-$d$ settings CD-KAN reaches AUROC~$\approx$~0.90--0.97.

\paragraph{Statistical significance.} Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests
(Table~\ref{tab:causal_stats}, F1 over matched configuration/seed pairs) show
CD-KAN significantly \emph{outperforms} GC-KAN ($p{=}0.031$), GC-KAN$+$ALM
($p{=}0.031$), and NOTEARS ($p{=}0.006$), and is statistically indistinguishable
from the correlation baseline ($p{=}0.30$); PCMCI, VAR-LiNGAM, VAR-Lasso, and
GOLEM remain ahead ($p<0.05$). CD-KAN is thus a \emph{competitive}
causal-discovery method---the strongest of the KAN-based approaches---rather than
a uniform state-of-the-art; we make no such claim and explicitly retract the
earlier, unreproducible F1~=~0.897. Its distinctive value (interpretable
mechanisms, lag attribution, coupled forecasting, and scaling to $d{=}50$) is
developed in the sections that follow.

\paragraph{Why the component-wise design matters.} A revealing contrast is with a
variant that feeds the causal layer into a dense residual KAN backbone (the
``coupled'' design). Table~\ref{tab:ablation} shows this variant collapses to
chance (AUROC~$\approx$~0.5): the backbone minimizes forecasting error regardless
of the adjacency, so structure is not identified. Enforcing the information
bottleneck---each target predicted \emph{only} through its incoming edges---is
what makes CD-KAN's structure faithful, and the edge-level group-lasso is what
makes it sparse. GC-KAN, which shares the B-spline edges but lacks per-lag
resolution, sits below CD-KAN, and adding our acyclicity term to it
(GC-KAN$+$ALM) changes little on these purely-lagged graphs---confirming that the
gains come from the identifiable architecture, not the DAG penalty alone.

\subsection{Instantaneous (contemporaneous) structure and the acyclicity constraint}
\label{sec:contemp}

The experiments above are purely lagged, so the acyclicity constraint is inactive
(temporal order already forbids cycles). To exercise it, we generate SVAR data
with a \emph{known instantaneous DAG} plus lagged edges, and fit the
contemporaneous CD-KAN variant (Section~\ref{sec:opt}), which predicts each
variable from its instantaneous parents (self-masked) and its lags, with the
NOTEARS penalty on the contemporaneous block $W_0$ dualized by ALM. This is a
non-linear, KAN-based analogue of DYNOTEARS.

Table~\ref{tab:contemp} reports recovery of both blocks. CD-KAN recovers the
instantaneous DAG well at $d{=}6$ (AUROC~0.89) and---crucially, since the edges
are non-linear---\emph{substantially outperforms a linear instantaneous-NOTEARS
baseline} (0.89 vs.\ 0.67), while simultaneously recovering the lagged block
(AUROC~0.92) and driving the acyclicity residual to $h(W_0)\!\approx\!2\times10^{-3}$
(i.e.\ an essentially acyclic contemporaneous graph). Recovery is harder at
$d{=}10$ (fewer samples per instantaneous relation) but still matches or exceeds
the linear baseline. This confirms the acyclicity machinery works as designed and
that KAN edges add value precisely where mechanisms are non-linear.

\subsection{Scalability and runtime}
\label{sec:scalability}

The component-wise design is inexpensive: CD-KAN fits in seconds at $d{=}5$
(vs.\ minutes for the discarded coupled variant), though its edge-wise spline
evaluation still makes it slower than the highly optimized classical baselines
(Figure~\ref{fig:comprehensive}, right; seconds vs.\ sub-second). Cost grows with
$d$ as the $O(d^2 L)$ edge count increases (Table~\ref{tab:scale}, reporting
parameter count and fit time at $d\in\{5,10\}$ from the main benchmark and at
$d\in\{15,20\}$ from a dedicated timing sweep). CD-KAN is thus well suited to
moderate-width systems (tens of variables); the thousands-of-series regime would
require vectorized spline evaluation and low-rank acyclicity
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\subsection{Forecasting}
\label{sec:forecast}

Table~\ref{tab:forecast} reports one-step-ahead forecasting on the 8-asset
financial dataset under the leakage-free rolling-origin protocol. CD-KAN attains
the lowest error (MSE~$=$~0.044). We are careful about interpretation: CD-KAN's
RevIN wrapper removes per-window level and scale, which is decisive on strongly
non-stationary financial \emph{levels}---the deep baselines here lack built-in
normalization and degrade badly (LSTM and PatchTST perform worse than a naive
predictor). The comparison should therefore be read as evidence for coupling the
interpretable causal edge-functions \emph{with} reversible normalization, not as a
claim that the CD-KAN structure module alone beats Transformers; a
normalization-matched forecasting study is future work.

\paragraph{Reconciling the reported error (addressing the abstract/table
inconsistency).} An earlier version reported MSE~$\approx$~0.0008 and a
``20$\times$'' improvement. We traced this to an \emph{evaluation artifact}:
computing normalization statistics on the full series (test values leaking into
the scaling) rather than on training data only. Table~\ref{tab:leak} isolates the
mechanism on a single split: global (leaky) normalization yields a markedly lower
MSE (0.016) than the correct train-only normalization (0.028)---confirming the
direction of the bias---and the original 0.0008 figure combined this leak with a
more favourable single split. We therefore \emph{retract} the 0.0008 / 20$\times$
claim; the correct, leakage-free numbers are those in Table~\ref{tab:forecast},
and all abstract and conclusion statements are updated accordingly.

\subsection{Ablation and sensitivity}
\label{sec:ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates CD-KAN's design choices on non-linear synthetic
data. The decisive factor is the \textbf{information bottleneck}: replacing the
component-wise readout with a dense residual KAN backbone (the ``coupled''
variant) collapses recovery to chance (AUROC~$0.58$), because the backbone can fit
the forecast irrespective of the adjacency. The edge-level group-lasso, by
contrast, is not essential for ranking quality on these small dense graphs
(AUROC is comparable with and without it); its role is to yield an explicitly
sparse graph, which matters more as $d$ grows and the true graph becomes sparser.

\subsection{Real-world causal insights: financial markets 2020--2025}
\label{sec:real}

We apply CD-KAN to daily closing prices of eight assets: gold (GLD), WTI crude
oil (WTI), the U.S. dollar index (DXY), the S\&P~500 (SPX), Bitcoin (BTC), silver
(SLV), copper (COPPER), and natural gas (NG), over 2020--2025 ($N{=}1{,}827$).
No ground-truth graph exists, so we treat the output as \emph{candidate}
mechanisms and validate via stability (Section~\ref{sec:stability}) rather than
F1/SHD.

\subsubsection{Learned functional forms (spline shapes)}
\label{sec:splines}
A central interpretability claim is that edge functions expose the \emph{shape}
of a dependency. Figure~\ref{fig:splines} plots the learned B-spline for the
strongest edges: several are near-linear, while others saturate or show
threshold behaviour---information a scalar coefficient cannot convey, and which
changes the managerial reading (e.g., a saturating driver implies diminishing
returns to further exposure).

\paragraph{Lag attribution.} On synthetic data with \emph{known} lags, CD-KAN's
contribution-weighted lag estimate is accurate---mean absolute error 0.43 trading
days, correctly separating lag-1 from lag-3 edges. On the financial \emph{price
levels}, by contrast, the estimated lags concentrate near the middle of the
window ($\approx$3 days), reflecting the near-integrated (random-walk-like)
dynamics of prices where all recent lags are similarly predictive---consistent
with the non-stationarity concern; lag attribution is therefore most informative
on stationary or differenced series.

\subsubsection{Rolling-window stability and its significance}
\label{sec:stability}
We estimate the graph on three rolling windows and measure the mean off-diagonal
Jaccard overlap of the binarized graphs (Figure~\ref{fig:stability}), obtaining
\textbf{0.31}. To test whether this exceeds chance---the analysis Reviewers
requested---we compare against a permutation null of random graphs matched to each
fold's edge count. The observed stability is \textbf{4.7$\times$} the null mean
(0.066) with permutation $p<0.0001$: the learned structure is far more consistent
across time than random graphs of the same density, i.e.\ it reflects persistent
dependencies rather than noise.

\subsubsection{Validation beyond stability: time-reversal and regime shifts}
\label{sec:validation}
Stability shows consistency, not correctness. We add two stronger checks
requested by Reviewers.

\paragraph{Time-reversal placebo.} A genuine temporal causal method should recover
the true (forward) graph far better from forward data than from time-reversed
data; invariance to reversal would indicate spurious correlation. On non-linear
synthetic data ($d{=}10$, 3 seeds), CD-KAN's recovery AUROC drops from
\textbf{0.94} (forward) to \textbf{0.64} (reversed)---a 0.30 gap---confirming that
it exploits temporal precedence rather than symmetric association.

\paragraph{Ground-truth structural change (intervention).} The most direct
version of the Reviewer's request---``does CD-KAN recover the \emph{expected
change} in causal structure?''---requires a known change. We concatenate two SVAR
regimes with \emph{different} graphs at a known break ($t{=}1500$) and slide
CD-KAN across the series (Figure~\ref{fig:intervention}A). The graph-dissimilarity
signal between adjacent windows peaks around the break, and CD-KAN recovers the
correct regime graph on each side (AUROC~0.89 before, 1.00 after). Thus CD-KAN
detects the intervention \emph{and} recovers the two distinct structures---not
merely a stable graph.

\paragraph{Event-anchored regime analysis (real data, illustrative).} Applying the
same sliding-window analysis to the financial panel
(Figure~\ref{fig:intervention}B) surfaces structural shifts during the 2020 COVID
and 2021--2022 rate-hike eras; splitting at the 2022 rate-hike onset, the graph
changes in an economically coherent direction (post-2022, gold becomes a
downstream hub as flight-to-safety links appear). We stress this is
\emph{illustrative}: the rigorous, placebo-controlled version below shows real-data
single-event attribution is only marginal, so the clean intervention evidence is
the synthetic experiment.

\paragraph{Documented policy shock (real data), reported candidly.} We also test
whether documented policy shocks coincide with larger structural change than
placebo dates. Two findings. First, a methodological one that echoes a Reviewer
concern: on daily \emph{price levels} the learned structure is degenerate (e.g.\
the US-dollar index carries $<$1\% of outgoing influence, dominated by co-trending
non-stationarity), whereas on \emph{log-returns} it is balanced and economically
plausible---so this analysis uses returns. Second, a placebo-controlled test
(pre/post-split graph dissimilarity, log-returns, $W{=}300$ each side): the March
2022 Fed rate-hike liftoff coincides with the \emph{largest} pre/post structural
shift in the sample (dissimilarity 0.96 vs.\ a placebo mean of 0.86), though this
is only marginally above chance ($p\!\approx\!0.10$), and the 2020 COVID shock is
not individually distinguishable ($p\!=\!0.76$). Daily-return graphs vary
substantially window-to-window, which limits attribution of any single real-world
event; accordingly we regard the \emph{controlled synthetic} experiment
(Figure~\ref{fig:intervention}A), where ground truth is known, as the definitive
demonstration that CD-KAN recovers structural change, and the real-data result as
suggestive rather than conclusive.
