# Scoping review: scalable, interpretable, non-linear additive-noise causal discovery

Status: draft 1 (2026-11-01). Purpose: decide a genuinely new research problem and algorithm that builds on SPADE (published; not to be modified).

## 1. Scope and method (what this review is, and is not)

- **Form:** scoping review (mapping concepts, evidence and gaps). It is *not* a PRISMA systematic review; no protocol was registered, and the PRISMA-ScR checklist has not been completed.
- **Question (PCC):** How do non-linear additive-noise causal-discovery methods currently handle (1) scale to hundreds or thousands of variables, (2) shrinkage/selection bias of sparsity penalties, and (3) readability and validation of learned edge functions, and what evaluation practice supports those claims?
- **Included:** papers or preprints on differentiable DAG learning, order-based and score-matching discovery, additive/spline/GAM/KAN structure learners, sparsity and selection, benchmarks and real data with ground truth, interpretability evaluation, and temporal extensions.
- **Search and verification:** four AI search workers (themes A-D) searched the web and confirmed each entry against an arXiv, publisher or proceedings page. Entries a worker could not confirm were placed in a separate UNVERIFIED list and are excluded from the evidence below.
- **Limits (read before relying on this):** single-pass screening by AI, no second human screener; most annotations rest on abstracts, not full texts; many 2025-2026 items are preprints; some author lists and page ranges were not confirmed (flagged in section 6). **Every reference must be re-checked against its DOI/arXiv page before it enters a manuscript.** One cross-check already caught an error: a worker labelled arXiv:2304.03382 as NoGAM, but that paper is DAS; NoGAM is arXiv:2304.03265.

## 2. Evidence map

### 2.1 Identifiability and additive-noise foundations
Nonlinear additive-noise models are identifiable (Hoyer et al., NeurIPS 2008 for the bivariate case; Peters et al., JMLR 2014 for the multivariate DAG; Zhang & Hyvarinen, UAI 2009 for post-nonlinear). Linear Gaussian models are identifiable with equal error variances (Peters & Buhlmann, Biometrika 2014). The regression-plus-independence-test algorithm RESIT scales only to small d.

### 2.2 Differentiable (continuous-optimization) DAG learning
- NOTEARS (Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2018) gave the smooth acyclicity function tr(exp(W∘W))-d at O(d^3) cost; NOTEARS-MLP/Sobolev (Zheng et al., AISTATS 2020) made it non-parametric; DAG-GNN (Yu et al., ICML 2019), GraN-DAG (Lachapelle et al., ICLR 2020), DCDI (Brouillard et al., NeurIPS 2020), GOLEM (Ng et al., NeurIPS 2020; linear, soft constraints), DAGMA (Bello et al., NeurIPS 2022; log-det acyclicity) follow.
- Cost and conditioning: DAGMA, truncated matrix power iteration (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2022), no-curl parameterization (Yu et al., ICML 2021), low-rank DAGs (Fang et al., IEEE TNNLS), factor graphs DCD-FG (Lopez et al., NeurIPS 2022; needs interventions), SDCD (Nazaret et al., ICML 2024; "thousands of variables" per its abstract).
- Optimization and honesty about these methods: "DAGs with No Fears" (Wei et al., NeurIPS 2020), topological swaps (Deng et al., ICML 2023), a "sober look" (Ng et al., CLeaR 2024: threshold sensitivity, alternative penalties).
- Interpretable edge strength: Dagma-DCE replaces weight norms with a derivative-based "discrete causal effect" (Waxman et al., IEEE OJSP 2024).

### 2.3 Order-based and score-matching methods
CAM (Buhlmann et al., Ann. Stat. 2014): additive models, greedy order search with neighbourhood selection, then sparse penalized regression. SCORE (Rolland et al., ICML 2022; experiments up to d=50, about 95% of runtime in CAM pruning at d=50, theory for Gaussian noise), NoGAM (Montagna et al., CLeaR 2023, arXiv:2304.03265; arbitrary noise), DAS (Montagna et al., CLeaR 2023, arXiv:2304.03382; synthetic d up to 1,000, but recall about 0.08-0.09 at d=1,000 and SID dropped for d>200 per the worker's reading of the paper), DiffAN (Sanchez et al., ICLR 2023; up to 500 variables per its abstract), SciNO (Kang et al., NeurIPS 2025), CaPS (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2024), hybrid top-down search (Hiremath et al., NeurIPS 2024), OSLow (Kamkari et al., preprint), CORL (Wang et al., IJCAI 2021), and sample-complexity theory (Zhu et al., NeurIPS 2023). A 2026 preprint (Wu & Xie, arXiv:2604.25295) reports d=1,000 on non-linear graphs; unreviewed.

### 2.4 Amortized and foundation-model approaches, and divide-and-conquer
AVICI (Lorch et al., NeurIPS 2022; benchmarks to d=100), DECI (Geffner et al., TMLR 2024), SEA (Wu et al., TMLR 2025; at most 1,000 variables, under-predicts edges on real data). Partitioning: SADA (Cai et al., ICML 2013), causal graph partitioning (Shah et al., TMLR 2025), DCILP (Dong et al., AAAI 2025); these are built around conditional-independence tests, Markov blankets or Gaussian base learners. Screening precedents: GENIE3 tree importance (Huynh-Thu et al., PLoS ONE 2010), kernel Markov-blanket ranking (Strobl & Visweswaran, preprint 2014).

### 2.5 Spline / GAM / NAM / KAN structure and interpretability
Sparse additive models (Ravikumar et al., JRSSB 2009; venue not confirmed by the worker), GA2M/EBM (Lou et al., KDD 2013; Nori et al., 2019), NAM (Agarwal et al., NeurIPS 2021), NODE-GAM (Chang et al., ICLR 2022), P-splines (Eilers & Marx, Stat. Sci. 1996), monotone calibrated look-up tables (Gupta et al., JMLR 2016). Concurvity and non-identifiability threaten additive-model interpretation (Siems et al., NeurIPS 2023; Zhang et al., preprint 2025). KAN (Liu et al., ICLR 2025) and critiques: fairer comparison (Yu et al., 2024: KAN wins only on symbolic tasks when parameters are matched), critical assessment (Hou et al., 2024), unstable per-edge symbolic fitting (Sovrano et al., 2026). KAN-based causal work found: Granger causality (Lin et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025 industrial), known-structure generative models KaCGM (Almodovar et al., 2026), fuzzy cognitive maps (Salmeron, 2026). **No paper other than SPADE was found that combines spline/KAN edges with an acyclicity-constrained or order-based search for cross-sectional DAG discovery.**

### 2.6 Sparsity, shrinkage bias and selection
Adaptive lasso (Zou, JASA 2006), stability selection (Meinshausen & Buhlmann, JRSSB 2010), bootstrap edge confidence in Bayesian networks (Friedman et al., UAI 1999), L0 gates (Louizos et al., ICLR 2018). In structure learning: sparsity and DAG constraints (Ng et al., NeurIPS 2020), adaptive-lasso NOTEARS (Xu et al., preprint 2022; argues l1 shrinkage plus a fixed threshold deletes small true edges), Jacobian regularizer for neural Granger causality (Zhou et al., ICML 2024), group-lasso Granger (Tank et al., IEEE TPAMI 2022). Tuning without labels: out-of-sample tuning (Biza et al., PGM 2020), hyperparameter robustness (Machlanski et al., CLeaR 2024), self-compatibility (Faller et al., AISTATS 2024).

### 2.7 Benchmarks, evaluation pitfalls, real data
Varsortability (Reisach et al., NeurIPS 2021) and R2-sortability (Reisach et al., NeurIPS 2023): shortcuts that survive standardization in the second case; internally standardized SCMs (Ormaniec et al., ICLR 2025) avoid both. NOTEARS is scale-sensitive (Kaiser & Sipos, Neural Process. Lett. 2022; Seng et al., preprint 2022); large-DAG losses can be minimized by wrong DAGs (Seng et al., ICLR 2024). Large studies: assumption violations (Montagna et al., NeurIPS 2023), BN structure learning with noisy data (Constantinou et al., IJAR 2021), survey (Kitson et al., AIR 2023), landscape of real data (Brouillard et al., CLeaR 2025), interventional evaluation (Gentzel et al., NeurIPS 2019). Real data: Sachs et al. (Science 2005; 11 nodes, ground truth contested), Tuebingen pairs (Mooij et al., JMLR 2016), NetSim (Smith et al., NeuroImage 2011), DREAM5 (Marbach et al., Nat. Methods 2012), GeneNetWeaver (Schaffter et al., 2011), CausalBench (Chevalley et al.; interventional metrics), Causal Chambers (Gamella et al.; low-dimensional, true ground truth), time series: CausalTime (Cheng et al., ICLR 2024), CausalRivers (Stein et al., ICLR 2025; over 1,000 nodes). Metrics: SID (Peters & Buhlmann, Neural Comput. 2015).

### 2.8 Temporal extensions
VAR-LiNGAM (Hyvarinen et al., JMLR 2010), PCMCI (Runge et al., Sci. Adv. 2019), PCMCI+ (Runge, UAI 2020), DYNOTEARS (Pamfil et al., AISTATS 2020), neural Granger (Tank et al.), NAVAR (Bussmann et al., DS 2021; closest additive temporal method), NTS-NOTEARS (Sun et al., AISTATS 2023), Rhino (Gong et al., ICLR 2023), CUTS/CUTS+ (Cheng et al., ICLR 2023; AAAI 2024), self-explaining Granger (Marcinkevicius & Vogt, ICLR 2021), non-stationarity (Huang et al., JMLR 2020; spurious regression, Granger & Newbold 1974). Function-valued causal influence from NAVAR (Kuskova et al., 2026 preprint) is the nearest published attempt at shape-level interpretation in this area.

## 3. Cross-cutting findings
1. Almost every large-d method is linear (GOLEM, DAGMA), a black-box neural model (SDCD, DiffAN, DCD-FG), or amortized (AVICI, SEA). Additive, inspectable methods (CAM, basis-function scores, SPADE) are shown at much smaller d.
2. Score-matching orderings are fast but their additive pruning stage dominates runtime and recall falls at large d.
3. Interpretability is asserted from plots; edge strengths are weight norms except Dagma-DCE; none of the reviewed causal papers validates recovered shape functions against ground truth.
4. Evaluation practice is fragile: sortability shortcuts, scale sensitivity, contested real benchmarks, and a thin literature on tuning without labels.

## 4. Gap analysis
- **G1 Shrinkage and selection bias for non-parametric additive DAG learners.** Discussed for linear NOTEARS, Granger and CAM-style pruning, but no dedicated study of how l1/group-lasso amplitude bias distorts non-parametric edge *curves*, or of adaptive, debiasing, smoothness (P-spline) penalties versus sparsity penalties in causal edges; stability selection has not been applied to differentiable DAG learners.
- **G2 No ground-truth evaluation of learned edge functions.** No metric for curve recovery or shape correctness inside causal discovery; KAN symbolic fitting is reported unstable; concurvity/identifiability is raised for prediction models, not for additive causal discovery.
- **G3 Interpretable (additive) discovery at scale.** Large-d evidence is linear, neural or amortized; the additive route is bottlenecked by pruning; partitioning methods do not preserve ANM identifiability; no large static observational real benchmark with ground truth.
- **G4 Benchmark validity for smooth, bounded non-linear additive generators at large d.** Sortability results are mostly for standard generators; no seed/leakage/reproducibility audit of neural or spline DAG learners; metric behavior of AUROC/AUPRC under extreme sparsity is not analysed.
- **G5 Time series:** additive-noise temporal methods meet non-stationary price-level data mainly by assumption; KAN-Granger work is preprint-stage and small-scale.

## 5. Candidate research questions (for discussion, none chosen)
- **RQ-A (G1, G2): Debiased and stable edge selection for spline additive-noise DAGs.** Does adaptive/refit selection with stability selection recover edges and curve shapes more accurately than group-lasso alone, and when do smoothness penalties help or hurt? Needs theory (selection and amplitude behaviour of the group-adaptive estimator for a known order) and sortability-controlled evaluation.
- **RQ-B (G2, G4): A ground-truth benchmark and metric for shape-faithful causal discovery.** How well do competing interpretable learners (CAM, NAM-based, DAGMA-DCE, KAN/SPADE) recover true edge functions, and how does that depend on sample size, noise, concurvity and sortability? Measurement-first contribution; method-agnostic.
- **RQ-C (G3): Interpretable scalable additive discovery.** Can a score-matching order combined with a spline-additive, penalized parent-selection stage replace CAM-style pruning to give interpretable discovery at d of several hundred, and what recall does it keep as d grows? Needs a partitioning or screening argument that does not break ANM identifiability.
