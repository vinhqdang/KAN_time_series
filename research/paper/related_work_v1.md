# Related work (v1, proposed rewrite)

Only references verified in research/lit_verify/REPORT.md are cited. Markers: [CHECK] = citation detail still uncertain; [UNREAD] = only the abstract was seen.

**Identifiability and continuous DAG learning.** Nonlinear additive-noise models make the DAG identifiable (Hoyer et al., 2008; Peters et al., 2014). NOTEARS (Zheng et al., 2018) turned structure search into continuous optimisation with a smooth acyclicity penalty, and NOTEARS-MLP (Zheng et al., 2020), GraN-DAG (Lachapelle et al., 2020), GOLEM (Ng et al., 2020) and DAGMA (Bello et al., 2022) extended it to nonparametric models and better-conditioned constraints; truncated matrix power iteration (Zhang et al., 2022) reduces its cost. Order-based methods split the problem: CAM (Buhlmann et al., 2014) searches orders under additive models and then prunes with sparse regression, SCORE (Rolland et al., 2022), NoGAM and DAS (Montagna et al., 2023) obtain the order from score matching, and DiffAN (Sanchez et al., 2023) does so with diffusion models. These works report structure metrics (SHD, SID, AUROC). Evaluation caveats are well documented: varsortability and R2-sortability (Reisach et al., 2021, 2023), scale sensitivity of NOTEARS (Kaiser and Sipos [CHECK volume and year]; Seng et al., 2024), and internally standardised generators (Ormaniec et al., 2025), which we use.

**Additive and spline models, and sparsity bias.** Sparse additive models (Ravikumar et al., 2009) and adaptive group lasso for spline additive models (Huang et al., 2010) select components by group penalties; the latter shows that adaptive weights restore selection consistency by reducing the bias on strong groups. Refitting after selection is classical: OLS post-lasso has smaller bias than the lasso (Belloni and Chernozhukov, 2013). In DAG learning, adaptive lasso has been proposed to avoid hard thresholding that deletes small true edges (Xu et al., 2022, preprint) and thresholding is analysed by Ng et al. [CHECK: 2024 venue]. Neural Granger causality uses first-layer group lasso for selection (Tank et al., 2022). A concurrent ICML 2026 paper combines an independence criterion with an adaptive group lasso on first-layer MLP weights to prune constant edges under non-Gaussian, heteroscedastic noise (Yao et al., 2026 [UNREAD beyond abstract]); its stated focus is identifiability and structure, not recovery of edge amplitudes. We found no study that measures how group-lasso shrinkage distorts the amplitude of recovered edge functions in additive DAG learners; we therefore treat the plain refit and adaptive penalties as baselines rather than contributions.

**Interpretable edge functions.** Neural additive models (Agarwal et al., 2021) and KANs (Liu et al., 2025; critiques by Yu et al., 2024 and Hou et al., 2024) promise readable univariate components. Dagma-DCE (Waxman et al., 2024) replaces weight norms by a derivative-based causal-effect strength. In time series, NAVAR (Bussmann et al., 2021) is additive, and Kuskova et al. (2026) estimate function-valued causal influence from trained NAVAR models and show that equal scalar scores can hide different shapes; they do not report an accuracy metric against known functions in the part we read [UNREAD beyond abstract]. Concurvity can make additive components non-identifiable (Siems et al., 2023). To our knowledge no benchmark scores recovered edge functions against ground truth in cross-sectional nonlinear causal discovery. Interventional evaluation of causal models has been advocated (Gentzel et al., 2019), and recent Bayesian work reports errors of average causal effects (Toth et al., 2025); our do-effect error follows this line and is not claimed as a new idea.

**Bayesian structure learning.** Order MCMC (Friedman and Koller, 2003) samples node orders and sums over parent sets; its prior bias towards graphs compatible with many orders is analysed by Viinikka et al. (2020). Nonlinear Bayesian structure learners include Gaussian process networks with marginal-likelihood scores (Friedman and Nachman, 2000), spline-based Bayesian DAGs with edge and functional selection (Ni et al., 2015), DiBS (Lorch et al., 2021), BayesDAG (Annadani et al., 2023) and structural marginalisation with autoregressive orders for Gaussian-process additive models (Toth et al., 2025). Sharma and van Beek (2022) use spline regression scores in score-and-search learning. Wang et al. (2025/2026) build confidence sets for causal orderings under additive errors. Our estimator, BASIL-W, combines a g-prior closed-form marginal likelihood on spline features with order MCMC warm-started from a continuous learner. Its ingredients are established and we do not claim the inference idea as new; what we study is how it repairs the amplitude shrinkage of a sparse spline learner, compared with a plain refit.
