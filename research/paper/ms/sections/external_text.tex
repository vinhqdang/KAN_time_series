This section compares our estimators with published nonlinear DAG learners, using the registered plan \texttt{research/external/PLAN.md} (commit c245cc1, before any run on the evaluation seeds). It was added after the other experiments and changes the reading of the earlier ones. Setting: iSCM, mechanism family 1, $n=1000$, three cells of 10 datasets each (seeds 4000--4009): $d=10$ uniform (E1), $d=10$ Laplace (E3), $d=20$ uniform (E2); 13 arms $\times$ 30 datasets $=390$ runs, no errors. \textbf{Only $d\le20$ was tested}; the large-$d$ regime for which SPADE-scaled and BASIL-W were designed was not, and CAM-like was not run at $d\ge50$.

\paragraph{Arms.} Ours: SSC, SSA, refit (SSR, $k=1.5$), BASIL-W (the pre-registered best arm by pooled TOPK NRMSE) and GAM-ridge as a reference. External: NOTEARS-MLP (repository version) and a port of the original (NOTEARS-MLP-gc), DAGMA-nonlinear (repository call, 4000/8000 iterations rather than the library defaults of 50000/80000), DCE-like (derivative-based post-hoc score on the DAGMA-MLP fit, so its curves are identical to DAGMA-nonlinear's), GraN-DAG (gcastle defaults, without CAM pruning), \textbf{CAM-like} and two NAM-parameterised SPADE variants. CAM-like is \textbf{our own Python reimplementation of the CAM recipe} (greedy forward edge addition with a BIC-penalised log-RSS gain, OLS B-spline terms, F-test pruning at $p<0.001$), not the original R code, which could not be run; the NAM variants are not the original NAM and were untuned. Dagma-DCE proper could not be run. Edge-function NRMSE is scored under the TOPK rule (every method receives the oracle edge count $k=|E|$, missed edges count 1), which is the pre-registered primary; the own-graph and no-selection rules give the same conclusions.

\paragraph{An external method beats ours in part of the grid.} CAM-like has a lower edge-function NRMSE than BASIL-W under Laplace noise at $d=10$: 0.151 against 0.281 (paired difference 0.130, 95\% interval [0.061, 0.218], Wilcoxon $p=0.002$, CAM-like better on 10/10 datasets). At $d=20$ uniform it is lower, 0.155 against 0.211, but not significantly (interval [$-0.005$, 0.110], $p=0.084$); against the best of our arms in that cell, SSA at 0.172, the gap is 0.017, a tie. At $d=10$ uniform it is slightly worse, 0.176 against 0.144 (difference 0.031, interval [$-0.014$, 0.083], $p=0.625$; CAM-like better on 6/10 datasets), which the registered rule labels inconclusive and which is practically a tie. Pooled over the 30 datasets CAM-like is at 0.161 and BASIL-W at 0.212 (difference 0.052, interval [0.010, 0.096], $p=0.013$), but the 30 datasets are not independent because E1 and E3 share DAGs, so the pooled test overstates the evidence. The registered prediction that CAM-like would match or beat our best arm in at least two of three cells was met. CAM-like also ties on structure (pooled AUROC difference $-0.012$ [$-0.041$, 0.012]; BASIL-W is better at $d=10$ uniform by 0.035, CAM-like at Laplace by 0.045 with an interval touching 0) and on do-effects (pooled direct 0.168 against 0.145 and indirect 0.281 against 0.236 for BASIL-W and CAM-like, both intervals including 0), and its amplitude is calibrated (median slope 0.99--1.00, BASIL-W 0.93--1.00). \emph{Our family therefore does not win a head-to-head against a well-specified additive spline learner at $d\le20$ on this benchmark.} The expected weakness of CAM-like under Laplace noise did not appear (orientation 0.97 against 0.92 for BASIL-W).

\paragraph{Neural learners are far behind, untuned.} The MLP-based learners (NOTEARS-MLP, NOTEARS-MLP-gc, DAGMA-nonlinear, DCE-like, GraN-DAG) have TOPK NRMSE 0.69--0.94 per cell (pooled 0.72--0.83), AUROC 0.54--0.71 against 0.93--0.99 for the spline-based arms, pooled orientation 0.45--0.59 (near chance), and median calibration slopes of 0.00--0.28 (a missed edge counts as slope 0). Differences against our best arm are $+0.51$ to $+0.61$ NRMSE pooled, $p=0.002$ (the smallest attainable with 10 pairs) in every cell. Their published evaluations are structural, and here they ran with default or repository settings and were not tuned on this generator, whereas the SPADE rule $\lambda=0.2/d$ and $k=1.5$ were chosen on earlier validation seeds from the same generator family. The gap is therefore an upper bound on their disadvantage and must not be quoted as ``published methods fail''. GraN-DAG returns very sparse graphs (4.4--4.7 edges on average against 10--20 true).

\paragraph{Within our family at small $d$.} BASIL-W is not better than the refit in the uniform cells ($-0.023$ at $d=10$, $+0.008$ at $d=20$, intervals include 0) and is better under Laplace noise ($-0.124$, $p=0.010$); the $d=100$ advantage over the refit is therefore not reproduced at these sizes except under heavy tails. SSA ties the refit (pooled $+0.004$). The NAM-parameterised refit is worse than the spline refit in the uniform cells (by 0.177 at $d=10$ and 0.217 at $d=20$, both $p\le0.01$) and better under Laplace noise ($-0.092$, $p=0.049$); the registered prediction of ``within 0.05'' was wrong, but the NAM variant is untuned (one hidden layer of 16 tanh units, 400 epochs), so this says only that this untuned variant does not beat splines.

\paragraph{Reading and limits.} The benchmark is additive with smooth one-dimensional mechanisms, which is exactly the model class of CAM-like, NAM and the spline family: the additive learners are matched to the truth and the MLP learners are not. The result is a warning that the edge-function accuracy of the SPADE family at $d\le20$ is not specific to it, and it is the reason the claims about BASIL-W were revised throughout this paper. CAM-like and the NAM and DCE variants are reimplementations and may differ from the originals in ways that matter; the repository NOTEARS-MLP differs from the original. Each cell has 10 datasets; best-arm choices (BASIL-W, CAM-like) were made on the evaluation data, symmetrically; p-values are uncorrected for multiplicity over 3 cells and 6 metrics, and only the TOPK NRMSE row is the registered primary. The cycle-breaking applied to cyclic own graphs was not logged. Nothing is claimed beyond $d\le20$, $n=1000$, mechanism family 1, iSCM, uniform and Laplace noise and expected degree 2. Not run: KAN-based Granger methods (time-series task), SCORE and NoGAM here (structure only; they appear in the earlier tables), and, because no semi-synthetic results existed when the plan was written, a Sachs-based arm.
