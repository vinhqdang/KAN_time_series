\paragraph{Construction.} The true structure is the Sachs consensus graph with one edge removed: the 18-edge graph contains the directed cycle PIP2 $\to$ PIP3 $\to$ plcg $\to$ PIP2, and the weakest cycle edge by endpoint correlation, PIP3 $\to$ plcg ($r=0.03$), was dropped by a rule fixed beforehand, leaving \textbf{17 true edges} on 11 nodes. For each node an additive cubic B-spline model with ridge penalty chosen by cross-validation was fitted to all 7466 log-transformed, z-scored rows; these 17 fitted functions are the ground truth. Noise is either a bootstrap of each node's fitted residuals (``bootstrap'', real, heavy-tailed and skewed) or Gaussian with the same standard deviation. The fitted model has median node $R^2$ 0.33, true-curve amplitudes from 0.05 to 0.79 standard deviations (9 of 17 edges with amplitude at least 0.3) and non-linear shares from 0.08 to 0.97. At $d=110$ there are ten disjoint copies (170 true edges, columns shuffled), bootstrap noise, $n=2000$, five datasets. In all, 85 of 85 datasets finished with none dropped (seeds 3500--3819 at $d=11$, 5000--5004 at $d=110$). At $d=11$, SSC and plain SPADE are bit-identical in all 80 datasets, because the screening is vacuous (K=15 exceeds $d-1$) and $\lambda=\min(0.01,0.2/d)=0.01$, so every $d=11$ statement about SPADE-scaled is a statement about plain SPADE; at $d=110$ plain SPADE collapses (NRMSE 1.00, AUROC 0.58) while SSC keeps structure (0.957).

\paragraph{Results.} BASIL-W lowers the NRMSE of SSC in all 80 $d=11$ datasets and all 5 $d=110$ datasets, but the size of the gain depends on $n$. The BASIL-W/SSC ratio is 0.47 [0.455, 0.490] (bootstrap noise) and 0.40 (Gaussian) at $n=2000$, 0.78 and 0.81 at $n=500$, and 0.43 at $d=110$. At $n=500$ the gain comes from strong edges (amplitude at least 0.3; ratios 0.57 and 0.44) while weak edges are not improved (0.94 and 1.03): BASIL-W drops them. SSC is heavily shrunk here (median slope 0.01--0.22, far below the iSCM simulations' 0.14--0.46). The standard fixes help less than in the simulations: SSA improves on SSC by only 0.09--0.22 NRMSE because re-weighting cannot recover edges SSC has already shrunk to zero (58--73\% of true edges at $n=2000$), and the refit (SSR$_{k=1.5}$ keeps only 16 edges where 17 are true, with AUROC down to 0.70) is only 0.08--0.20 below SSC. SSA minus BASIL-W is positive in four of five settings (+0.320, +0.211, +0.089, and +0.423 at $d=110$; the exception is $n=500$ Gaussian, $-0.022$ [$-0.044$, $+0.001$]). BASIL-W's band coverage of the true curve is 0.39 ($n=500$ bootstrap), 0.45, 0.52, 0.63 ($n=2000$ Gaussian) and 0.53 ($d=110$), against 0.74--0.81 in the simulations; coverage is below the plan's 0.60 line in three of five settings. Part of the reason is that 23--48\% of true edges are dropped under bootstrap noise, so their bands collapse, and part that the true functions are fitted splines with some wiggle.

\paragraph{Agreement with the simulation story is only partial.} By the rule stated in the plan (BASIL-W/SSC at most 0.7 with the interval's upper limit below 0.8 in at least three of four $d=11$ cells and at $d=110$), the main prediction P3 is \textbf{partial}: it holds in two of four $d=11$ cells and at $d=110$. The repair headline replicates in direction in all five settings (SSC worst, BASIL-W best among the SPADE-family arms), but in size only at $n=2000$. The parts specific to the structure cost, the refit comparison and calibration do not replicate cleanly. (i) BASIL-W has higher AUROC than SSC at $d=11$ (by 0.02 to 0.13, with much higher AUPRC and orientation), the opposite sign to the simulations; at $d=110$ the simulation pattern returns ($-0.037$ [$-0.051$, $-0.028]$, 5/5 datasets lower). (ii) The refit is not close to BASIL-W as it is at $d=100$: BASIL-W is better by 0.34 and 0.23 at $n=2000$, 0.107 at $n=500$ bootstrap, and tied at $n=500$ Gaussian ($-0.002$). (iii) Do-effect ratios BASIL-W/SSC are 0.58, 0.42, 0.81, 0.80, 0.45 (direct) and 0.85, 0.26, 1.01, 0.85, 0.70 (indirect); at $n=500$ bootstrap there is no indirect gain. (iv) SSC's AUROC at $d=11$ is only 0.82--0.86, so the simulation claim that structure is almost perfect does not hold here. (v) Calibration of the bands collapses (see above).

\paragraph{A plain GAM is a competitor at $d=11$.} GAM-ridge, a ridge-penalised additive regression on all parents without a DAG constraint, ties BASIL-W on edge NRMSE at $n=2000$ with bootstrap noise (0.394 against 0.391, paired $p=0.96$) and is better at $n=500$ (0.539 against 0.652, GAM lower in 20/20 datasets; 0.530 against 0.585 under Gaussian noise). BASIL-W is better at $n=2000$ with Gaussian noise (0.283 against 0.362). GAM-ridge has worse structure and orientation (AUPRC 0.53--0.59, orientation 0.56--0.77). It did not finish at $d=110$ (more than 60~min of CPU on one dataset, against 14~min for all other arms), so it is infeasible at the scale the pipeline targets; no GAM-ridge comparison is made on the $d=100$ iSCM data. Other baselines: NOTEARS-linear reaches AUROC 0.73--0.74 at $d=11$ and chance-level AUPRC at $d=110$; var-sortnregress 0.60--0.70.

\paragraph{The real-data pattern is not reproduced.} On the real Sachs data SSC has AUROC 0.613 and BASIL-W 0.563 (Section~\ref{sec:sachs}); here the same graph and fitted functions with iid data give SSC 0.82--0.86 and BASIL-W 0.84--0.99, BASIL-W above SSC. Whatever limits the real-data scores (pooled interventional conditions, the contested graph, hidden variables, non-additivity) is therefore not captured by estimator behaviour on this generator, and conversely this benchmark says nothing about the selection instability seen on real data, because subsample stability was not measured here. Real-data amplitude ratios (refit/SSC 1.60, BASIL-W/SSC 1.41) are much smaller than the shrinkage visible here, though they are computed on the top-ranked edges only.

\paragraph{Caveats.} The truth is a fitted model of contested real data: the consensus graph is contested, contains a cycle (one edge dropped by our rule) and omits known relations; results concern estimators on a generator with real-data-derived functions, not Sachs biology. Many fitted edges are weak or nearly linear (six of 17 below amplitude 0.25), and near-linear edges are weakly oriented under additive-noise identifiability; BASIL-W drops plcg $\to$ PIP2 (NRMSE 1.00) where GAM-ridge reaches 0.16, probably an orientation ambiguity (not checked). Ridge-CV picked very small penalties for several nodes, so some true functions are wiggly fits. The four $d=11$ cells share one SEM, so they are not replications of the construction; $d=110$ is ten identical copies with five datasets (minimum attainable exact Wilcoxon $p=0.0625$, only effect sizes and intervals are interpretable). The truth uses the same B-spline family as the spline learners, which favours them. Hyperparameters were not tuned for this generator. The cost column includes metric code and is not comparable with Section~\ref{sec:rep}. One post-registration code change (a fixed CSV column set) did not alter any metric; GAM-ridge was dropped at $d=110$ after exceeding the time limit.
