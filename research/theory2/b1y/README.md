## Q1 verdict (pre-specified rules; metric OA_x1 = order accuracy of rel-SortnRegress with the edges leaving slice 1 dropped; non-SC families pooled; unit = summary graph, 300 graphs; graph-cluster bootstrap CI)

- **V1 negative control: valid.** OA(G1rep) − OA(G1) = −0.004 [−0.011, +0.002], inside ±0.01. Sanity rules **S1-S4 all hold** (burn-in mixed: |G2s − G2| = 0.001; CF2 definable: 1.2% of cases at the lambda grid floor; CF2_m1 detectability equals G1's up to near-threshold pairs in 95.5% of cases; B1's gain reproduces on new seeds: +0.090 [+0.080, +0.101]).
- **Gain G1 − G2 (OA_x1) = +0.090 [+0.080, +0.101]** (full-window OA: +0.119, see `analysis_Q1.txt`).
- **V2 (population pattern at undetectable strength): "detectable strength, not population pattern".** CF2_m1 has G2's exact population relatives (hence G2's exact s_rho) and every new correlation is below half the detection threshold; it retains **R = 0.955 [0.884, 1.030]** of the G1 − G2 gain (rule: R ≥ 0.75 and CI lower bound ≥ 0.50). Retention of CF2_m3 and CF2_m10, where the G2 pattern becomes detectable: 0.919 [0.848, 0.989] and 0.788 [0.713, 0.862]. In the SC families (symER, ring) the same arms retain 0.988, 0.897 and 0.371.
- **V3 (strength dose at G1's exact pattern): "flat".** OA(CF1_4) − OA(CF1_0.25) = +0.0083 [+0.0007, +0.0158]; the CI excludes 0 but the difference is below 0.25 × the gain (0.0226), so the rule returns "flat". In the SC families it is −0.0001.
- **V4 (n-dose): supported.** Realised share Q(n) of the exact gap: 0.18 (n = 200), 0.40 (1,000), 0.57 (5,000), 0.76 (25,000); Q(25000) − Q(1000) = +0.360 [+0.294, +0.425], Q(25000) = 0.76 ≥ 0.60.
- **V5 (rho-dose): supported.** Q(cap 0.5) = 0.09, Q(cap 0.95) = 0.51; difference +0.418 [+0.349, +0.489].
- **Within-family slope of the gain on the exact gap** (family × p fixed effects removed): 0.569 [0.402, 0.731] (pooled B1 slope was 0.53); SC families 0.046 [−0.347, 0.444].
- SHD_x1n arms (T in {3, 5}, descriptive): G1 0.799, G2 1.477; retention (G2 − arm)/(G2 − G1): CF1_0.25 1.08, CF1_4 1.07, CF2_m1 1.00.
- Oracle calibration (consistency check of the simulation, not a mechanism claim): observed OA_full against the population threshold-oracle OA over 378 (family, p, T, arm) cells: R² 0.896, slope 0.77.

**Story-table selection (fixed in `PLAN.md` section 1): V2 = "detectable strength, not population pattern" and V4 supported gives U-A.** The exact relatives gap is the n → infinity ceiling of what the fixed start does to rel-SortnRegress; at finite n the realised effect is its detectable image, so its size depends on n and on correlation strength (V4, V5). The wording the plan allows for the paper: exact theory plus finite-n detectability with n- and correlation-dose curves.

**What this does and does not show.** V2 says the effect does not require the population relatives pattern to be G1's: a start that has G2's population relatives but G1's detectable pattern at n = 1,000 produces the same order-accuracy gain. Equivalently, the sorting statistic reacts to which pairs clear the detection threshold, not to the population relatives structure itself; the exact gap becomes the right description as n grows (V4). V3 is flat: changing the start variance at fixed pattern moves the gain little (about 0.008). The rho-dose (V5) shows that the transition matrix strength, hence correlation strength, controls how much of the exact gap is realised. None of this tests the learner reversals (Q2).

- **HY1 by the rule is "variance channel" for all four methods** (R_VM <= 0.40 and CI upper <= 0.60). R_VM is *negative* throughout: giving the G1 data G2's marginal variances does not just remove the CO methods' advantage, it makes them worse than under G2 (A(VM_G1) = -1.07 for DAGMA). So rescaling to G2's variances is more than a removal of the variance advantage; the rule's reading ("variance channel") holds, with that qualification. **Variance injection is not sufficient** (R_inj 0.43-0.49 for DAGMA and NOTEARS, below 0.60): putting G1's variances on G2 data restores less than 60% of the CO methods' G1 advantage.
