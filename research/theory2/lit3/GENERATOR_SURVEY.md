Terms. **Fixed start**: the first `max_lag` values are set to constants (zeros) or to noise only, and the returned data include the start. **Burn-in**: the start is simulated and then dropped. **Stationary start**: the first `max_lag` values are drawn from the stationary distribution. The paper's structural (ancestor-count) artefact goes away once at least Δ·τ_max slices are dropped. A burn-in this long does not remove the distributional transient, which decays geometrically with the spectral radius.

## Summary of the pattern

- Fixed start returned to the user with no burn-in: tigramite `var_process` (N(0,1) start), Reisach et al. 2026 (graph starts at t = 1), gCastle THP (empty history).
- Very short burn-in from zeros: CDML (2·max_lag).
- Real-data seed with short rollouts and no burn-in: CausalTime.
- Burn-in of 100 to 1000 steps or 20% of T: tigramite `structural_causal_process`, Neural-GC VAR and Lorenz-96, causalnex.
- Exact stationary start: UUMCdata (Herman et al.).
- Only the time-unrolled proposal of Reisach et al. gives every slice, including those after the fixed start, to an i.i.d. method as a separate variable without time labels. Lohse and Wahl give an i.i.d.-style regression the rows of a burnt-in series without labels (the summary-graph view). causalnex gives explicit lag labels. CausalTime, tigramite and Neural-GC data are used with time-series methods.

## Paragraph for the paper (who is affected)

We checked the source code of the generators in common use (tigramite, CDML, CausalTime, UUMCdata, causalnex, the Neural-GC VAR and Lorenz-96 simulators, gCastle); file and line references are in the supplement. Most of them start from zeros or i.i.d. noise. They then either discard a burn-in of 100 to 1000 steps or 20% of the series length (tigramite's `structural_causal_process`, causalnex, Neural-GC), or draw the initial lags from the stationary distribution (UUMCdata). Our Δ·τ_max condition is then met for the usual lengths, and the fixed-start effect does not apply to them. One exception is tigramite's burn-in of 0.2·T, which is only ⌊0.2T⌋ steps for short series (2 steps at T = 10). It does apply to designs that keep the first slices after a deterministic start and pass them to methods without time labels. The time-unrolled construction of Reisach et al. (2026) is the clearest case: it starts at t = 1 with no burn-in, and every slice v^(t) is a separate variable. The effect also applies, more weakly, to generators that discard little or nothing: tigramite's older `var_process` (N(0,1) start, no burn-in), the CDML generator of Lawrence et al. (2021) (zero start, 2·max_lag steps dropped), and the 20-step rollouts in CausalTime's demo script. CausalTime starts each rollout from a window of real data, not from a fixed state. We could not check how the CauseMe/C4C data were initialised; its documentation site was not reachable.
