\begin{abstract}
Causal-discovery benchmarks can often be solved by sorting variables by a
marginal statistic. Rel-sortability orders nodes by their number of relatives
and was shown to tend to the uninformative value $1/2$ on time-unrolled DAGs
whose summary graph is strongly connected in every weakly connected component.
We give the exact rel-sortability of every edge of the time-unrolled DAG, for
every summary graph with self-loops and every horizon, with an $O(1/T)$ rate,
and show that the condition is also necessary: every other summary graph, for
instance any river network, stays strictly above $1/2$. Much of this excess is
an artefact of the fixed start of the simulation. With a stationary start the
value is exact for every horizon and depends only on the source strongly
connected components. For Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi summary graphs we prove the
limiting values on paper. The graph results are machine-checked in Lean~4. A
finite-sample analysis says when a relatives count can be estimated from one
autocorrelated record and when a latent common driver saturates it. In
real-data studies with internally time-stamped analysis plans, a river
benchmark failed (a post hoc analysis is consistent with saturation) and a
physical testbed gave partial support. In a benchmark experiment with time
labels hidden, the fixed start raised the accuracy of a relatives-sorting
baseline by amounts that track the exact values, also under nonlinear
dynamics and signed weights; ranking reversals among baselines occurred in
the linear-Gaussian setting but did not replicate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Simulated benchmarks drive much of causal discovery, and several of their
regularities can be exploited without any causal reasoning. Marginal variances
tend to grow along the causal order (\emph{var-sortability};
\citealp{reisach2021beware}), so a method that sorts by variance does well on
data from common generators, and it explains the strong benchmark performance of some
continuous-optimisation methods, a family that includes NOTEARS
\citep{zheng2018notears}. The
coefficient of determination $R^2$ has the same property and is scale
invariant \citep{reisach2023scale}; internally standardised structural
models are not var-sortable by construction and, empirically, mostly not
$R^2$-sortable \citep{ormaniec2025standardizing}; and \citet{herman2025unitless} relate the
$R^2$ pattern to colliders and confounders and propose a generator that avoids
it. Most recently, \citet{reisach2026topological} defined
\emph{rel-sortability}, which orders a node by the number of its
\emph{relatives} (nodes joined to it by an open path, marginally dependent on
it under faithfulness). Relatives are a property of the graph alone, so the
criterion cannot be removed by reweighting. For random DAGs it is high; for
\emph{time-unrolled} DAGs built from a summary graph with self-loops,
\citet[Thm.~4]{reisach2026topological} show that it tends to $1/2$ if every
weakly connected component of the summary graph is strongly connected, and
they propose such graphs as benchmarks. Only sufficiency is shown in the arXiv
version; they do not analyse the value beyond this case.

Three questions remain open, and they matter for real time series, whose
summary graphs are often feed-forward (rivers, supply chains, signalling
cascades): (Q1) what is the rel-sortability of a time-unrolled DAG when the
summary graph is not strongly connected; (Q2) does it depend on how the series
is started and observed; and (Q3) can a relatives count be estimated from one
finite, autocorrelated record, so that a method could actually exploit it?

\textbf{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1)} The exact rel-sortability of every edge of the time-unrolled DAG
$\GT$, for every summary graph with self-loops and every $T$
(\cref{thm:edge}), its limit $\tfrac12+|\Ex|/(2|\Es|)$ with an explicit
$O(1/T)$ rate (\cref{thm:limit}), and necessity of the condition of
\citet[Thm.~4]{reisach2026topological} (\cref{cor:iff}). The excess above
$1/2$ for chains and out-trees is a boundary effect of the deterministic start
(\cref{prop:boundary}); with a stationary start the value is exact for every
$T$ and fixed by the \emph{source} strongly connected components
(\cref{thm:stat,cor:statiff}).
\textbf{(2)} Limits for directed Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi summary graphs, proved on
paper (\cref{prop:er}, \cref{app:erproof}).
\textbf{(3)} A finite-sample layer (\cref{sec:finite}): an effective sample
size and recovery bound for the relatives count, saturation by a latent
driver at $n\approx z_c^2/\ell^2$, and conditions for a proxy to repair it.
\textbf{(4)} A Lean~4 formalisation of the exact and stationary results
(\cref{sec:lean}), and real-data studies with time-stamped analysis plans
(\cref{sec:real}), reported with every failed test.
\textbf{(5)} A benchmark experiment (\cref{sec:b1}): with time labels hidden,
the fixed start shifts sort-based baselines by amounts the exact values
predict, robustly across parametrisations; and a discriminating held-out test
of the source-SCC rule against tie rules (\cref{app:b3}).

The graph results of (1) are short consequences of a walk-length lemma and of
\citet[Lemma~1]{reisach2026topological}. Their value is that they determine
the value for every summary graph, horizon and start, and change what a
benchmark designer should conclude: much of the rel-sortability of
time-unrolled benchmarks is an artefact of how the simulation is started. The
finite-sample results use textbook tools. We see the paper as a completion of
the unrolled-graph analysis of \citet[Section~5]{reisach2026topological} plus
a finite-sample and real-data layer, not as a new theory of sortability.

\textbf{Related work.} Varsortability~\citep{reisach2021beware} and
$R^2$-sortability~\citep{reisach2023scale} have been analysed for standardised
SCMs~\citep{ormaniec2025standardizing}, for unit-free generators with a time-series
extension~\citep{herman2025unitless}, and, for time series, by
\citet{lohse2025sortability}, who adapt both criteria to summary graphs, note that
cyclic pairs push the signal towards $1/2$, and measure them on river-flow and Causal
Chambers data; they give no theorem and do not study relatives or observation
schemes. \citet{reisach2026topological} introduce rel-sortability and the
time-unrolled DAG, and prove that strong connectivity of every weak component of the
summary graph is sufficient for the limit $1/2$. Block means of a VAR converge to a
static linear SEM with coefficients $A_{ji}/(1-A_{jj})$~\citep{gong2017aggregated};
that work and \citet{gong2015subsampled} study identifiability from aggregated or
subsampled data, not sortability, and aggregation is classically known to change
time-series models~\citep{weiss1984systematic} and to induce contemporaneous
correlation among the innovations of the aggregated system~\citep{breitung2002temporal}.
The size distortion of a Pearson test with the nominal sample size under
autocorrelation is classical~\citep{bartlett1935, clifford1989, roy1989} and is
handled in practice by effective sample sizes~\citep{runge2019detecting,
afyouni2019effective}; testing the independence of two series through lagged
cross-correlations goes back to \citet{haugh1976checking}. Residual confounding by
unreliable covariates is also known~\citep{westfall2016confounding}. Formal libraries
for causal inference exist~\citep{tan2026causalsmith}. Time-series discovery methods
such as PCMCI~\citep{runge2019detecting} test conditional independences, whereas our
finite-sample results concern only the marginal tests that define relatives.

\section{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

For a directed graph, $\anc(y)$ and $\desc(y)$ contain $y$; the relatives of
$y$ are $\rel(y)=\desc(\anc(y))=\{z:\anc(y)\cap\anc(z)\neq\emptyset\}$ and
$\rho(y)=|\rel(y)|$. For an edge $(x,y)$, $\srho(x,y)=1,\tfrac12,0$ if
$\rho(x)<,=,>\rho(y)$, and $\srho(G)$ is the mean over \emph{edges}, as in
\citet[Eq.~(4)]{reisach2026topological} (quoted verbatim in \cref{app:exact}).
A value $1/2$ means the criterion carries no orientation information.

A \emph{summary graph} $\GS=(V,\Es)$ has $p\ge2$ nodes and a self-loop at every
node. The \emph{time-unrolled DAG} $\GT$ has nodes $v(t)$, $t\in\{1,\dots,T\}$,
and an edge $(i(t),j(t{+}1))$ for each $(i,j)\in\Es$ and $t<T$, so every
summary edge has $T-1$ copies, all at lag one (a VAR(1)-shaped unrolling;
other lags are discussed after \cref{thm:stat}). $d(u,v)$ is the shortest-path
length in $\GS$ (self-loops ignored; $d(v,v)=0$; $\infty$ if unreachable), and
$\anc_S$, $\rel_S$ are taken in $\GS$. SCC means strongly connected component.
$\Ex\subseteq\Es$ is the set of edges between different SCCs and
$\Eo=\Es\setminus\Ex$. A \emph{source} SCC has no incoming edge from another
SCC, and $\Src(v)$ is the set of source SCCs that reach $v$.

\section{Exact Rel-Sortability of Time-Unrolled DAGs}
\label{sec:exact}

\begin{theorem}[every edge, every $T$]\label{thm:edge}
For $(i,j)\in\Es$ and $1\le t<T$, the edge $(i(t),j(t{+}1))$ of $\GT$ has
$\srho=1$ if some $u\in V$ satisfies $d(u,j)\le t\le d(u,i)$, and $\srho=1/2$
otherwise. The value does not depend on $T$.
\lean{\lname{edge_score_dist}, \lname{edge_score}, \lname{strictCond_iff_dist}.}
\end{theorem}
\emph{Sketch.} Every edge of $\GT$ advances time by one, so $u(s)$ is an
ancestor of $v(t)$ iff $d(u,v)\le t-s$ (pad a shortest path with self-loops).
Hence the roots of $v(t)$ are $\{u(1):d(u,v)\le t-1\}$. Since $i(t)$ is a
parent of $j(t{+}1)$, $\rel(i(t))\subseteq\rel(j(t{+}1))$, and the inclusion
is strict iff $j(t{+}1)$ has a root that $i(t)$ lacks
\citep[Thm.~1, Lemma~1]{reisach2026topological}. Full proof in
\cref{app:exact}. (The Lean proof characterises $\rel$ in $\GT$ directly and
does not use their Lemma~1.)

\begin{theorem}[exact limit and rate]\label{thm:limit}
For every $T\ge2$, with $L(\GS)=\tfrac12+\frac{|\Ex|}{2|\Es|}$ and
$\Delta\le p-1$ the largest finite shortest-path distance in $\GS$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
0\le \srho(\GT)-L(\GS)&\le\frac{\min(\Delta,T{-}1)\,|\Eo|}{2(T-1)|\Es|}\\
&\le\frac{p-1}{2(T-1)},
\end{aligned}
\]
so $\lim_{T\to\infty}\srho(\GT)=L(\GS)$.
\lean{\lname{sRho_unrolled_exact}, \lname{sRho_unrolled_bounds}, \lname{tendsto_sRho_GT}.}
\end{theorem}
\emph{Sketch.} For a crossing edge, $j$ does not reach $i$; take $u=j$ in
\cref{thm:edge}: all $T-1$ copies score $1$. For an edge inside an SCC, $i$ and
$j$ are reached by the same nodes, which forces $t\le d(u,i)\le\Delta$, so at
most $\Delta$ copies score $1$. (Lean checks the bound with $p-1$; the
$\Delta$ form is this one-line paper argument, checked by enumeration on
21{,}000 graph--horizon cases with no violation, \cref{app:b1}.)

\begin{corollary}[necessary and sufficient condition]\label{cor:iff}
$\lim_T\srho(\GT)=1/2$ iff every weakly connected component of $\GS$ is
strongly connected. Otherwise $\srho(\GT)\ge\tfrac12+\frac1{2|\Es|}$ for every
$T$. The ``if'' half is \citet[Thm.~4]{reisach2026topological}. If $\GS$ is
acyclic apart from self-loops, with $m$ other edges, $L=\tfrac12+\frac{m}{2(m+p)}$
whatever its shape (chain, in-tree, out-tree, collider).
\lean{\lname{tendsto_half_iff}, \lname{crossEdges_eq_empty_tfae}, \lname{sRho_unrolled_ge_of_cross}, \lname{reisach_theorem4}, \lname{limitValue_dag}.}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[the excess is a boundary effect]\label{prop:boundary}
For $t\ge p$, $\rho(v(t))=T\,|\rel_S(v)|-\sum_{w\in\rel_S(v)}d(\anc_S(v),w)$.
\lean{\lname{rho_unrolled_large_t}, \lname{rho_unrolled_eq}.}
\end{proposition}
So for a crossing edge with $\rel_S(i)=\rel_S(j)$ (root to child in an
out-tree, a chain), the strict order rests on $O(1)$ extra relatives out of
$\Theta(T)$, all located in the first $p$ slices. \Cref{fig:chain} shows the
smallest case. A sample-based relatives count on a long record cannot see such
a difference, which motivates the stationary version.


\begin{theorem}[stationary start]\label{thm:stat}
Observe the window of the last $T\ge2$ slices of $G_{B+T}$ with a latent
burn-in $B\ge\Delta$ (or of the infinite-past graph on $V\times\mathbb Z$), with
relatives computed in the full graph. Then every window node has
$\rho_W(v(t))=T|\rel_S(v)|$, and for every $T$
\[
\srho^{W}=\tfrac12+\frac{|\Eu|}{2|\Es|},\;
\Eu=\{(i,j)\in\Es:\Src(i)\neq\Src(j)\}.
\]
\lean{\lname{rhoBurn_eq}, \lname{sBurn_eq}, \lname{rhoZ_eq}, \lname{sZ_eq}, \lname{relSet_eq_iff_src}.}
\end{theorem}
\emph{Sketch.} After $\Delta$ slices every finite distance is absorbed, so
$w(s)\in\rel(v(t))$ iff $\anc_S(v)\cap\anc_S(w)\ne\emptyset$. For an edge
$(i,j)$, $\rel_S(i)\subseteq\rel_S(j)$ with equality iff $\Src(i)=\Src(j)$.

\textbf{Scope: unit lags.} \Cref{thm:edge,thm:limit,thm:stat} are stated, and
machine-checked, for VAR(1)-shaped unrollings. If each summary edge $(i,j)$
instead acts at a lag $\tau_{ij}\in\{1,\dots,\tau_{\max}\}$ (self-loops at lag
one), the same proofs go through with $d$ replaced by the lag-weighted
distance $d_\tau$: the copy $(i(t),j(t{+}\tau_{ij}))$ is strict iff some $u$ has
$d_\tau(u,j)-\tau_{ij}+1\le t\le d_\tau(u,i)$, the limit is unchanged, and the
stationary result needs a burn-in $B\ge\Delta\tau_{\max}$ (paper proof,
\cref{app:lags}; not machine-checked). Contemporaneous (lag-0) edges are not
covered.

\begin{corollary}[stationary condition; graph classes]\label{cor:statiff}
$\srho^W=1/2$ iff every weakly connected component of $\GS$ has exactly one
source SCC. Strongly connected graphs, out-forests, chains and forks give
$1/2$. In an in-forest (a river network), $\srho^W=\tfrac12+\#\{\text{edges into
confluences}\}/(2|\Es|)$, which exceeds $1/2$ iff there is a confluence.
\lean{\lname{sBurn_eq_half_iff}, \lname{sZ_eq_half_iff}, \lname{stat_half_of_outForest}, \lname{statValue_inForest}, \lname{statValue_inForest_gt_iff}, \lname{collider_stat}, \lname{fork_stat}.}
\end{corollary}

\textbf{Without self-loops} (\cref{app:noloop}). Every edge of $\GT$ still
scores $1/2$ or $1$, independently of $T$, by an explicit walk-length
criterion (machine-checked), and \citet[Thm.~4]{reisach2026topological} still
holds, but the closed form and the converse of \cref{cor:iff} fail: for the
single edge $0\to1$ without loops $\srho(\GT)=1/2$ for every $T$ (with loops
the limit is $2/3$). The loop assumption is therefore necessary for the closed
forms.

\subsection{Random summary graphs}\label{sec:er}
Let $\GS$ be the directed Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi graph $G(p,c/p)$ plus a self-loop at
every node, and $x$ the largest root of $x=1-e^{-cx}$ ($x=0$ for $c\le1$). By
\cref{thm:limit,thm:stat} only $|\Ex|$ and $|\Eu|$ matter.

\begin{proposition}[ER limits; proved on paper]\label{prop:er}
Let $L(\GS)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\srho(\GT)$ at fixed $p$ and $S(\GS)=\srho^W$.
In probability and in $L^1$ as $p\to\infty$,
$L(\GS)\to\tfrac12+\frac{c(1-x^2)}{2(1+c)}$ and
$S(\GS)\to\tfrac12+\frac{c\,F_\uparrow}{2(1+c)}$ with
$F_\uparrow=(1-x)(1-e^{-c})+x(1-x)\big(1-e^{-c(1-x)d}\big)$, where $d$ is the
smallest fixed point in $[0,1]$ of $d=1+e^{-c}-e^{-c(1-x)d}$ ($d=1$ for $c\le1$).
\lean{only the deterministic witness lemmas for $E_\uparrow$ (\lname{upEdge_witness}, \lname{upEdges_subset_confluenceNL}); the probabilistic proof (\cref{app:erproof}) uses textbook Poisson-coupling, Chernoff and Galton--Watson facts \citep{hofstad2016random}. Not proved: rates, the window $c=1+\lambda p^{-1/3}$, a.s.\ convergence, fixed $T$ as $p$ grows.}
\end{proposition}
The first formula says that the fraction of non-loop edges inside an SCC tends
to $x^2$ (giant strongly connected component, \citealp{dorogovtsev2001giant,dovgal2020birth}).
$L(1)=3/4$, $L$ peaks at $0.754$ near $c=1.07$ and then decays like $e^{-c}$;
$S$ peaks at $0.665$ near $c=1.09$ (\cref{fig:er}). Random summary graphs are
thus rel-sortable only near or below criticality, as the giant SCC removes the
signal. For in-forests (rivers) the identities are exact (\cref{app:er}).

\textbf{What the value means.} Show an edge in random order with the two
relatives counts; the rule ``orient towards the larger count, flip a coin on
ties'' has accuracy exactly $\srho$, never errs on an edge it decides, and no
pairwise rule on the two counts does better (a few lines from Reisach et al.'s
Theorem~1, \cref{app:x3}). So $\srho=0.7$ caps such a method at $70\%$
orientation accuracy. Rules that also see node degrees can exceed it.
\lean{\lname{accRule_sortRule}, \lname{accRule_le_sRho}, \lname{edgeAcc_sortRule_strict}.}

\subsection{Does the start artefact change benchmark results?}\label{sec:b1}
We unrolled 420 summary graphs (30 per family and $p\in\{5,10\}$; families in
\cref{tab:b1}) over $T\in\{3,5,10\}$ and drew $n=1000$ linear-Gaussian
trajectories (positive weights, heterogeneous noise) either with the fixed
start of \citet{reisach2026topological} (G1) or after a latent burn-in of 100
steps (G2; an exact stationary start reproduced G2 to within $0.013$). Methods
saw the $pT$ columns shuffled, with time labels hidden: rel-, $R^2$- and
Var-SortnRegress \citep{reisach2026topological,reisach2023scale}, FLOP, PC and
a random order. The analysis plan was committed before any algorithm was run
(\cref{app:b1}). The fixed start raised rel-SortnRegress order accuracy in all
five non-strongly-connected families (pooled $+0.125$, 95\% CI
$[0.116,0.134]$; Holm $p\le1.1\cdot10^{-10}$ per family), and the gain tracked
the exact population gap of \cref{thm:limit,thm:stat} across the 42 (family,
$p$, $T$) cells (Spearman $0.80$; slope $0.53$, so the estimated counts see
about half the population gap). Strong connectivity did not protect short
horizons: on rings and symmetrised ER graphs the gain was $+0.13$ to $+0.31$
at $T<p$ and still $+0.08$ to $+0.22$ in most cells with $T\ge p$, as the
exact values $\srho(\GT)$ predicted (rings: $\srho(\GT)=1$ for all $T\le p$).
The shifts reversed rankings: 11 method pairs swapped significantly on SHD
between G1 and G2 (Holm over 1{,}190 tests; 9 remain when first-slice pairs
are excluded), e.g.\ on chains rel-SortnRegress beat $R^2$-SortnRegress under
G1 and lost under G2. The best method changed in 10 of 42 cells, but FLOP was
best on average under both generators. The fixed start also creates
var-sortability (Var-SortnRegress $+0.20$ on raw data, $+0.002$ after
standardisation), which drives the largest swaps (Var-SortnRegress against
FLOP and PC on in-trees); this second artefact is outside our theory.
A separately planned replication (B1-NL, \cref{app:b1nl}; new seeds, PC
dropped) with nonlinear additive dynamics, signed weights, and signed weights
with uniform noise reproduced the accuracy gain in all 126 cells (pooled
$+0.108$, $+0.137$, $+0.137$; 5/5 families significant in each; tracking
Spearman $0.68$--$0.82$) and the var/rel split, but not the ranking
reversals (one SHD swap between two weak baselines under NL, none under
signed weights). The robust finding is the shift in sortability and in
sort-based baselines, not a change of the best method.

\section{Aggregation versus Subsampling}
\label{sec:agg}

For a stable linear SVAR with independent innovations, nodes with disjoint
summary ancestor sets are independent at all lags, and so are any per-node,
time-invariant transforms, including block means and subsamples; otherwise
the lag-0 covariance is nonzero for almost every parameter
(\cref{prop:zero}, \cref{app:agg}). So the population relatives of
contemporaneous values are $\rel_S$ for every observation scheme, and the
population relatives sort is the stationary value of \cref{thm:stat} (on
non-self-loop edges), not the finite-$\GT$ value. Var- and $R^2$-sortability
behave differently (\cref{thm:agg}): block means converge, at the textbook
Fej\'er rate for averages of a stationary series
\citep[Ch.~7]{brockwell1991time}, to the long-run structural model of
\citet{gong2017aggregated}, whereas subsamples keep the snapshot covariance. As a
consequence, for large block size, block-mean $R^2$-sortability is $0$ on
out-stars and $1$ on in-stars with at least two arms for all weights; on
out-stars subsampled data are $R^2$-sorted correctly on average ($0.84$) while
block means drift to $0$. We claim only these sortability consequences.

\section{Finite-Sample Theory}
\label{sec:finite}

We now ask whether the population relatives can be estimated. $X$ is a
zero-mean stationary Gaussian process with absolutely summable auto- and
cross-covariances $\gamma_{ab}(h)$, standardised so that $\gamma_{aa}(0)=1$,
observed at $n$ times. Two nodes are declared relatives if the Fisher-$z$ test
of the sample correlation $\hat r_{ab}$ rejects at level $\alpha$ with
Bonferroni over $m=d(d{-}1)/2$ pairs, i.e. $|\hat r_{ab}|>\tanh(z_c/\sqrt{n'-3})$,
$z_c=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2m)$; $\hat\rho(v)=1+\#$declared partners. The
\emph{nominal} test uses $n'=n$. Let
$B_{ab}=\sum_h|\gamma_{aa}(h)\gamma_{bb}(h)+\gamma_{ab}(h)\gamma_{ab}(-h)|$
(for an independent pair, the Bartlett sum $\sum_h\rho_a(h)\rho_b(h)$).

\textbf{F1: recovery and effective sample size.} That the nominal-$n$ test has
the wrong size under autocorrelation is classical: effective degrees of
freedom go back to \citet{bartlett1935}, with spatial and multivariate versions
in \citet{clifford1989} and \citet{roy1989}, and the variance with exactly our
summand $B_{ab}$ is in \citet{afyouni2019effective}. We add a non-asymptotic
exact-recovery bound for the relatives set (\cref{thm:f1}, \cref{app:finite}):
if the thresholds separate the population correlations by more than explicit
deviations, the declared set and hence $\hat s=\srho^{\mathrm{pop}}$ are
recovered with failure probability decaying as
$\exp(-n\min(\tau,\rho_{\min}-\tau)^2/(2(\sqrt B+\rho_{\max}\sqrt{D_\gamma})^2))$,
so $n/B$ acts as an effective sample size. For i.i.d.\ snapshots $B=1+\rho^2$;
under strong persistence $B$ can be in the tens.

\begin{proposition}[size distortion of the nominal test]\label{prop:nominal}
In this setting, a non-relative pair is declared by the nominal test with
probability $\to2\bar\Phi(z_c/\sqrt{W_{ab}})$, $W_{ab}=\sum_h\gamma_{aa}(h)\gamma_{bb}(h)$
($=B_{ab}$ when all products are nonnegative), above the nominal $\alpha/m$ when $W_{ab}>1$. With
$\neff=n(1-\rho^2)^2/W_{ab}$ ($W$ the delta-method Bartlett variance) the
false-positive rate tends to $\alpha/m$.
\nolean{uses the multivariate Breuer--Major theorem}
\end{proposition}
The joint asymptotic normality of the two sample variances and the sample
cross-covariance is not an assumption: it follows from the Breuer--Major
theorem for stationary Gaussian vector sequences \citep{arcones1994limit}, in
the form stated by \citet[Thm.~1.1]{nourdin2011quantitative}, applied through
the Cram\'er--Wold device to linear combinations of the three products, which
have Hermite rank $2$; its condition $\sum_h|\gamma_{ab}(h)|^2<\infty$ for all
$a,b$ follows from absolute summability (\cref{app:finite}).

\textbf{F2: saturation by a latent driver.} Let $X_v=Y_v+b_vC$ with $C$ a
stationary unit-variance process independent of the VAR $Y$, and
$\ell_v=b_v^2/\mathrm{Var}X_v$ the driver's share of variance.

\begin{proposition}[saturation]\label{prop:f2}
For summary non-relatives,
$\rho_{uv}=\mathrm{sgn}(b_ub_v)\sqrt{\ell_u\ell_v}=:\kappa_{uv}$. If all $b_v\ne0$,
every pair is a population relative and the population rel-sortability is
exactly $1/2$; $P(\hat s_n\ne\frac12)\le(d^2{+}3d)e^{-cn}$ with
$c\approx\kappa_{\min}^2/(18(\sqrt B+\sqrt{D_\gamma})^2)$. Asymptotically, a pair is
detected with probability $>1/2$ once
$n>\nhalf=3+(z_c/\atanh\kappa)^2\approx z_c^2/\ell^2$ (nominal test; for the
effective test, the same bound on $\neff$).
\lean{\lname{prop21_corr_std}, \lname{sOf_saturated}, \lname{abs_sOf_sub_half_le}, location of $\nhalf$: \lname{ncrit_eq_iff}; the probability statements are not machine-checked.}
\end{proposition}
$\nhalf$ does not depend on autocorrelation, which only sets the width of the
transition. A driver with share $\ell=0.1$ saturates at $n\approx10^3$, and
one with $\ell=0.64$ at $n\approx22$ (both for $z_c=3.29$). A relatives count on a long record can
recover the summary relatives only while
$\max_{\text{non-rel}}|\kappa|<\tau_n<\min_{\text{rel}}|\rho|$, a window that
closes at $n$ of order $\nhalf$.

\textbf{F3: conditioning on a proxy} (\cref{prop:f3}, \cref{app:finite}). For a
proxy $Z=\sqrt r\,C+\sqrt{1-r}\,e$ with reliability $r$, the partial
correlation of summary non-relatives given $Z$ is strictly decreasing in $r$
and vanishes only at $r=1$, so spurious conditional relatives appear for every
$r<1$ once $n$ is large; fewer than half of the non-relative pairs are declared
only if $r>r_{\mathrm{crit}}=1-\frac{\tau'(1-\ell)}{\ell(1-\tau')}$,
$\tau'=\tanh(z_c/\sqrt{n'-4})$ (equal shares; $r_{\mathrm{crit}}=0.95$ for
$\ell=0.64$, $\tau'=0.082$). A driver that reaches nodes through different
filters leaves a residual even at $r=1$. The reliability is identified by
$\rho_{uZ}\rho_{vZ}/\rho_{uv}=r$, the identity behind the three-indicator rule
for one-factor models \citep[Ch.~7]{bollen1989structural}. Formalisation found
four missing nondegeneracy hypotheses in our earlier statements (e.g.\
$b_ub_v\ne0$; \cref{app:lean}). The non-asymptotic bounds are loose (factors
3--13 in $n$, \cref{app:sims}); the asymptotic statements matched every
simulation to within $0.03$ and are used below.

\section{Real-Data Evidence}
\label{sec:real}

\textbf{Protocol and its limits.} We ran four studies (P1--P9, then E1/E2),
each with an analysis plan (predictions, thresholds, estimator, seeds, kill
rules) committed to version control before any dependence statistic of the
study's data was computed in this project (timeline in \cref{app:prereg}).
Three qualifications matter. First, the plans, analyses and results were
produced by AI agents within about six hours of one day, and commit
timestamps are set by the committing client; no third-party registry was
used, so we call them \emph{time-stamped analysis plans}, not
pre-registrations. Second, an earlier exploratory project in the same
repository (one day before plan 1) had already analysed CausalRivers with the
same regions, data loaders and subgraph sampler, at resolutions including ours,
and found unrelated stations about as
correlated as river siblings (VAR(1) residual correlation $0.51$ against
$0.51$; \cref{app:prereg}); the CausalRivers predictions were therefore not
made blind to that dependence structure, and our F2 account of their failure
is explanatory, not predictive. Third, the graph refinement used by E1 was
motivated by study-3 observations on the same device.

\textbf{Estimand.} For an observed node set $O$ of a known summary graph
$\GS$, the predicted value $\tilde s$ is the edge average, over the induced
non-self-loop edges, of the scores of the counts $|\rel_S(v)\cap O|$:
population lag-0 relatives among the observed nodes, with unobserved nodes
acting as latent common ancestors (\cref{def:estimand}). It is what a perfect
lag-0 dependence oracle would give under \cref{prop:zero}. It is not the
rel-sortability of any unrolled graph. Every scored edge has value $1/2$ or
$1$, and $\tilde s>1/2$ iff some induced edge $(i,j)$ has an observed node in
$\rel_S(j)\setminus\rel_S(i)$; this forces an up-edge $(i,j)\in\Eu$ among the
induced edges, but not conversely, because the extra relatives of $j$ may all
be unobserved (\cref{prop:estimand}, machine-checked). The estimator is the
relatives count of \cref{sec:finite} at $\alpha=0.01$ with Bonferroni;
orientation accuracy is the mean edge score of $\hat\rho$. CIs are cluster
bootstraps (2{,}000 resamples). \citet{lohse2025sortability} measured var- and
$R^2$-sortability on the same families; we know of no earlier test of
rel-sortability on real data.

\textbf{CausalRivers: a failed test, explained post hoc.}
River networks are in-forests, so \cref{cor:statiff} predicts
$\tilde s>1/2$ for subgraphs with confluences (mean $0.82$). The estimated
relatives sort carried no orientation information (P2, P3 fail;
\cref{tab:real}) because 98\% of non-relative station pairs tested dependent.
A post hoc reading through F2: non-relative correlations
$\kappa\approx0.64$--$0.74$ give $\nhalf\approx20$--$27$, while the records
have $n=377$--$3{,}652$ steps, so the study sat far inside the saturated regime
(\cref{fig:sat}), where \cref{prop:f2} predicts $\hat s=1/2$. Regional
precipitation acts as a latent driver; adding it to the summary graph leaves
one source per component, and \cref{cor:statiff} then predicts exactly $1/2$.
The second study's plan fixed the F3 remedy (condition on a leave-out regional
mean). It also failed (P4: $0.74$). F3 is consistent with this failure: the
triad estimate of reliability is $\hat r\approx0.62$, below the
$r_{\mathrm{crit}}\approx0.87$ required at the observed $\neff$, and the
plug-in prediction of the post-conditioning dependent fraction is $0.743$
($0.736$) against an observed $0.741$ ($0.735$) at 12\,h (24\,h); this check is
in-sample, because $\hat r$ and $\hat\ell$ come from the tested correlations.
Post hoc, 10 principal components still leave 49\% of non-relative pairs
dependent, consistent with a driver of rank $>1$.

\textbf{Causal Chambers: designed independent sources.} The third study chose
data in which \cref{cor:statiff}'s non-trivial case holds by design: in the
wind-tunnel random-walk runs \citep{gamella2025chambers} five actuators are
independent experimenter-generated random walks, so they are separate source
SCCs; 25 of 26 edges are predicted to be strictly sorted. Saturation is absent
(P7, $0.039$) and the relatives count orients confluence edges above chance
(P8, $0.656$). The graded agreement P9 missed its threshold ($0.267<0.3$; CI
contains $0.3$), so by the plan's own definition the study is \emph{not} a
positive real-data demonstration. The reason is F1, not a driver: 16 of the 26
ground-truth edges are essentially undetectable at lag~0 at $n\approx10^3$,
which compresses the estimated counts. The light-tunnel replication with
i.i.d.\ inputs passes all three criteria. F-theory predictions computed from
the first half of each unit and evaluated on the second half are accurate to
within $0.02$ (\cref{tab:ftheory}); F2 predicts no saturation
($\hat\kappa\approx0.03$ and $0.02$ give $\nhalf\approx15{,}600$ and
$33{,}800$, far above $\neff\approx430$--$1{,}000$). The wind-tunnel graph is
bipartite, so the prediction coincides with ``sensors have more relatives than
actuators''; all units of a chamber share one device.

\textbf{E1: the theorem against naive predictors} (details in \cref{app:e1}).
Plan 4 tested the edge values of \cref{cor:statiff} against eight naive
predictors (sensor indicator; ancestor count, in-degree and depth on the full

coincides with the trivial rule ``tie iff same weak component, otherwise
compare the observed sizes of the two components'', which was not among the
competitors. (iii) Most of the separation comes from ties ($1{,}365$ of the
predictions are $1/2$, which no competitor can output). (iv) The data are 10
units of one series on one device; on 64 wind-tunnel regimes the theorem again
beats all eight, but a constant-$1/2$ rule has lower absolute error ($0.311$
against $0.363$). E1 thus shows that the component structure of
$G_{\mathrm{lin}}$ predicts the observed ties and orderings; it does not
discriminate the theory from that simpler rule.
\textbf{B3} (\cref{app:b3}) therefore used held-out light-tunnel data whose
components have several independent sources (three AR(1)-driven series, 30
units; an actuator collider, 20 units), with constant $1/2$, the
weak-component rule and the theorem on $G_D$ added as competitors. On the
2{,}361 instances where the theorem and the weak-component rule disagree, the
counts were sorted as predicted in $99.8\%$ ($\Delta=-0.50$ against both tie
rules, 30/30 units); in the collider, R$\to$G was sorted whenever B was
observed (264/264) and tied otherwise (123/123). The test was low-risk
(same device; colour edges known to be detectable), and where the graph is
unfaithful it failed: in one interventional set with an almost constant source
the tie rule won ($\Delta=+0.12$, 0/10 units).

\textbf{E2: lagged relatives} (\cref{app:e2}). A Bonferroni max-over-lags
variant of classical cross-correlation independence tests
\citep{haugh1976checking} ($|h|\le30$ on first differences, against lag $0$)
on 64 held-out wind-tunnel regimes raised detection from $0.328$ to $0.398$
without more false positives, but failed the graded criterion ($0.243<0.3$),
so E2 does not succeed. The lag-0 estimator failed that criterion again on
these regimes, so P9's verdict stands.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:disc}

\textbf{For benchmark design.} The finite time-unrolled generator is
rel-sortable above $1/2$ for every summary graph that is not a union of
strongly connected components (\cref{cor:iff}), and part of the excess comes
only from the start at $t=1$. Benchmarks should use strongly connected summary
graphs or a burn-in of at least $\Delta$ slices ($\Delta\tau_{\max}$ with lags up to
$\tau_{\max}$; $\Delta$ cannot be lowered in general), after which the value is the graph property of
\cref{thm:stat}; strong connectivity alone does not suffice at the short
horizons proposed for benchmarks (\cref{sec:b1}). Columns should also be
standardised, since the fixed start inflates var-sortability as well. A latent burn-in makes its nodes latent common causes, so a
ground truth that assumes causal sufficiency must be stated for the window
graph with this in mind; an algorithm that sees time stamps orients every
cross-time edge for free, so the artefact matters for methods that do not use
time order. Random summary graphs are sortable only near criticality.

\textbf{For real data.} Population rel-sortability above $1/2$ does not imply
that a relatives count can be estimated. Two conditions are needed: no latent
common driver with share $\ell$ such that $n\gtrsim z_c^2/\ell^2$ (or a
near-perfect, rank-one proxy), and relatives detectable at lag~0 at the
available $\neff$. Diagnostic: compare $\neff$ with
$3+(z_c/\atanh\hat\kappa)^2$, where $\hat\kappa$ is the typical correlation of
pairs believed unrelated; if $\neff$ is far above it, the count is saturated
whatever the graph.

\textbf{Limitations.} The closed forms assume self-loops at every node and
unit lags (\cref{app:noloop,app:lags}). The ER limits are paper proofs not yet
checked by a human probabilist; they are iterated limits ($T\to\infty$ at
fixed $p$, then $p\to\infty$; the stationary value needs no $T$-limit), with
no rates and no result for fixed $T$. \Cref{sec:agg} covers linear SVARs only.
The finite-sample bounds are loose; \cref{prop:zero} and the probabilistic
parts of F1--F3 are proved on paper only, for Gaussian processes. The analysis
plans are internally time-stamped, agent-executed on one day, and the
CausalRivers plan was not blind to an earlier analysis of the same data. The
positive real-data evidence comes from one device per chamber; E1 coincides
with a weak-component-size rule on its primary graph and is carried mainly by
ties; B3 separates the theorem from that rule but on the same light tunnel,
with no external registration. The graded criteria P2, P3, P4, P6, P9, Q9 and
one B3 secondary set failed. B1 and B1-NL are simulations at $n=1000$; the
ranking reversals of B1 involve sort-based baselines only, the largest come
from var-sortability, and they did not replicate in B1-NL.
