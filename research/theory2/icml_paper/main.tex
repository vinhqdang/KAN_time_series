\begin{abstract}
Simulated causal-discovery benchmarks can be solved by sorting variables by
marginal statistics (var-, $R^2$- and rel-sortability). Time-unrolled DAGs have
recently been proposed as a remedy, with a sufficient condition for their
rel-sortability to tend to $1/2$. We give the exact rel-sortability of the
time-unrolled DAG for every summary graph, every horizon $T$ and every edge,
with an explicit $O(1/T)$ rate. The condition of the earlier result is also
necessary, and every non-strongly-connected summary graph, for instance any
river network, stays strictly above $1/2$. This excess is caused by the fixed
start at $t=1$: with a stationary start the value is exact for every $T$ and
depends only on the source strongly connected components of the summary graph.
We show that block-mean aggregation and point subsampling leave this value
unchanged but move var- and $R^2$-sortability to different limits. A
finite-sample analysis explains why the population value is hard to see in
real series: raw correlation tests are inconsistent under autocorrelation, a
latent common driver saturates the relatives count at $n\approx z_c^2/\ell^2$,
and proxy conditioning works only with near-perfect reliability and a rank-one
driver. All graph-theoretic results and the algebraic core of the finite-sample
results are machine-checked in Lean~4 (116 theorems). In pre-registered
real-data studies, a river benchmark fails as the theory predicts, and a
physical testbed with designed independent sources gives a partial positive
result, including out-of-sample predictions of detection rates. A
fourth pre-registration (E1) asks whether the theorem's edge values beat eight
naive predictors on a multi-layer light-tunnel series. It does, mostly through
predicted ties, and only for the linearly dependent relatives (on the published
graph one naive predictor is significantly better on the strictly ordered edges).
A lagged-relatives estimator (E2) raises detection on wind-tunnel data, but its
pre-registered graded criterion fails, as P9 did.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Simulated benchmarks drive much of causal discovery, and several of their
regularities can be exploited without any causal reasoning. Marginal variances
tend to grow along the causal order (\emph{var-sortability};
\citealp{reisach2021beware}), so a method that sorts by variance does well on
data from common generators, and it explains the strong benchmark performance of some
continuous-optimisation methods, a family that includes NOTEARS
\citep{zheng2018notears}. The
coefficient of determination $R^2$ has the same property and is scale
invariant \citep{reisach2023scale}; internally standardised structural
models are not var-sortable by construction and, empirically, mostly not
$R^2$-sortable \citep{ormaniec2025standardizing}; and \citet{herman2025unitless} relate the
$R^2$ pattern to colliders and confounders and propose a generator that avoids
it. Most recently, \citet{reisach2026topological} defined
\emph{rel-sortability}, which orders a node by the number of its
\emph{relatives} (nodes joined to it by an open path, marginally dependent on
it under faithfulness). Relatives are a property of the graph alone, so the
criterion cannot be removed by reweighting. For random DAGs it is high; for
\emph{time-unrolled} DAGs built from a summary graph with self-loops,
\citet[Thm.~4]{reisach2026topological} show that it tends to $1/2$ if every
weakly connected component of the summary graph is strongly connected, and
they propose such graphs as benchmarks.

Three questions remain open, and they matter for real time series, whose
summary graphs are often feed-forward (rivers, supply chains, signalling
cascades) and whose observations are aggregated or subsampled:
(Q1) what is the rel-sortability of a time-unrolled DAG when the summary graph
is not strongly connected; (Q2) does it depend on how the series is started
and observed; and (Q3) can a relatives count be estimated from one finite,
autocorrelated record, so that a method could actually exploit it?

\textbf{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1)} The exact rel-sortability of every edge of the time-unrolled DAG
$\GT$, for every summary graph and every $T$ (\cref{thm:edge}), its limit
$\tfrac12+|\Ex|/(2|\Es|)$ with $\Ex$ the edges between strongly connected
components, and an explicit $O(1/T)$ rate (\cref{thm:limit}). The condition
of \citet[Thm.~4]{reisach2026topological} is also necessary
(\cref{cor:iff}). The excess above $1/2$ for, e.g., chains and out-trees is a
boundary effect of the deterministic start at $t=1$ (\cref{prop:boundary}).
\textbf{(2)} A stationary version (latent burn-in of $p-1$ slices or an
infinite past) whose value is exact for every $T$ and is fixed by the
\emph{source} strongly connected components (\cref{thm:stat}); river networks
exceed $1/2$ iff they contain a confluence (\cref{cor:statiff}).
\textbf{(3)} Under temporal aggregation the relatives are unchanged, whereas
var- and $R^2$-sortability converge to those of a long-run structural model
under block means and stay at the snapshot values under subsampling
(\cref{sec:agg}).
\textbf{(4)} Finite-sample theory (F1--F3, \cref{sec:finite}): a recovery bound
with an effective sample size and the inconsistency of the nominal-$n$ test
under autocorrelation; saturation by a latent driver at
$n\approx z_c^2/\ell^2$; and a proxy-conditioning analysis showing that the
proxy needs reliability close to $1$ and the driver must be one-dimensional.
\textbf{(5)} Machine-checked proofs in Lean~4 with Mathlib of all graph results
and of the deterministic core of F1--F3 (116 theorems, standard axioms only;
\cref{sec:lean}). Every theorem below names its Lean counterpart.
\textbf{(6)} Four pre-registered real-data studies (\cref{sec:real}). On
CausalRivers \citep{stein2025causalrivers} the relatives sort carries no
orientation information, and F2/F3 predict the observed dependence rates. On
the Causal Chambers \citep{gamella2025chambers} two of three pre-registered
wind-tunnel criteria pass, a light-tunnel replication passes all three, and
F1/F2 predict held-out detection rates within $0.02$. A fourth study (E1)
tests the theorem against eight naive edge predictors on a light-tunnel series
with multi-layer ground truth: it passes 8/8, but under a refined graph of
linearly dependent relatives and mostly through predicted ties, and it fails
for the published graph on strictly ordered edges. A lagged-relatives
estimator (E2) passes three of four criteria and fails the graded one. We
report every failed pre-registered test in the main text (\cref{tab:real}).

The graph results of contributions (1)--(2) are short consequences of a
walk-length lemma and of \citet[Lemma~1]{reisach2026topological}. Their value
is that they settle the question completely, are machine-checked, and change
what a benchmark designer should conclude: much of the rel-sortability of
time-unrolled benchmarks is an artefact of how the simulation is started.
The finite-sample results use textbook tools; their value is that they
predicted, quantitatively and in one case out of sample, the outcome of
real-data tests.

\textbf{Related work.} Beyond the sortability papers above,
\citet{lohse2025sortability} measure var- and $R^2$-sortability of real and
simulated time series and note that cyclic pairs push the signal towards
$1/2$; they give no theorem on relatives or aggregation. Identifiability from
subsampled \citep{gong2015subsampled} and aggregated
\citep{gong2017aggregated} series is a separate question from sortability.
Time-series discovery methods such as PCMCI \citep{runge2019detecting} test
conditional independences, and our finite-sample results concern only the
marginal tests that define relatives.

\section{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

For a directed graph, $\anc(y)$ and $\desc(y)$ contain $y$; the relatives of
$y$ are $\rel(y)=\desc(\anc(y))=\{z:\anc(y)\cap\anc(z)\neq\emptyset\}$ and
$\rho(y)=|\rel(y)|$. For an edge $(x,y)$, $\srho(x,y)=1,\tfrac12,0$ if
$\rho(x)<,=,>\rho(y)$, and $\srho(G)$ is the mean over edges
\citep{reisach2026topological}. A value $1/2$ means the criterion carries no
orientation information.

A \emph{summary graph} $\GS=(V,\Es)$ has $p\ge2$ nodes and a self-loop at every
node. The \emph{time-unrolled DAG} $\GT$ has nodes $v(t)$, $t\in\{1,\dots,T\}$,
and an edge $(i(t),j(t{+}1))$ for each $(i,j)\in\Es$ and $t<T$, so every
summary edge has $T-1$ copies. $d(u,v)$ is the shortest-path length in $\GS$
(self-loops ignored; $d(v,v)=0$; $\infty$ if unreachable), and $\anc_S$,
$\rel_S$ are taken in $\GS$. SCC means strongly connected component.
$\Ex\subseteq\Es$ is the set of edges between different SCCs and
$\Eo=\Es\setminus\Ex$. A \emph{source} SCC has no incoming edge from another
SCC, and $\Src(v)$ is the set of source SCCs that reach $v$.

\section{Exact Rel-Sortability of Time-Unrolled DAGs}
\label{sec:exact}

\begin{theorem}[every edge, every $T$]\label{thm:edge}
For $(i,j)\in\Es$ and $1\le t<T$, the edge $(i(t),j(t{+}1))$ of $\GT$ has
$\srho=1$ if some $u\in V$ satisfies $d(u,j)\le t\le d(u,i)$, and $\srho=1/2$
otherwise. The value does not depend on $T$.
\lean{\lname{edge_score_dist}, \lname{edge_score}, \lname{strictCond_iff_dist}.}
\end{theorem}
\emph{Sketch.} Every edge of $\GT$ advances time by one, so $u(s)$ is an
ancestor of $v(t)$ iff $d(u,v)\le t-s$ (pad a shortest path with self-loops).
Hence the roots of $v(t)$ are $\{u(1):d(u,v)\le t-1\}$. Since $i(t)$ is a
parent of $j(t{+}1)$, $\rel(i(t))\subseteq\rel(j(t{+}1))$, and the inclusion
is strict iff $j(t{+}1)$ has a root that $i(t)$ lacks
\citep[Thm.~1, Lemma~1]{reisach2026topological}. Full proof in
\cref{app:exact}. (The Lean proof characterises $\rel$ in $\GT$ directly and
does not use their Lemma~1.)

\begin{theorem}[exact limit and rate]\label{thm:limit}
For every $T\ge2$, with $L(\GS)=\tfrac12+\frac{|\Ex|}{2|\Es|}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
0\le \srho(\GT)-L(\GS)&\le\frac{\min(p{-}1,T{-}1)\,|\Eo|}{2(T-1)|\Es|}\\
&\le\frac{p-1}{2(T-1)},
\end{aligned}
\]
so $\lim_{T\to\infty}\srho(\GT)=L(\GS)$.
\lean{\lname{sRho_unrolled_exact}, \lname{sRho_unrolled_bounds}, \lname{tendsto_sRho_GT}.}
\end{theorem}
\emph{Sketch.} For a crossing edge, $j$ does not reach $i$; take $u=j$ in
\cref{thm:edge}: all $T-1$ copies score $1$. For an edge inside an SCC, $i$ and
$j$ are reached by the same nodes, which forces $t\le d(u,i)\le p-1$, so at
most $p-1$ copies score $1$.

\begin{corollary}[necessary and sufficient condition]\label{cor:iff}
$\lim_T\srho(\GT)=1/2$ iff every weakly connected component of $\GS$ is
strongly connected. Otherwise $\srho(\GT)\ge\tfrac12+\frac1{2|\Es|}$ for every
$T$. The ``if'' half is \citet[Thm.~4]{reisach2026topological}. If $\GS$ is
acyclic apart from self-loops, with $m$ other edges, $L=\tfrac12+\frac{m}{2(m+p)}$
whatever its shape (chain, in-tree, out-tree, collider).
\lean{\lname{tendsto_half_iff}, \lname{crossEdges_eq_empty_tfae}, \lname{sRho_unrolled_ge_of_cross}, \lname{reisach_theorem4}, \lname{limitValue_dag}.}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[the excess is a boundary effect]\label{prop:boundary}
For $t\ge p$, $\rho(v(t))=T\,|\rel_S(v)|-\sum_{w\in\rel_S(v)}d(\anc_S(v),w)$.
\lean{\lname{rho_unrolled_large_t}, \lname{rho_unrolled_eq}.}
\end{proposition}
So for a crossing edge with $\rel_S(i)=\rel_S(j)$ (root to child in an
out-tree, a chain), the strict order rests on $O(1)$ extra relatives out of
$\Theta(T)$, all located in the first $p$ slices. \Cref{fig:chain} shows the
smallest case. A sample-based relatives count on a long record cannot see such
a difference, which motivates the stationary version.

\begin{theorem}[stationary start]\label{thm:stat}
Observe the window of the last $T\ge2$ slices of $G_{B+T}$ with a latent
burn-in $B\ge p-1$ (or of the infinite-past graph on $V\times\mathbb Z$), with
relatives computed in the full graph. Then every window node has
$\rho_W(v(t))=T|\rel_S(v)|$, and for every $T$
\[
\srho^{W}=\tfrac12+\frac{|\Eu|}{2|\Es|},\;
\Eu=\{(i,j)\in\Es:\Src(i)\neq\Src(j)\}.
\]
\lean{\lname{rhoBurn_eq}, \lname{sBurn_eq}, \lname{rhoZ_eq}, \lname{sZ_eq}, \lname{relSet_eq_iff_src}.}
\end{theorem}
\emph{Sketch.} After $p-1$ slices every finite distance is absorbed, so
$w(s)\in\rel(v(t))$ iff $\anc_S(v)\cap\anc_S(w)\ne\emptyset$. For an edge
$(i,j)$, $\rel_S(i)\subseteq\rel_S(j)$ with equality iff $\Src(i)=\Src(j)$.

\begin{corollary}[stationary condition; graph classes]\label{cor:statiff}
$\srho^W=1/2$ iff every weakly connected component of $\GS$ has exactly one
source SCC. Strongly connected graphs, out-forests, chains and forks give
$1/2$. In an in-forest (a river network), $\srho^W=\tfrac12+\#\{\text{edges into
confluences}\}/(2|\Es|)$, which exceeds $1/2$ iff there is a confluence.
\lean{\lname{sBurn_eq_half_iff}, \lname{sZ_eq_half_iff}, \lname{stat_half_of_outForest}, \lname{statValue_inForest}, \lname{statValue_inForest_gt_iff}, \lname{collider_stat}, \lname{fork_stat}.}
\end{corollary}

\section{Aggregation versus Subsampling}
\label{sec:agg}

Let $X$ be a stable linear SVAR with lags $\tau\ge1$, independent innovations
with diagonal covariance $\Sigma$, and summary graph $\GS$; $\Gamma(h)$ is its
autocovariance and $A(1)=\sum_\tau A_\tau$. Consider block means
$\bar Y_s=\frac1D\sum_{u<D}X(sD{+}u)$ and subsamples $Z_s=X(sD)$.

\begin{proposition}[relatives survive any per-node filter]\label{prop:zero}
If $\anc_S(i)\cap\anc_S(j)=\emptyset$ then $X_i$ and $X_j$ are independent at
all lags, and so are any per-node, time-invariant (possibly nonlinear)
transforms of them, including $\bar Y$ and $Z$ for every $D$. If the
ancestor sets intersect, $\Gamma(0)_{ij}$, $\mathrm{Cov}(\bar Y)_{ij}$ and the
long-run covariance are nonzero for Lebesgue-almost every parameter.
\nolean{needs the SVAR moving-average representation and real-analytic genericity}
\end{proposition}

So the population relatives of contemporaneous values are $\rel_S$ for every
observation scheme, and the population relatives sort equals the stationary
value of \cref{thm:stat} (on non-self-loop edges), not the finite-$\GT$ value.
Var- and $R^2$-sortability behave differently.

\begin{theorem}[block means vs subsampling]\label{thm:agg}
(a) $\|D\,\mathrm{Cov}(\bar Y)-S_{LR}\|\le\frac1D\sum_h|h|\|\Gamma(h)\|$,
with $S_{LR}=(I-A(1))^{-1}\Sigma(I-A(1))^{-\top}$.
(b) If $\GS$ is acyclic apart from self-loops, $S_{LR}$ is the covariance of a
static SEM on $\GS$ with weights $A(1)_{ji}/(1-\lambda_j)$ and noise variances
$\sigma_j^2/(1-\lambda_j)^2$, $\lambda_j=A(1)_{jj}<1$.
(c) $\mathrm{Cov}(Z_s)=\Gamma(0)$ for every $D$.
(d) Hence, for large $D$, block-mean $R^2$-sortability is $0$ on out-stars and
$1$ on in-stars with $\ge2$ arms, for all weights; under subsampling every
contemporaneous criterion keeps its $\Gamma(0)$ value.
\lean{(a) counting identity \lname{fejer_identity}; (b) \lname{longrun_factor}, \lname{selfloop_sum_lt_one}; the probabilistic steps, the rate and (c)--(d) are not machine-checked.}
\end{theorem}

Block means and subsampling therefore converge to different contemporaneous
objects (a long-run SEM versus the stationary snapshot), and block means can
reverse the $R^2$ order a snapshot has: in our exact computations on
out-stars, subsampled data are $R^2$-sorted correctly on average ($0.84$)
while block means drift to $0$ (\cref{app:agg}). Rel-sortability is the only
one of the three criteria that depends on the graph alone and is invariant to
the observation scheme, which is why we study it further.

\section{Finite-Sample Theory}
\label{sec:finite}

We now ask whether the population relatives can be estimated. $X$ is a
zero-mean stationary Gaussian process with summable autocovariances
$\gamma_{ab}(h)$, standardised so that $\gamma_{aa}(0)=1$, observed at
$n$ times. Two nodes are declared relatives if the Fisher-$z$ test of the
sample correlation $\hat r_{ab}$ rejects at level $\alpha$ with Bonferroni over
$m=d(d{-}1)/2$ pairs, i.e. $|\hat r_{ab}|>\tanh(z_c/\sqrt{n'-3})$,
$z_c=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2m)$; $\hat\rho(v)=1+\#$declared partners. The
\emph{nominal} test uses $n'=n$. Let
$B_{ab}=\sum_h|\gamma_{aa}(h)\gamma_{bb}(h)+\gamma_{ab}(h)\gamma_{ab}(-h)|$
(for an independent pair, the Bartlett sum $\sum_h\rho_a(h)\rho_b(h)$).

\textbf{F1: recovery and effective sample size.}
\begin{theorem}[recovery bound]\label{thm:f1}
For $x,y>0$ let
$\varepsilon_{ab}=\sqrt{2B_{ab}x/n}+\kappa_2x/n+2\kappa_1y/n$,
$\varepsilon_d=\sqrt{2D_\gamma y/n}+4\kappa_1y/n<1$ and
$\delta_{ab}=(\varepsilon_{ab}+|\rho_{ab}|\varepsilon_d)/(1-\varepsilon_d)$, with
autocovariance constants $D_\gamma,\kappa_1,\kappa_2$ defined in
\cref{app:finite}. If thresholds separate
($\delta_{ab}\le\tau_{ab}$ on non-relatives and $|\rho_{ab}|-\delta_{ab}>\tau_{ab}$
on relatives), the declared set equals the true one, and hence
$\hat s=\srho^{\mathrm{pop}}$, with probability at least
$1-d(d{-}1)e^{-x}-4de^{-y}$. For a common threshold $\tau<\rho_{\min}$ the
failure probability decays as
$\exp(-n\min(\tau,\rho_{\min}-\tau)^2/(2(\sqrt B+\rho_{\max}\sqrt{D_\gamma})^2))$,
so $n/B$ plays the role of an effective sample size.
\lean{deterministic core \lname{thm15_det}, \lname{decide_eq_target}, \lname{corr_perturb}, \lname{sOf_cntDecl_congr}; Chernoff and trace algebra \lname{chernoff_choice}, \lname{phi_le}, \lname{lemma12_trace}, \lname{lemma12_le_B}. The Gaussian tail bound itself is not machine-checked.}
\end{theorem}
For i.i.d.\ snapshots $B=1+\rho^2$; under strong persistence $B$ can be in
the tens, and so can the loss in effective sample size. The proof combines a
Gaussian-chaos tail bound (the Gaussian case of
\citealp[Prop.~1]{hsu2012tail}; see also \citealp{laurent2000adaptive}), an
exact finite-$n$ variance identity for $\hat r$, and a deterministic
perturbation lemma.

\begin{proposition}[the nominal test is inconsistent; asymptotic]\label{prop:nominal}
Assuming the standard CLT for quadratic forms of stationary Gaussian processes,
a non-relative pair is declared by the nominal test with probability
$\to2\bar\Phi(z_c/\sqrt{B_{ab}})$, which does not vanish. With
$\neff=n(1-\rho^2)^2/W_{ab}$ ($W$ the delta-method Bartlett variance) the
false-positive rate tends to $\alpha/m$.
\nolean{relies on a CLT not available in Mathlib}
\end{proposition}

\textbf{F2: saturation by a latent driver.} Let $X_v=Y_v+b_vC$ with $C$ a
stationary unit-variance process independent of the VAR $Y$, and
$\ell_v=b_v^2/\mathrm{Var}X_v$ the driver's share of variance.

\begin{proposition}[saturation]\label{prop:f2}
For summary non-relatives,
$\rho_{uv}=\mathrm{sgn}(b_ub_v)\sqrt{\ell_u\ell_v}=:\kappa_{uv}$. If all $b_v\ne0$,
every pair is a population relative and the population rel-sortability is
exactly $1/2$; $P(\hat s_n\ne\frac12)\le(d^2{+}3d)e^{-cn}$ with
$c\approx\kappa_{\min}^2/(18(\sqrt B+\sqrt{D_\gamma})^2)$. Asymptotically, a pair is
detected with probability $>1/2$ once
$n>\nhalf=3+(z_c/\atanh\kappa)^2\approx z_c^2/\ell^2$ (nominal test; for the
effective test, the same bound on $\neff$).
\lean{\lname{prop21_corr_std}, \lname{sOf_saturated}, \lname{abs_sOf_sub_half_le}, location of $\nhalf$: \lname{ncrit_eq_iff}; the probability statements are not machine-checked.}
\end{proposition}
$\nhalf$ does not depend on autocorrelation, which only sets the width of the
transition. A driver with share $\ell=0.1$ saturates at $n\approx10^3$, and
one with $\ell=0.64$ at $n\approx25$. Since $\tau_n\to0$ for any consistent
test, a relatives count on a long record can recover the summary relatives
only inside a window $\max_{\text{non-rel}}|\kappa|<\tau_n<\min_{\text{rel}}|\rho|$,
which closes at $n$ of order $\nhalf$. Autocorrelation (\cref{prop:nominal})
and a latent driver are two separate failure modes; both push $\hat s$
towards $1/2$.

\textbf{F3: conditioning on a proxy.} Let $Z=\sqrt r\,C+\sqrt{1-r}\,e$ with
reliability $r=\mathrm{corr}(Z,C)^2$, and let $\pi_{uv}$ be the partial
correlation given $Z$.

\begin{proposition}[conditioning]\label{prop:f3}
With $\rho^Y$ the driver-free correlation,
\[
\pi_{uv}=\frac{\rho^Y_{uv}\sqrt{(1{-}\ell_u)(1{-}\ell_v)}+(1{-}r)\,\mathrm{sgn}(b_ub_v)\sqrt{\ell_u\ell_v}}{\sqrt{(1-r\ell_u)(1-r\ell_v)}} .
\]
At $r=1$ the conditional relatives are the summary relatives and
\cref{thm:stat} is restored. For non-relatives with $\ell_u,\ell_v\in(0,1)$,
$|\pi_{uv}|$ is strictly decreasing in $r$ and vanishes only at $r=1$.
Spurious conditional relatives therefore appear for every $r<1$ once $n$ is
large; fewer than half of the non-relative pairs are declared only if
$r>r_{\mathrm{crit}}=1-\frac{\tau'(1-\ell)}{\ell(1-\tau')}$, $\tau'=\tanh(z_c/\sqrt{n'-4})$
(equal shares). If the driver reaches nodes through different filters (e.g.\
travel times $k_v$), a scalar proxy leaves a nonzero residual even at $r=1$.
The reliability is identified by $\rho_{uZ}\rho_{vZ}/\rho_{uv}=r$.
\lean{\lname{prop31_std}, \lname{cor32}, \lname{absPi_strictAntiOn}, \lname{absPi_eq_zero_iff}, \lname{rcrit_iff}, \lname{thm35_bracket}, \lname{pc_lipschitz}, \lname{prop36_ar1_ne_zero}, \lname{prop36_rank_one}, \lname{prop38_ratio}.}
\end{proposition}
Formalisation found four missing nondegeneracy hypotheses in our own earlier
statements (e.g.\ $b_ub_v\ne0$ for strict monotonicity); they are included
above and listed in \cref{app:lean}. For $\ell=0.64$ and a typical threshold
$\tau'=0.082$, $r_{\mathrm{crit}}=0.95$.

The non-asymptotic bounds are valid but loose (factors 3--13 in $n$ in
simulation, \cref{app:sims}); the asymptotic statements matched every
simulation to within $0.03$ and are what we use for prediction below.

\textbf{CausalRivers: a negative result the theory explains.}
River networks are in-forests, so \cref{cor:statiff} predicts
$\tilde s>1/2$ for subgraphs with confluences (mean $0.82$). The estimated
relatives sort carried no orientation information (P2, P3 fail;
\cref{tab:real}) because 98\% of non-relative station pairs tested dependent.
Read through F2 (post hoc for this study): non-relative correlations
$\kappa\approx0.64$--$0.74$ give $\nhalf\approx20$--$27$, while the records
have $n=377$--$3{,}652$ steps; the study sat two orders of magnitude inside the
saturated regime (\cref{fig:sat}), where \cref{prop:f2} predicts $\hat s=1/2$.
Regional precipitation acts as a latent driver; adding it to the summary graph
leaves one source per component, and \cref{cor:statiff} then predicts exactly
$1/2$. The second study pre-registered the F3 remedy (condition on a
leave-out regional mean). It failed (P4: $0.74$), and F3 accounts for it: the
triad estimate of reliability is $\hat r\approx0.62$, below the
$r_{\mathrm{crit}}\approx0.87$ required at the observed $\neff$ (only 26\% of
pairs exceed it), and the plug-in prediction of the post-conditioning
dependent fraction is $0.743$ ($0.736$) against an observed $0.741$ ($0.735$)
at 12\,h (24\,h). This check is in-sample, because $\hat r$ and $\hat\ell$ come
from the tested correlations. Post hoc, conditioning on 10 principal
components still leaves 49\% of non-relative pairs dependent, consistent with
a driver of rank $>1$ (\cref{prop:f3}).

\textbf{Causal Chambers: designed independent sources.} The third study chose
data in which \cref{cor:statiff}'s non-trivial case holds by design: in the
wind-tunnel random-walk runs \citep{gamella2025chambers} five actuators are
independent experimenter-generated random walks, so they are separate source
SCCs; 25 of 26 edges are predicted to be strictly sorted. Saturation is absent
(P7, $0.039$) and the relatives count orients confluence edges above chance
(P8, $0.656$). The graded agreement P9 missed its threshold ($0.267<0.3$; CI
contains $0.3$), so by our own pre-registered definition the study is
\emph{not} a positive real-data demonstration. The reason is F1, not a driver:
16 of the 26 ground-truth edges are essentially undetectable at lag~0 at
$n\approx10^3$ (smallest relative correlation $\approx0.002$), which compresses
the estimated counts. The
light-tunnel replication with i.i.d.\ inputs passes all three criteria
(\cref{tab:real}). F-theory predictions computed from the first half of each
unit (plug-in correlations and autocorrelations, Fisher-$z$ asymptotics of
\cref{prop:nominal,prop:f2}) and evaluated on the second half are accurate to
within $0.02$ (\cref{tab:ftheory}). In particular F2 predicts no saturation:
$\hat\kappa\approx0.03$ (wind) and $0.02$ (light) give $\nhalf\approx15{,}600$
and $33{,}800$, far above the available $\neff\approx430$--$1{,}000$.

\textbf{E1: does the theorem beat naive predictors?} Pre-registered
(\cref{app:prereg}) to answer the bipartite caveat. The test unit is an edge
instance: the theorem's value $\srho\in\{0,\frac12,1\}$ against the observed value
from the estimated counts, with absolute loss, on the instances where the theorem and a
competitor disagree. The eight competitors are the sensor indicator;
ancestor count, in-degree and depth (each on the full graph and on the
induced subgraph); and the finite-$T$ limit ``every edge is $1$''. A
competitor counts as beaten if $\Delta\le-0.10$ with an exact sign-flip test over
units and Holm correction over the eight. The primary data are the
light-tunnel series \texttt{citris\_1} (10 units), the only real time series
we found whose ground truth has several layers among measured variables
(red$\to$green$\to$blue$\to$sensors). \textbf{It passes 8/8.} The pre-registered
power prerequisites hold (non-relatives declared $0.000$, relatives detected
$0.969$); $\Delta=-0.502$ (sensor indicator) to $-0.533$, in 10/10 units each, Holm
$p=0.0078$, the smallest attainable; restricted to pairs declared dependent, $\Delta=-0.500$.
The theorem's value equals the observed one on $99.2\%$ of 1,662 instances,
the competitors' on $11$--$20\%$; its three anti-sorted predictions (red and blue $\to$ pol$_2$, true edges
whose head has fewer relatives) hold in $99.1\%$ of 110 instances. The same
test on 45 i.i.d.\ structural-model units (pooled, 8,734 instances) also passes 8/8 ($\Delta\approx-0.49$, 45/45 units); the
chain gives $1/2$ everywhere and the collider $1$ into the collider, as
\cref{cor:statiff} predicts.
Four statements bound this result.
\emph{(i) The primary prediction uses a refined graph.} It removes six real causal edges
(red, blue $\to$ pol$_2$; the polarisers $\to$ two sensors) because, by a symmetry lemma
(\cref{app:e1}) that assumes ideal polariser sensors, they carry no linear dependence at any lag. The
claim is therefore only that the theory predicts the \emph{linearly dependent}
relatives. On the published graph the theorem still beats all eight overall
($\Delta=-0.32$ to $-0.42$), but on the 442 edges where it predicts strict order the sensor indicator is significantly
better ($\Delta=+0.453$, reverse Holm $p=0.002$), and the theorem's exact-match rate ($71.4\%$) is
below that of a constant-$1/2$ rule ($81.5\%$, post hoc).
\emph{(ii) Most of the separation comes from ties.} Single-source components are predicted $1/2$, which none of the
naive rules can express: $1{,}365$ of the 1,662 predictions are $1/2$. Strict $0/1$ predictions are
fewer (297 on \texttt{citris\_1}, 2,768 on the snapshot sets). On \texttt{citris\_1} the theorem also wins on them against all eight
($\Delta=-0.54$ to $-0.99$), where an estimator without power would favour the competitors.
Post hoc, a constant-$1/2$ rule matches $81.5\%$ of \texttt{citris\_1} ($68.8\%$ of the snapshot sets) against $99.2\%$ ($92.3\%$).
\emph{(iii) The evidence is one device in a favourable regime}: a light tunnel
with strongly responding sensors and detection of $0.97$; the snapshot sets are also light-tunnel data.
\emph{(iv) The wind tunnel does not replicate the strength.} On the 64
held-out regimes the theorem passes the same test (primary $\Delta=-0.225$, secondary $-0.203$), but
detection is only $0.33$--$0.59$ (limited by F1), so part of this is power-induced. On the primary
configuration a constant-$1/2$ rule has lower absolute error than the theorem ($0.311$ vs.\ $0.363$); at $D{=}10$ the two are close ($0.313$ vs.\ $0.294$).

\textbf{E2: lagged relatives.} E2 tests whether a relatives test that uses
cross-correlations at lags $|h|\le L$ (Bonferroni over lags; same population
relatives under lag-$L$ faithfulness, \cref{app:e2}) recovers the weak lag-0
edges behind P9, on 64 held-out regimes (primary: first differences, $L=30$,
against $L=0$). Success required Q7--Q10 to pass. \textbf{It does not}, because Q9 fails
(\cref{tab:real}): false positives stay rare (Q7 $0.003$), the accuracy on edges predicted $1$
is $0.553$ (Q8), detection rises from $0.328$ to $0.398$ ($+0.069$, Q10), but the graded agreement is
$0.243<0.3$ (Q9). The secondary configuration (block means, $L=3$) fails Q9 ($0.202$) and
Q10 ($-0.007\,[-0.012,-0.003]$): the Bonferroni cost exceeds the gain. On \texttt{citris\_1} lags add
$+0.008\,[0,0.024]$ (detection is already $0.97$). The lag-0 estimator of P7--P9, rerun on these held-out
regimes, again fails the graded criterion ($0.202$ at $D{=}10$, $0.120$ at $D{=}1$): the P9 failure repeats,
and its original verdict ($0.267$) stands. E2 does not rescue P9. Re-drawing only the subgraph sample on the
P9 data moved that statistic from $0.267$ to $0.338$ (development, not a verdict); we report this as
variability of the statistic of about $0.07$, not as a pass. Lags recover some edges (load\_in $\to$ upwind pressure
$0.56\to0.98$) and lose others to the larger threshold ($\mathrm{pot}_1\to\mathrm{signal}_1$ $0.36\to0.16$); every
microphone edge stays undetected, so the F1 shortfall is not mainly a lag problem. Out-of-sample F1/F2 checks pass in all six
configurations (\cref{app:e2}).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:disc}

\textbf{For benchmark design.} The finite time-unrolled generator is
rel-sortable above $1/2$ for every summary graph that is not a union of strongly
connected components (\cref{cor:iff}), and part of the excess comes only from
the start at $t=1$. Benchmarks should either use strongly connected summary
graphs or a burn-in of at least $p-1$ slices, after which the value is the
graph property of \cref{thm:stat}, for every $T$. Reporting $|\Ex|/|\Es|$ or
$|\Eu|/|\Es|$ alongside a benchmark costs nothing.

\textbf{For real data.} Population rel-sortability above $1/2$ does not imply
that a relatives count can be estimated. Two conditions are needed, and F1--F3
quantify both: the absence of a latent common driver with share $\ell$ such
that $n\gtrsim z_c^2/\ell^2$ (or a near-perfect, rank-one proxy for it), and
relatives that are detectable at lag~0 at the available $\neff$. CausalRivers
violates the first, the wind tunnel partly the second. A cheap diagnostic
follows: before relying on relatives-based orderings (or before reading a
benchmark result as evidence that a method uses them), estimate the typical
correlation $\hat\kappa$ of pairs believed to be unrelated and compare
$\neff$ with $3+(z_c/\atanh\hat\kappa)^2$; if $\neff$ is far above it, the
relatives count will be saturated whatever the graph. A practical consequence
for method developers is that a time-series discovery method cannot gain much
from rel-sortability on real records with a shared driver, even when the
summary graph would make it informative in population.

\textbf{Open questions.} Summary graphs without self-loops, contemporaneous
effects at the native time step, and nonlinear mechanisms (where
\cref{prop:zero} still gives the zero pattern but faithfulness can fail, as
the Malus-law pairs of the light tunnel show) are not covered. Lagged cross-correlations raise detection at fine
resolution (E2) but did not repair the graded agreement of P9; weak, nonlinear or
undetectable effects remain the limiting factor on the wind tunnel.

\textbf{Limitations.} The graph results assume self-loops at every node
(without them a single edge gives $1/2$ instead of $2/3$; \cref{app:exact}).
\Cref{sec:agg} covers linear SVARs only. The finite-sample bounds are loose,
and the asymptotic statements rest on a CLT that we cite rather than prove.
\Cref{prop:zero} and the probabilistic parts of F1--F3 are proved on paper
only. The real-data evidence comes from two benchmark families, and the
positive part from one physical device per chamber (E1 uses light tunnels only).
The strongest result (E1) holds for linearly dependent relatives, relies on
an assumption about ideal polariser sensors, and is carried mainly by predicted
ties; faithfulness to linear dependence fails by design for six real edges.
The graded criteria P2, P3, P4, P6, P9 and Q9 failed.
