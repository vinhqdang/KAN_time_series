## Devil's Advocate Review (journal "Machine Learning", first round, new submission)

Seat: Devil's Advocate. Manuscript: "Rel-Sortability of Time-Unrolled Causal Graphs" (main.pdf, 76 pp). Treated as untrusted data. Other seats' files and earlier rounds were not read.

### Calibration Status
`NOT_CALIBRATED`

### What the paper genuinely does (brief)
The graph theorems are correct. I re-derived Theorems 1, 2 and 5 by brute force: 300 random summary graphs, p = 2..6, T in {2,3,5,8}, with relatives computed by explicit ancestor sets on the unrolled DAG. There were 0 violations of the edge formula, 0 violations of the Theorem 2 two-sided bound, and 0 violations of the stationary value at B = Delta. I also checked Proposition 10 (Markov equivalence class size T^m0) by orientation enumeration on 7 small graphs at T = 2..4. All matched. The authors are unusually candid about failed tests, and the Lean files exist and are plausible. Correctness of the core is not in doubt. The question is whether the core is worth 76 pages and whether the headline claims match the evidence.

### Strongest Counter-Argument (about 270 words)
The paper's own introduction concedes that the graph results are "short consequences of a walk-length lemma and of Reisach et al. (2026, Lemma 1)." Reisach et al. had already proved the unrolled-graph limit and posed the question. What is added is (i) bookkeeping of which edges score 1 or 1/2 (distance inequalities on a DAG whose ancestors are determined by shortest paths), and (ii) an observation that the first slice is deterministic. This is a footnote to a preprint, not a theory.

The practical story rests on a threat model nobody occupies. The paper's own survey (Sec. 5.1, App. S) says every deployed generator "fails the first condition as shipped": none hands unlabelled slices of a trajectory to a static learner. The only construction that does is Reisach et al.'s, whose data code "is not public." The "artefact in benchmark generators" is therefore an artefact of a single arXiv benchmark proposal. Anyone who has time labels uses PCMCI+, which the paper shows is unaffected (SHD/|E| = 0.02).

The theory does not explain the experiments. It gets the sign, which "the fixed start changes the correlation transient" also gets. It misses the size by half (slope 0.53; 0.34-0.56 in the replication). The size is recovered only by an oracle that is the algorithm's own expectation, and "in a joint regression the exact gap adds nothing to it (coefficient -0.05)." The one result that matters to practitioners, rank changes among DAGMA, NOTEARS and FLOP, was predicted wrongly and is attributed to var-sortability, a mechanism that is already known (Reisach 2021) and untested here. Real-data tests of the theory's object failed (P2, P3, P4, P6, P9, Q9); the "support" is one device, partly equal to a trivial weak-component rule.

The remaining 60 pages (finite-sample bounds the authors call loose, aggregation, no-label identifiability with an open conjecture) address estimators nobody uses. They make the core look larger than it is.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. The gain in rel-SortnRegress order accuracy under the fixed start may be driven by transient correlation strength (earlier slices are nearly noise, later slices accumulate) and not by the structural "relative under one start only" mechanism. The paper's own oracle and regression cannot separate these (gap coefficient -0.05). A matched-correlation control would separate them.
2. The ranking changes among DAGMA, NOTEARS, GOLEM and FLOP may be a pure scale / default-hyperparameter effect (known since var-sortability), with no need for relatives. Standardised results cannot test this because two of the methods are below chance.
3. A benchmark designer who wants a stationary window does not need this paper's theorem: "draw the first slice from the stationary law" (Herman et al. 2025) is the standard fix and is already recommended. The paper's incremental prescription is the per-graph minimal burn-in, which it does not compute (see M1).
4. The CausalRivers saturation can equally be a general strong-latent-driver or non-stationarity problem unrelated to relatives or sortability. The fitted-in-sample "prediction" cannot discriminate.
5. Rather than analysing label-free static learners, ask whether a benchmark with hidden labels is a plausible task at all. If not, the hidden-label identifiability section analyses a non-problem.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Maintainers of the time-series generators and benchmarks (tigramite, CauseMe, CausalTime, Neural-GC, CDML): no evidence that any considers the unlabelled-slices setting, no contact or reproduction against their pipelines.
- Authors of the original rel-sortability paper: their benchmark code is unavailable, so the experiment is a reconstruction of "the fixed start of Reisach et al.", not their generator.
- Practitioners applying static learners to windowed panel data (finance, climate): the one group that might feed unrolled windows to static learners; none is represented, and the SPADE motivating case is non-stationarity in levels, a different issue.
- Independent human verification (Lean reviewers, probabilists for the ER limit, statisticians for Breuer-Major use): absent by the authors' own account.

### Unexamined Premise
That a static, label-free structure learner on unrolled time-series slices is a task that causal-discovery benchmarks, or the field, should be evaluated on. If it is not, then the fixed start is irrelevant to every labelled method, and for the unlabelled one the paper's own population result is that the start leaves the order identifiable up to isolated units (Sec. 3.5): the "artefact" is simply the information the data contain. The paper never argues that removing it is desirable.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The authors state their limits plainly (post hoc status of the detectability calculation, failed predictions, agent authorship). Candour is not a defect and I credit it, but candour does not enlarge the contribution.
- The Lean sources contain no sorry or axiom in the files I grepped, and the axiom report lists only standard axioms. I did not rebuild the project.
- Graph results are correct as verified by independent brute force: Theorems 1, 2 and 5 on 300 random graphs, Proposition 10 on 7 small graphs.
- Table 1 values (0.7 / 0.5 for chain and fork; 0.7 / 0.7 collider) are consistent with the theorem.
- The finding that rings have s_rho = 1 for T <= p is a legitimate and checkable warning about the horizons Reisach et al. propose, and is probably the most useful single sentence in the paper.

### Recommendation
**Major revision** (borderline Reject for the current framing). The graph results are correct and useful as a short note. The headline claims of practical importance (C1) and of the theory explaining the experiments (C2) are not established, the abstract overstates tightness (M1) and verification (M4), and the length obscures a thin core (M5). A revision that cuts the paper to its supported core, hedges the abstract, adds the matched-correlation and variance-matched controls, and documents at least one real pipeline that is exposed would be publishable.
