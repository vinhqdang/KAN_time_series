# Semi-synthetic Sachs benchmark (ground-truth edge functions from real data): results

Plan: PLAN.md (committed before any run). Code: gen.py (generator), bench.py (runner, reuses research/adaptive/bench.py read-only), run_all.sh / run_d110.sh (drivers), aggregate.py -> results/analysis.md (all tables and tests; this README quotes it). Raw: results/d*_s*.csv (one file per dataset, 85 datasets), results/edges/ (per-edge records). 85 of 85 datasets finished, no crashes, none dropped. Every number below is copied from results/analysis.md.

## Setting in one paragraph
True structure: the Sachs consensus graph with ONE edge removed, 17 edges on 11 nodes (the 18-edge graph contains the cycle PIP2 -> PIP3 -> plcg -> PIP2; the weakest cycle edge by endpoint correlation, PIP3 -> plcg, r = 0.03, was dropped by a rule fixed beforehand). Per node, an additive cubic-B-spline model with ridge chosen by CV was fitted to the real log + z-scored Sachs data (all 7,466 rows): these 17 fitted functions are the ground truth. Noise: bootstrap of each node's fitted residuals ("boot", real, heavy-tailed) or Gaussian with the same sd ("gauss"). Fitted SEM descriptors (seen when the plan was written): median node R2 0.33; true curve amplitude (SD of the centred curve, SD units) from 0.05 to 0.79, 9 of 17 edges with amplitude >= 0.3 ("strong"), nonlinear share of the curve (variance not explained by its best line) from 0.08 to 0.97. d = 110: ten disjoint independent copies (170 true edges, columns shuffled), boot noise, n = 2000, 5 datasets. Seeds: d=11 3500-3519 (n500 boot), 3600-3619 (n500 gauss), 3700-3719 (n2000 boot), 3800-3819 (n2000 gauss); d=110 5000-5004.

## 3. Verdicts against the pre-registered predictions
- P1 (SSC ~ plain SPADE at d=11): CONFIRMED, exactly identical (see above).
- P2 (SSC NRMSE >= 0.6 and median slope < 0.8 in every d=11 cell): CONFIRMED (NRMSE 0.71-0.84, slope 0.011-0.216).
- P3 (main story, BASIL-W/SSC <= 0.7 with CI upper < 0.8 in >= 3 of 4 d=11 cells and at d=110): **PARTIAL by the stated rule** (2 of 4 d=11 cells plus d=110). My expected range 0.6-0.85 was too pessimistic at n=2000 (observed 0.40-0.47) and about right at n=500 (0.78-0.81). The disagreement criterion (CI lower bound >= 0.9 in two or more cells) was not met in any cell. BASIL-W lowers the NRMSE in 80/80 d=11 datasets and 5/5 d=110 datasets, but at n=500 only by 19-22%, and the gain is concentrated on strong edges.
- P4 (SSA between SSC and BASIL-W; gap SSA - BASIL-W positive 0.05-0.2; disagreement if SSA <= BASIL-W - 0.02 in >= 2 cells): CONFIRMED in direction (positive and CI above 0 in 4 of 5 cells; one cell, n=500 gauss, has SSA lower by 0.022 with CI touching 0), disagreement threshold not reached (1 cell). The gap is LARGER than predicted at n=2000 and d=110 (+0.21 to +0.42, iSCM d=100: +0.07). Reason visible in the data: SSA re-weights the SSC fit and cannot resurrect edges SSC has already shrunk to zero (58-73% of true edges); SSA improves on SSC by only 0.09-0.22 NRMSE.
- P5 (BASIL-W AUROC >= SSC - 0.03; loss expected at n=500): the criterion passes in 4 of 5 cells but **the sign is the opposite of the iSCM result and of my prediction at d=11**: BASIL-W has higher AUROC than SSC by +0.02 to +0.13 and much higher AUPRC and orientation (0.77-0.98 vs 0.58-0.83). At d=110 the iSCM pattern returns: -0.037 [-0.051, -0.028] (5/5 datasets lower; misses the -0.03 line narrowly; AUPRC 0.752 vs 0.399 and orientation 0.848 vs 0.547 in BASIL-W's favour). Probable reason (not tested): at d=11 BASIL-W's candidate set is all 10 other nodes (no screening, exact local scores), whereas at d=110 it inherits SSC's top-20 candidates; SSC's d=11 AUROC is itself only 0.82-0.86.
- P6 (SSR_k1.5 < SSC in all cells; SSR above BASIL-W at n=500, similar at n=2000): first half CONFIRMED (SSR NRMSE lower than SSC in all cells, Holm p <= 0.0003, but only by 0.08-0.20); second half WRONG: BASIL-W is far better than SSR at n=2000 (-0.34 boot, -0.23 gauss), better at n=500 boot (-0.107) and tied at n=500 gauss (-0.002 [-0.022, +0.020]). SSR_k1.5 keeps only 16 edges (17 are true) and its AUROC falls to 0.70 (d=11) / 0.73 (d=110); the supplementary k=2.5 is better (0.68, 0.72) but still far from BASIL-W.
- P7 (noise): SSC NRMSE changes by < 0.05 between boot and gauss: NOT CONFIRMED (0.826 vs 0.714 at n=2000, 0.835 vs 0.721 at n=500, a difference of 0.11 in both); BASIL-W advantage over SSR larger under boot noise: CONFIRMED (n=2000: -0.34 vs -0.23; n=500: -0.107 vs -0.002). NOTE: the boot and gauss data differ in more than tails (bootstrap residuals are also skewed and keep the real noise shape), so this is not a pure tail effect.
- P8 (do-effect ratio BASIL-W/SSC <= 0.7 direct, <= 0.85 indirect): direct met in 3 of 5 cells (0.58, 0.42, 0.45; not 0.81, 0.80 at n=500), indirect met in 4 of 5 (0.85, 0.26, 0.85, 0.70; at n=500 boot 1.01, i.e. no gain). PARTIAL.
- P9 (baselines): GAM-ridge has worse structure than SSC: NOT CONFIRMED (GAM-ridge AUROC 0.86-0.91 is above SSC's 0.82-0.86 in all four d=11 cells, AUPRC 0.53-0.59, orientation near chance 0.56-0.77). NOTEARS-linear AUROC 0.6-0.8: confirmed at d=11 (0.73-0.74), not at d=110 (0.523, AUPRC 0.04 = chance). var-sortnregress 0.5 +- 0.1 on standardised data: borderline (0.60-0.67 at d=11, 0.70 at d=110; the order is sampling noise but a random order still recovers more than chance directed signal); var-sortnregress-raw is not better (0.47-0.59).
- P10 (d=110: same ordering, BASIL-W completes, cost ratios as before): ordering BASIL-W < SSR < SSA < SSC < SPADE in NRMSE, as at d=11 except SSA vs SSR (SSA 0.82 above SSR 0.76; at d=11 SSA is slightly below SSR_k1.5 in the cell means, differences 0.02). BASIL-W completes. Cost (wall-clock including metric and do-effect code, so NOT comparable with the replication's t_cost): SSC 103 s, SSA 148 s, SSR 130 s, BASIL-W 104 s per dataset; plain SPADE 205 s.
- P11 (SSC AUROC 0.85-0.95 at n=2000, d=11): not met for boot noise (0.821), met for gauss (0.860).
- Coverage and calibration (reported, no pre-set d=11 target; research/07 R5 asks coverage >= 0.80, ECE <= 0.10): BASIL-W 95% band coverage of the true curve is **0.39 (n500 boot), 0.45, 0.52, 0.63 (n2000 gauss), 0.53 (d=110)**, below the 0.60 kill line of research/07 in 3 of 5 cells and far below the iSCM values 0.74-0.81. ECE is 0.103, 0.070, 0.052, 0.018 at d=11 and 0.005 at d=110 (R5 line met except n500 boot 0.103). Coverage is low because the bands cover only edges that BASIL-W keeps and ~23-48% of true edges are dropped in boot cells (curve zero, band collapses), and because the true functions are fitted splines with some wiggle.

## 4. Comparison with the earlier iSCM results and with the real Sachs data
- iSCM (research/replication; d=100-200, n=500-2000, ER graph, hand-made mechanisms): BASIL-W/SSC 0.22-0.38 in all 8 cells; SSA/SSC about 0.4-0.5 (SSA - BASIL-W +0.06 to +0.26); SSC median slope 0.14-0.46; BASIL-W AUROC 0.006-0.032 BELOW SSC; BASIL-W coverage 0.74-0.81; SSR within 0.03-0.05 of BASIL-W except under heavy tails. Here, with Sachs structure and real-data-derived functions: the qualitative ordering SSC worst, BASIL-W best among the SPADE-family arms survives in all 5 settings (the direction of the repair headline replicates), but the size is smaller (ratio 0.40-0.47 at n=2000, 0.78-0.81 at n=500), the standard fixes do worse (SSA, SSR stay above 0.5 NRMSE or at 0.5-0.75), the structure effect flips sign at d=11 and has the old sign at d=110, band coverage collapses, and a plain ridge GAM over all parents is a competitor at d=11 (below). So: the iSCM story is NOT contradicted but is substantially weaker and less clean on this generator; the strongest parts (BASIL-W reduces NRMSE vs SSC; SSC shrinks heavily) replicate, the parts specific to the refit/adaptive comparison and the structure-cost and calibration claims do not.
- GAM-ridge is a new, uncomfortable finding: at d=11 it matches BASIL-W's edge NRMSE at n=2000 boot (0.394 vs 0.391, Holm-free paired p = 0.96) and is better at n=500 (0.539 vs 0.652, ratio 1.21 [1.16, 1.26], 20/20 datasets for GAM); at n=2000 gauss BASIL-W is better (0.283 vs 0.362, 20/20). GAM-ridge's structure and orientation are worse (AUPRC 0.53-0.59 vs 0.69-0.98; orientation 0.56-0.77). At d=11 the full-parent GAM is feasible; at d=110 it did not finish within 60 min on one core, so it is not an option at the scale where the paper's methods are aimed. This comparison against GAM-ridge on iSCM data is not made here.
- Real Sachs (research/real_data): SSC AUROC 0.613, BASIL-W 0.563 (-0.050), NOTEARS-linear 0.566, orientation 0.51-0.58, BASIL-W/SSR curves less stable than SSC through selection flips. Semi-synthetic data with the same graph and fitted functions give SSC AUROC 0.82-0.86 at d=11 and BASIL-W 0.84-0.99, with BASIL-W above SSC. So the real-data weakness (AUROC 0.56-0.61, BASIL-W below SSC) is NOT reproduced by a clean single-SEM version of the consensus model: whatever limits the real-data scores (pooled interventional conditions, the contested graph, hidden variables, non-additivity) is not captured by estimator behaviour on this generator. Conversely, the semi-synthetic benchmark cannot explain the real-data stability result (selection flips), because stability across subsamples was not measured here. The real-data amplitude ratios (SSR/SSC 1.60, BASIL-W/SSC 1.41 on the top-18 SSC edges) are far smaller than the shrinkage visible here (median SSC slope 0.01-0.2); they are not the same quantity (top-ranked edges only) but suggest real Sachs signals are stronger relative to lambda than the fitted truth, or that SSC's top edges are the least shrunk ones.
- iSCM claim "structure almost perfect, curves shrunk": here structure is not almost perfect for SSC (AUROC 0.82-0.86, orientation 0.58-0.83 at d=11); only at d=110 is SSC AUROC 0.957 but with orientation 0.55 and AUPRC 0.40.

## 5. Caveats
- The "truth" is a fitted model of contested real data: the consensus graph is contested, contains a cycle (one edge dropped by my rule), and omits known relations. Results are about estimators on a generator with real-data-derived functions, not about Sachs biology.
- Many fitted edges are weak or nearly linear (6 of 17 edges have amplitude < 0.25 in SD units; plcg -> PIP2 and PKC -> P38 are nearly linear with amplitude 0.5-0.7): near-linear edges are weakly oriented under additive-noise identifiability, and weak edges are unrecoverable at n = 500. Per edge (n=2000 boot) BASIL-W gets NRMSE 1.00 (drops the edge) for plcg -> PIP2 (amplitude 0.52, nonlinear share 0.08) while GAM-ridge gets 0.16, probably an orientation ambiguity (not checked). The all-edge mean mixes such edges with strong ones; strata are in results/analysis.md.
- Ridge-CV picked very small penalties for several nodes (alpha 0.01 at the grid boundary for p44/42, pakts473, PKC), so some "true" functions are wiggly fits; the truth includes whatever the fit overfitted.
- The 4 d=11 cells share one SEM (independent noise draws only); they are not replications of the construction. d=110 is ten identical copies with 5 datasets (exact Wilcoxon min p 0.0625, Holm 0.25: only effect sizes and CIs are interpretable). The truth uses the same B-spline family as the spline learners (favourable inductive bias; basis 9 vs the learners' basis of grid 8, not identical).
- Data are iid draws from one SEM, easier than the pooled interventional Sachs data. Method hyperparameters (SSA gamma/eps, BASIL-W g, pi, SSR k) were not tuned for this generator; SSR_k1.5 retains 16 (d=11) or 165 (d=110) edges, below the true 17 or 170; k2.5 is a labelled supplement. The shared machine had load 4-10 on 4 cores, so timings are rough.
- Coverage statistics for BASIL-W rest on the model-averaged curve bands; low coverage here is a negative result for the calibration claim on this generator.

## 6. Deviations from PLAN.md and the task text
1. 17 true edges, not 18 (cycle; rule in PLAN.md section Construction). research/real_data/README_results.md already carries a correction of its acyclicity claim (commit 910ecf1), so no further erratum was written.
2. GAM-ridge was dropped at d=110: on seed 5000 it had not finished after > 60 min of CPU on one core (all other methods on that dataset took 14 min), so the run was killed and restarted with GAM-ridge omitted (run_d110.sh; seed 5000 rows for the other methods were kept, they had completed before the kill and are identical code). GAM-ridge is therefore absent at d=110 (negative result: infeasible in this setup).
3. bench.py was changed once after the pre-registration commit: the CSV is written with a fixed column set (EXTRA keys differ by method and had broken the file in the smoke test). No metric or method code changed. The smoke test used seed 0 in a scratch directory and none of its numbers were used.
4. The `time` column (used for cost) includes metric and do-effect code and the shared SPADE-scaled fit, so cost ratios are not comparable to research/replication's t_cost; cost is only reported descriptively.
5. In the aggregate, the P3 verdict rule was implemented exactly as written in PLAN.md (3 of 4 d=11 cells and d=110); it gives PARTIAL. The paired tests use the whole dataset set per cell, no dataset excluded.
